Bottom line. The equity is a thin, highly geared slice sitting on top of about RM34bn of assets. The listed stakes alone are worth 3.6x the market cap. However, about RM21.8bn of net debt sits outside the listed subsidiaries, at the holdco, in Resorts World Las Vegas, in the energy assets and in GOHL. Once that is deducted, the listed stakes are fully absorbed by it (−RM1.8bn). The market is therefore paying about RM7.4bn of implied EV for the unlisted assets, against our base value of RM14.3bn.
The headline 55% "discount" is mostly a leverage and governance discount, not a mispricing that will close by itself. It narrows only if (a) cash is moved up from the subsidiaries or assets are sold (rather than raised through dilutive equity), (b) RWLV EBITDA and Kasuri LNG deliver, and (c) the RWS licence renewal in Feb 2027 is clean.
Positioning view: a cheap option with negative near-term momentum (rights overhang, possible fallen-angel credit). It is investable as a catalyst-driven trade from around RM1.40–1.45 with tight sizing, ideally long GENT / short GENS to isolate the stub. A classic activist campaign is structurally hard because of 43.8% family control. A "constructive engagement" campaign, run with the domestic institutions and focused on upstreaming cash from GENS and selling GENP, is viable.

1. Bull case: why own it, and how the discount narrows

  • Extreme look-through value. GENT's 52.6% GENS stake (RM12.3bn) alone is worth 2.2x GENT's market cap. GENS itself trades at about 5.7x EV/EBITDA with S$2.95bn of net cash and no debt, so even that stake is marked at a trough multiple.
  • Trough earnings in every unlisted segment at the same time.
    • RWLV EBITDA fell from about US$194m (FY23) to about US$31m (FY25). It is now recovering: 1H26 was about US$74m, and Fitch expects about US$160m for 2026.
    • Oil & gas is temporarily loss-making after the Chengdaoxi contract expired (Apr 2026).
    • Kasuri's 1.2mtpa FLNG (over 70% built, first LNG targeted for early 2027) could add about US$200–300m of EBITDA at plateau. Genting owns 95% of it, so almost none of that leaks to minorities. Consensus gives it little credit.
  • Peak capex. RWNYC (licensed Dec 2025; US$5.5bn build plus US$2bn of community commitments) and RWS 2.0 (S$6.8bn to 2030) are front-loaded. Free cash flow inflects as these projects ramp from 2028. Fitch already models RWNYC at US$450m EBITDA by 2028.
  • Insiders are buying at 2007 prices. Kien Huat entities and Lim Keong Hui have irrevocably undertaken RM564m (45.1%) of the rights issue. The controller is putting in fresh cash, not extracting it.
  • Rights issue clears the funding overhang. RM800m goes to debt repayment. Once the issue is underwritten and priced (1Q27), the forced-selling and "more equity" narrative can fade. CreditSights calls it credit-positive.
  • Discrete catalysts in the next 6–18 months:
    • RWS casino licence renewal (Feb 2027). A full-term renewal removes a large overhang.
    • Kasuri first gas and the gas sales agreement.
    • Completion of the Kasuri farm-out and PTLNG divestment (by Feb 2027).
    • Monetisation of the Miami land (via GENM).
    • The UK MHRA decision on TauRx's HMTM (GENT owns about 20% of TauRx). Maybank once flagged RM5.09 per share on approval.
  • The discount has been far narrower before. CGS-CIMB estimates that the long-run (2007 onward) average discount to listed-subsidiary value was about 13%, and Affin put the average discount to SOP at about 9%. Every 10-point narrowing from our base 55% discount is worth about RM0.33 per share, or roughly 22% on the current price.

2. Bear case: why the discount widens or stays put

  • Holdco leverage swamps the listed value. Net debt outside the listed subsidiaries is RM21.8bn, more than the RM20.0bn of listed stakes. Equity holders own a call option on asset values. A 10% fall in gross asset value (about RM3.4bn) removes about 27% of base NAV.
  • Fallen-angel risk. Fitch rates Genting BBB- (cut Sep 2026) and RWLV BB+. S&P has Genting at BBB- with a negative outlook. A cut to high yield would raise refinancing costs on the GOHL and RWLV paper and could trigger index-driven bond selling.
    Maybank reads the rights issue as a sign of "limited success" in refinancing, and warns of further equity raises or dividend cuts.
  • Cash goes down the structure, not up.
    • GENT spent about RM3.3bn buying GENM at RM2.35 (Oct–Dec 2025), mostly debt-funded. GENM now trades at RM1.23, a 48% mark-to-market loss on those shares.
    • GENT will be asked to fund its 73.9% share of GENM's NY equity needs.
    • The GENS cash (S$2.95bn) is earmarked for RWS 2.0.
  • Dividend is gone. DPS fell from 22 sen (FY19, including specials) to 5 sen (FY25), with no interim in 1H25 or 1H26. A yield-driven domestic shareholder base has no reason to hold.
  • Track record of related-party deals.
    • Empire Resorts was bought from Kien Huat in stages in 2019 and 2025; the total investment was about US$724m.
    • Genting UK was sold from GENS to GENM in 2010, with 38% of the votes against.
    • The GENHK collapse in 2022.
    Brokers have explicitly loaded a related-party "risk premium" into their discounts (Maybank: 55–60%, Kenanga: 40%).
  • Licence and regulatory risk.
    • RWS received only a 2-year licence extension in 2025, citing "unsatisfactory" tourism performance.
    • RWLV paid a US$10.5m AML fine in 2025.
    • RWNYC pays 56% tax on slots and electronic table games, and faces new downstate competitors.
  • Structural sellers. GENT and GENM were dropped from the FBM KLCI (Dec 2024). Foreign ownership has been falling, and the stock is now a small-cap stub with EPS dilution of 17–18% (CIMB) still to come.

8. Pre-mortem: it is October 2028 and the position lost 50%. Why?

  1. Credit spiral. S&P cuts to BB+ and GENT becomes a fallen angel. GOHL paper reprices 150–200bp wider, and the perpetual coupons and step-ups bite. Refinancing of the 2027–2029 maturities forces a second rights issue (Maybank's warning comes true) at an even lower price.
  2. RWS licence shock (Feb 2027). Singapore renews for another short term with conditions, or imposes caps. GENS de-rates from 5.7x to 4x EV/EBITDA. GENS is about 36% of GAV, so GENT NAV falls by about RM0.55 per share (−17%) before any sentiment contagion.
  3. Kasuri fails to deliver. The GSA slips, the FLNG overruns, or the reservoir disappoints. The asset becomes a write-down rather than an EBITDA source. Our RM3bn base value goes to about zero.
  4. RWLV keeps bleeding. Strip competition and weak premium play keep EBITDA below US$100m. Ring-fenced lenders force a restructuring, or GENT injects equity to defend the brand.
  5. NY cash sink. RWNYC ramps slowly (56% slot tax, competition from the other downstate licensees). GENM calls on shareholders, and GENT funds 74% of the call.
  6. Another related-party transaction. The family uses GENT or GENM to absorb another family asset, or squeezes GENM minorities at a low price. Investors re-impose a 60–70% discount.
  7. Ringgit and macro. A stronger ringgit (already a 2Q26 headwind) compresses translated USD and SGD earnings, while Malaysian rates rise.

Early-warning indicators: the rights issue's underwriting terms and take-up; any S&P action; RWS renewal headlines; Kasuri GSA signing; RWLV quarterly EBITDA below US$30m; GENM equity calls; any new RPT circular.

9. Is the current valuation too high?

  • On earnings: arguably yes. FY25 and LTM EPS are negative, the dividend is suspended, and CIMB expects 17–18% EPS dilution. A P/E or yield investor cannot justify RM1.45 on 2026–27 numbers. That is why Maybank's RM1.49 Sell target sits close to the current price.
  • On assets: no. P/B is 0.19x. The GENS stake is 2.2x the market cap. The implied EV for the unlisted assets (RM7.4bn) is roughly half our base value and close to the low case.
  • On leverage-adjusted SOTP: modestly cheap. A "fair" discount for this structure is 30–40%, given leverage, related-party history, holdco costs and illiquidity, against a long-run 9–13%. That gives fair value of RM1.95–2.30 (RM1.70–2.00 pro forma for the rights issue). This is about 20–40% upside, not a multi-bagger, unless management changes capital allocation.
  • The low case (RM1.73) is barely above the price. The margin of safety depends on Kasuri and RWLV recovering, plus GENS holding its value. Downside is about 40% in a credit or licence shock scenario (about RM0.85–1.00).

10. Contrarian view: what the market is refusing to see

The market is pricing GENT as a distressed casino holdco. It is better understood as a capex-peak infrastructure conglomerate whose free cash flow turns sharply positive from 2028, controlled by a family that just wrote a RM564m cheque at 2007 prices.

  • Three EBITDA streams are bottoming at once, and the market extrapolates the trough:
    • RWLV: about US$31m in FY25, recovering to about US$160m.
    • Kasuri: zero today, US$200–300m at plateau, about 95% owned.
    • RWNYC: Fitch sees US$450m by 2028.
    Together that is about RM2.5–3.0bn of incremental EBITDA (GENT share), against current holdco net debt of RM21.8bn.
  • The rights issue is a floor, not a ceiling. At RM1.25bn it is small (about 6% of holdco net debt). The family chose dilution over a fire sale of GENS or GENP shares at trough prices. That is rational if they believe those assets are cheap. The family is effectively buying more GENS at 5.7x EBITDA through the holdco.
  • GENS is a hidden balance-sheet lever. It has zero debt, S$2.95bn of cash and about S$780m of EBITDA. Even modest leverage at GENS (1.5–2.0x) could fund a special distribution. GENT's share of a S$1.5bn payout is about RM2.5bn, double the rights issue, with no dilution at GENT.
  • Consolidation drove the GENM offer, so the creeping-control buying should now stop. That removes a source of capital leakage.
  • Free optionality is ignored: TauRx/HMTM approval, a possible Thailand or UAE licence, and the Miami land.

11.2 Activist playbook

  1. Build the position through the rights issue. Accumulate via nil-paid rights and the sub-underwriting shortfall to 3–5%. Pair with a GENS short (beta-hedge the ~36% GAV exposure) or a GOHL CDS/bond hedge.
  2. Coalition first. Present privately to EPF, KWAP, PNB, MSWG and the foreign long-onlies. Frame the campaign as credit-positive, which aligns with the family's goal of staying investment grade.
  3. Ask 1: upstream cash, don't dilute.
    • A GENS capital return through modest leverage (1.5–2.0x EBITDA) or an equal-access buyback (DBS has suggested S$0.90).
    • GENT's share of a S$1.5bn GENS payout is about RM2.5bn.
    • Push for the rights issue to be resized or cancelled if the GENS route is taken.
  4. Ask 2: sell the GENP stake. RM2.6bn at market, likely more with a control premium (about 30%), to a strategic buyer such as KLK, SD Guthrie, FGV or the Sime family. Plantations are non-core and cash-generative. This cuts holdco net debt by 12–15%.
  5. Ask 3: crystallise energy. Further Kasuri sell-down to an LNG major or NOC once the FLNG is commissioned. Sell Banten, Meizhou Wan II, Zhoushan and Jangi to SDIC or Asian infrastructure funds (about RM3–4bn).
  6. Ask 4: RWLV strategic review. Options include a JV partner, a propco/opco separation, or a sale. RWLV is ring-fenced, so a sale at more than about US$2.5bn EV is NAV-accretive. The bar is its debt, not its cost.
  7. Ask 5: capital-return policy. Commit to a dividend floor (for example, pass through 50% of dividends received from subsidiaries). Use the 4% buyback mandate whenever the discount exceeds 40%. Cancel the treasury shares.
  8. Ask 6: governance guardrails.
    • An RPT protocol: an independent committee with veto, and an outside fairness opinion for all Kien Huat-linked deals.
    • Management pay KPIs tied to NAV per share and holdco net debt.
    • One minority-nominated independent director (by negotiation).
  9. Ask 7: simplification. Either complete the GENM merger on fair terms (a share swap, not more debt) or stop buying. Long term, consider a GENS in-specie distribution to collapse the double holdco discount.
  10. Escalation. Public letter. Vote against the rights-issue EGM resolution and against any RPT. Petition through MSWG. Engage the credit-rating agencies and the Singapore and NY regulators' focus on financial strength.

Value at stake: Asks 1–3 cut holdco net debt by about RM6–8bn. Asset sales at market are roughly NAV-neutral; the value comes from three sources:
(i) a control premium on GENP (about +RM0.8bn);
(ii) avoiding the rights issue's dilution, so NAV per share rises to about RM3.45;
(iii) a compression of the justified discount from about 55% to 25–30%, as GAV/NAV gearing falls from 2.7x to about 1.8x.
Together these imply about RM2.4–2.6 per share, 65–80% upside from RM1.45.
