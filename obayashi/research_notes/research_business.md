### Segments - what they do
- Domestic Building: design-build and build-only for offices, high-rises, hospitals, data centres, factories incl. semiconductor, logistics. Includes mass-timber and ZEB. Mostly private clients, largely negotiated or limited-competition for repeat clients, plus competitive tenders. Revenue recognised on a cost-to-cost (percentage-of-completion) basis, as is typical.
- Domestic Civil: tunnels, dams, rail (Linear Chuo Shinkansen), roads, ports, renewable energy civil work. Mainly public (MLIT, NEXCO, JR, Japan Railway Construction, Transport and Technology Agency [JRTT], local government) via competitive/comprehensive-evaluation tenders; JR Tokai Linear awards are the key large private-public mix.
- Overseas Building: North America (Webcor, Kraemer North America, E.W. Howell, James E. Roberts-Obayashi - all historically documented: Roberts-Obayashi est. 1978, E.W. Howell acquired 1989, Webcor 2007, Kraemer 2015). Asia (Taiwan, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia, Vietnam etc. - [country list not verified from primary]). Source: https://www.obayashi.co.jp/chronicle/130th/en/presence/area-northamerica/
- Overseas Civil: infrastructure/ rail/ tunnelling; large part of segment growth in FY3/2027 Q1 ("overseas civil expansion"). Project specifics: n/a - not found.
- Real estate development: office/rental, logistics, data-centre development; plans to invest about JPY 100bn over next decade in data-centre development [secondary - verify].
- Other: renewable energy, biomass, wood; not quantified.
- Recent M&A: Webcor agreed to buy GCON Inc. (Phoenix, AZ; data centre/semiconductor/healthcare construction management, ten states), announced 17 Oct 2025. https://www.obayashi.co.jp/en/news/detail/news20251017_1_en.html
