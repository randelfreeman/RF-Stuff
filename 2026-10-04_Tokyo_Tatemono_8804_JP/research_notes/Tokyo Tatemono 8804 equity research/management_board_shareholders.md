## 1. Key executives: CEO, Chairman, CFO-equivalent and business heads

### Takeaway
Management is all career insiders: the CEO, the Representative Chairman and every executive director joined Tokyo Tatemono straight from university in 1981–1988. The one exception is the non-executive board chair, a former Fuji Bank/Mizuho banker. Succession in January 2025 was orderly and insider-to-insider (Nomura Hitoshi → Ozawa Katsuhito). The outgoing CEO stayed on as Representative Chairman and the previous Representative Chairman stayed on as chair of the board. Insider shareholdings are small, at roughly ¥40–115m per executive (illustrative valuation).

### Inferences
- **Archetype.** This is a classic Japanese salaryman management. The CEO's profile (corporate planning, overseas, then the building/office division) points to continuity with Nomura's office- and redevelopment-led strategy rather than a change of direction.
- **Legacy overhang.** Three current or former top executives sit on the board: Tanehashi as board chair, Nomura as Representative Chairman, and Ozawa. There are also three representative directors (Nomura, Ozawa, Izumi). This can dilute the new CEO's authority and slow strategic change. Investors who dislike "chairman/advisor" structures may flag it.
- **Alignment is modest.** The CEO's stake is about ¥68m (est.), roughly 1.6× the FY2025 average officer pay of ¥41.7m (see Q2). That is well below the multiples of salary typical of Western ownership guidelines, though the BBT means part of pay is delivered in shares.
- **No visible CFO.** No board member is publicly identified as "CFO". This is common at Japanese developers, where finance sits under a director responsible for Corporate Planning and Finance. A former Head of Corporate Planning (Ozawa) is now CEO.

## 2. Compensation

### Takeaway
Total FY2025 officer pay was about ¥750m across 18 recipients, an average of about ¥41.7m each. That is modest in absolute terms. The current split between fixed pay, bonus and BBT stock pay, the KPIs used, and whether anyone received ≥¥100m could not be verified. The last policy description found (2020) tied bonuses to ordinary profit and net income rather than ROE or TSR.

### Cited Findings
- **FY2025 total.** Officer compensation totalled about ¥750m (7.5億円) for 18 recipients (fiscal year ended 31 Dec 2025, per the FY2025 Yuho filed 23 Mar 2026). — [Nikkei company profile (役員報酬)](https://www.nikkei.com/nkd/company/gaiyo/?scode=8804); [Nikkei Yuho page](https://www.nikkei.com/nkd/company/ednr/?scode=8804)
- **Policy as described in the 2020 CSR/governance document** (may since have changed):
  - Directors' fixed compensation was capped at ¥35m per month (≈¥420m per year).
  - Performance-linked pay for internal directors was calculated from about 1% of prior-year consolidated ordinary profit together with consolidated net income attributable to owners of the parent.
  - A mechanism linking directors' pay to company performance was introduced in March 2018.
  - A Nomination & Compensation Advisory Committee (指名・報酬諮問委員会) deliberates on director candidates, representative directors and pay. — [Tokyo Tatemono CSR 2020 governance PDF](https://tatemono.com/sustainability/pdf/2020csr019.pdf)
- **Stock-based pay.** A Board Benefit Trust (株式給付信託) is in place. Directors accrue points that are converted into shares delivered after retirement, and the Yuho share counts include those points. — [irbank officers (FY2025 Yuho extract)](https://irbank.net/E03859/officer)
- **Employee pay benchmark.** Average annual employee salary was ¥11.86m (2025 data). — [yuho-nenshu.com](https://yuho-nenshu.com/company/88040)

### Inferences
- **Average officer pay.** About ¥41.7m (¥750m ÷ 18). This average includes outside directors and Audit & Supervisory Board members (estimate; exact category split unknown). The CEO's pay is therefore higher, but the overall pool looks restrained versus large-cap Japanese peers. The ratio to average employee pay is about 3.5×, a rough estimate that is also diluted by low-paid outside officers.
- **Metric mix may be weak on capital efficiency.** If the 2020-era formula (ordinary profit and net income) is still in use, bonuses reward profit growth even when it comes from balance-sheet expansion. No ROE, ROIC or TSR modifier was found. Verify whether the 2025–2027 medium-term plan added ROE, TSR or ESG KPIs.
- **Nothing suggests excessive pay**, but this cannot be confirmed until individual ≥¥100m disclosures are checked.

### Gaps
- **Not retrieved:**
  - The FY2025 pay breakdown by category (directors excluding outside / outside directors / A&SB) and by type (fixed / bonus / BBT).
  - Current KPIs and weights.
  - The clawback/malus provision.
  - Any individual paid ≥¥100m (the Yuho discloses these by name).
  - The current fixed-pay ceiling and any separate BBT ceiling approved at an AGM.
- **No peer comparison was possible this session** (e.g. Mitsui Fudosan, Mitsubishi Estate, Sumitomo Realty, Tokyu Fudosan HD CEO pay).

## 5. Founder vs professional-manager archetype

### Takeaway
Tokyo Tatemono is a professionally managed, founder-less keiretsu company. It was founded in 1896 and has no controlling family. Leadership has alternated between career insiders and Fuji Bank/Mizuho alumni, which implies consensus-driven, incremental decision-making, low insider ownership, and responsiveness to external pressure (TSE, stewardship codes, proxy advisers) rather than founder-style bold moves.

### Cited Findings
- **Founding.** Tokyo Tatemono was founded on 1 Oct 1896 (創立 明治29年（1896年）10月1日). — [Company overview PDF (Japanese)](https://tatemono.com/company/pdf/company_overview.pdf)
  - The founder, Yasuda Zenjiro of the Yasuda/Fuyo group, is given in the brief. The retrieved snippet did not confirm it independently.
- **CEO lineage per The Shashi:**
  - Hatanaka Makoto: 1994–2010.
  - Sakuma Hajime: became president in Feb 2012; ex-Fuji Bank (joined 1971), joined Tokyo Tatemono as Managing Director in 2003, Representative Senior Managing Director in 2006.
  - Nomura Hitoshi: 2016–2024, career insider (joined 1981).
  - Ozawa Katsuhito: 2025–, career insider (joined 1987). — [The Shashi CEO list](https://the-shashi.com/tse/8804/ceo/); [The Shashi – Sakuma](https://the-shashi.com/tse/8804/executives/ceo/sakuma-hajime/); [The Shashi – Nomura](https://the-shashi.com/tse/8804/executives/ceo/fy16-nomura-hitoshi/)
- **Executive directors are career employees.** Ozawa, Izumi and Akita joined in 1987, Jinbo in 1988 and Nomura in 1981. The non-executive board chair is an ex-Fuji Bank/Mizuho banker. — [j-lic](https://j-lic.com/companies/8804/directors); [Jutaku Shimpo](https://www.jutaku-s.com/newsp/id/0000061122)

### Inferences
- **Professional/keiretsu manager archetype. Implications:**
  1. Strategy continuity and predictable, planned succession, typically every 8–9 years.
  2. Low "skin in the game", so incentives depend on pay design (Q2).
  3. A historical tilt toward balance-sheet growth and relationship-based holdings (Fuyo group), which tends to give low-to-mid single-digit/high single-digit ROE and P/B near or below 1×. This is a sector pattern, not verified for Tokyo Tatemono this session.
  4. Governance changes tend to be reactive and pre-emptive (e.g. the 2025 move to one-year director terms; higher payout).
- **Bank-alumni leadership** (Sakuma, Tanehashi) suggests a conservative, lender-friendly financial culture. That is a possible brake on aggressive buybacks or higher leverage for shareholder returns.

### Gaps
- **The "1896 by Yasuda Zenjiro" attribution** and the details of the president lineage (who served 2010–2012; exact dates for Nomura and Tanehashi) were not verified against a primary source.

## 6. Board of Directors (after the 26 Mar 2026 AGM), changes 2021–2026, voting results, structure

### Takeaway
After the 26 Mar 2026 AGM the board has 12 directors: 7 internal and 5 independent outside directors (41.7%), of whom 2 are women (16.7%). All 12 were re-elected; there were no new directors in 2026. The company is a Company with Audit & Supervisory Board (監査役会設置会社) with four A&SB members, two of them outside; one outside member is a woman. A new full-time auditor, Yoshino Takashi, joined in March 2026.

The articles were amended at the March 2025 AGM to shorten director terms from two years to one. The only top-management voting results found show roughly 93–94% support (no vote below 80%), but the AGM year of those figures is uncertain.

## 8. Activism assessment (2018–2026)

### Takeaway
No activist stake, public campaign, letter or shareholder proposal at Tokyo Tatemono was found for 2018–2026, in either English or Japanese searches. All ≥5% filers identified are index or asset managers. This is a negative finding from limited searching (two targeted searches plus the large-holding review), not exhaustive proof.

Sector peers have been targeted: an activist, widely reported as Elliott, pressed Mitsui Fudosan in 2024. Tokyo Tatemono shares several typical vulnerabilities:
- a dividend-led, modest return policy (40% payout target plus small buybacks) versus Mitsui Fudosan's ≥50% total payout;
- a small and shrinking stable-shareholder base;
- bank-alumni governance and a non-independent board chair;
- probable cross-shareholdings and a sub-par ROE/P/B profile typical of the sector (unverified for Tatemono this session).

### Cited Findings
- **English search** ("Tokyo Tatemono activist investor stake shareholder proposal") returned no Tokyo Tatemono-specific activism. Results covered only broader Japanese activism: a record 50 Japanese companies received activist shareholder proposals by mid-2025, and Japan ranks second globally for activist activity after the US. — [Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/business-trends/record-50-japanese-firms-hit-with-activist-shareholder-proposals); [Chambers – Shareholder Activism 2025 Japan](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/shareholders-rights-shareholder-activism-2025/japan); [TMI – Trends in Shareholder Proposals in Japan (Sep 2025)](https://www.tmi.gr.jp/uploads/2025/09/29/Japan%20Corporate%20%20Finance%20Insights%20September%202025.pdf)
- **Japanese search** ("東京建物 アクティビスト ファンド 株主提案 物言う株主") found no activist fund holdings or shareholder proposals at Tokyo Tatemono. The only related governance item surfaced was the March 2025 articles amendment to one-year director terms, and nothing indicated it was activist-driven. — [finboard ownership page](https://finboard.jp/companies/JP%3A8804/ownership); [EDINET S100VIBB](https://disclosure2dl.edinet-fsa.go.jp/searchdocument/pdf/S100VIBB.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Activism status.** No known activism to date. Risk is moderate and rising. Activists in Japanese real estate have targeted:
  1. under-earning balance sheets (ROE below cost of equity, P/B below 1×);
  2. cross-shareholdings and non-core stakes;
  3. return policy below peers;
  4. asset recycling into sponsored REITs or funds.
- **Tokyo Tatemono plausibly screens on (3)**, and possibly (1), (2) and (4), pending financial verification.
- **Defences are weak.** The free float is held largely by passive and foreign holders and stable holders are only about 4%. On the other side, the 2025 payout increase and one-year director terms look like pre-emptive steps that blunt typical activist demands.
- **Watch-points:**
  - any new ≥5% filing by a non-index manager;
  - shareholder proposals in the AGM notice (due about 8 weeks before the late-March AGM);
  - falling support for the CEO or Chairman below about 85%.
