## 0. Executive Summary / Investment Case in Brief

**What it is.** Nakayama Steel Works is a **sub-scale Osaka electric-arc-furnace (EAF) steelmaker** — the ~4th-largest Japanese ordinary-steel EAF producer, unusually flat-product-tilted (hot-rolled coil, thick/medium plate, checkered plate) alongside long products (bar, wire rod, rebar). It shut its blast furnace in 2002, survived a 2013 state-backed (REVIC) rescue, and today melts scrap + purchased slab in a ~600kt/yr EAF and re-rolls it, with tiny Engineering (marine reefs, rolls, valves) and Real Estate (Osaka Bay land) segments (~2% of sales combined).

**The one-line thesis.** A **deep-value, net-cash, 0.31x-book** cyclical with a **transformational growth option** — the **NN Steel LLC joint venture with Nippon Steel (Nakayama 51% / Nippon Steel 49%, formed 1 Apr 2026)** to build a **~1.2Mt low-CO₂ EAF (~2× capacity, ~¥95–105bn, start-up ~2030)** that internalises slab and gives Nippon Steel an offtake — fighting a **weak, import-pressured steel spread**, a **four-year earnings decline**, and a **heavy capex cycle**. It is best understood as a **cheap, asset-backed call option on Nippon Steel's Kansai EAF/green-steel strategy**, not an earnings-multiple stock.

**Bull in one line:** 0.31x book with net cash and latent Osaka land = a hard floor; the Nippon Steel JV permanently removes the slab-purchase drag, adds green-steel/GX-subsidy upside, and makes Nakayama a clean consolidation/take-out candidate at 0.7–1.0x book (~¥1,400–2,000/share).
**Bear in one line:** a sub-scale, price-taking commodity EAF with ~5% EBITDA margins and structurally high Kansai power costs can earn below its cost of capital *forever* — 0.31x book may be the *correct* price for a value-destroyer about to sink ¥100bn into an oversupplied product; the "dividend hike" is one-off-land-gain-funded and not a sustainable run-rate.
**The crux:** the tension between a **0.3x-book, net-cash floor** and a **collapsing, sub-cost-of-capital earnings trajectory** (FY3/27 ordinary just ¥2.0bn). Re-rating needs a catalyst — ROE inflection, capital-return escalation, JV proof-points, or a Nippon Steel move — none of which is guaranteed, and any of which could take years. **Skew is positive; the risk is "dead money," not a zero.**

## 1. Layman's Guide: What the Company Does & How It Makes Money

**In one paragraph.** Nakayama Steel Works melts recycled steel scrap (and re-melts/re-rolls purchased semi-finished steel "slabs") in an **electric arc furnace** at its Funamachi works on Osaka Bay, then rolls that steel into flat products (hot-rolled coil/sheet, thick and medium **plate**, checkered/patterned "非常口" floor plate) and **long products** (steel bars, wire rod, bar-in-coil, rebar). It sells these mostly to Japanese trading houses and processors serving construction, civil engineering, shipbuilding and machinery. Around this core steel business it runs two much smaller units: an **Engineering** unit (steel marine structures — artificial fish reefs and aquaculture reefs — plus rolls and industrial valves) and a **Real Estate** unit that leases and develops land, much of it former plant land on the Osaka waterfront.

**How each division makes money (FY3/26 shape):**

| Division | What it sells | How it earns | Rough scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Iron & Steel** | HR coil/sheet, plate, checkered plate, bars, wire rod, rebar, slab, by-products (slag) | Spread: product price − (scrap + purchased slab + electricity + labour) × volume | ~90%+ of sales; the swing factor for group profit |
| **Engineering** | Steel fish reefs / aquaculture reefs, rolls, valves, marine & civil steel structures, construction | Project/order-based margins; counter-cyclical, public-works linked | Small (low-to-mid single-digit % of sales) |
| **Real Estate** | Leasing & sale of owned land/buildings (ex-plant Osaka Bay land) | Rental yield; occasional development/disposal gains | Small by sales but **structurally high-margin**; a hidden asset-value story |

**The one-sentence "how it makes money":** it is a **spread business** — group profit is driven far more by the gap between steel selling prices and input costs (scrap, purchased slab, electricity) than by revenue growth, which is why an 8–12% fall in sales can drop ordinary profit by 30–58%. Real Estate provides a steady, high-margin cash cushion and an under-appreciated NAV; Engineering is a small, lumpy, public-works-linked diversifier.

## 2. Business Model & Key Drivers

**Business model in simple terms.** Nakayama is a **mini-mill / EAF re-roller** sitting in the mid-tier of the Japanese steel hierarchy — far smaller than the blast-furnace majors (Nippon Steel, JFE, Kobe Steel) but with a **flat-product (plate/coil) tilt unusual for an EAF player**, most of which make long products (rebar/bar). It shut its **blast furnace and converter in 2002** and has since run an EAF-plus-rolling model, historically **buying semi-finished slab** (including from Nippon Steel) to feed rolling mills whose capacity exceeds its own steelmaking. The Nippon Steel JV new EAF is designed precisely to close that gap — internalising slab supply and turning Nakayama into a net slab producer.

**Key business drivers (ranked by profit sensitivity):**

1. **Steel spread (metal margin)** — selling price of plate/coil/bar minus scrap and purchased-slab cost. The single largest driver; compressed in FY3/25–26 by weak domestic demand and cheap Chinese/Asian imports.
2. **Scrap price** — principal raw material for the EAF (ferrous scrap). Volatile; a pass-through lag hurts margins when scrap rises faster than product prices.
3. **Electricity cost** — EAF steelmaking is power-intensive; Japanese industrial power prices (and fuel-cost adjustments) are a major, partly uncontrollable cost line. A structural EAF disadvantage vs. cheaper-power geographies.
4. **Volume / utilisation** — crude-steel output and mill utilisation. Existing EAF target is a **stable ~50,000 t/month (~600kt/yr)**; the **No. 5 substation accident (26 Sep 2025) that halted the EAF for ~3 months into 24 Dec 2025 (~¥1.6bn hit, FY3/26 H2)** shows how sensitive profit is to unplanned single-site downtime.
5. **Product mix** — higher-value plate and specialty vs. commodity rebar/bar. Mix is a lever Nakayama can partially control.
6. **Construction & civil-engineering demand** (domestic) — the dominant end-market; sensitive to Japanese public works, non-residential construction and the construction labour shortage.
7. **Import competition & FX** — low-priced imports (esp. China) cap domestic prices; a weak yen helps exports/import-substitution but raises imported input and energy costs.
8. **Capex cycle / decarbonisation** — the ¥95–105bn JV EAF is a multi-year capex supercycle for a company this size, with subsidy support (GX / green-transformation) and Nippon Steel co-investment shaping returns.

**Contracts & payment terms (typical for the sub-sector; company-specific terms not fully public):** Japanese steel is sold substantially through **trading houses (商社)** on a mix of "**himo-tsuki**" (tied, contract) volumes and spot; pricing on many flat products is negotiated quarterly/periodically with scrap-linked formulas; long products track published market/scrap indices. Standard domestic B2B terms (invoice + promissory-note/bank transfer, ~end-of-month-plus-30/60 settlement) apply. The Nippon Steel JV adds a **long-term slab-offtake arrangement** (Nippon Steel purchasing part of the JV EAF's slab), which will over time convert a portion of Nakayama's output into contracted, captive volume.

## 3. Macro & Micro Factors — Tailwinds vs. Headwinds

**Tailwinds**
- **Decarbonisation / green-steel premium.** EAF steel is far lower-CO₂ than blast-furnace steel. Nakayama's all-EAF route and the new low-CO₂ JV EAF position it for green-steel demand and **GX (green transformation) subsidies**; company targets a **46% supply-chain CO₂ cut by FY2030 vs. FY2013** (≈80% of its footprint is Scope 3 — i.e., purchased iron units, which the new EAF reduces).
- **Nippon Steel alliance & JV.** Deep-pocketed strategic partner co-investing ~¥50bn, providing technology, slab-offtake and de-risking the largest capex in Nakayama's history.
- **Japan structural EAF shift.** Japanese majors (Nippon Steel, JFE, Kobe) are themselves pivoting blast furnaces to EAFs; Nakayama is an early-mover incumbent EAF operator with a flat-product niche.
- **Weak yen** aids import-substitution economics for domestic mills and any export volumes.
- **TSE low-PBR reform** pressure to improve capital efficiency and shareholder returns at sub-1x-book names — Nakayama trades ~0.3x book.
- **Real-estate NAV.** Osaka Bay land carried at low historical cost; latent value and redevelopment optionality (Osaka growth: Expo 2025 legacy, IR/casino, urban redevelopment).

**Headwinds**
- **Cheap imports.** Chinese/Asian export flood depresses Japanese domestic steel prices and spreads — the dominant near-term margin pressure.
- **Weak domestic demand.** Sluggish construction (labour shortages, "2024 problem" logistics/overtime caps delaying projects), soft manufacturing.
- **Energy costs.** Elevated Japanese electricity prices structurally penalise EAF economics.
- **Scale disadvantage.** Sub-scale vs. majors and vs. large EAF peers (Tokyo Steel); a price-taker on commodity grades.
- **Capex & execution risk.** ¥95–105bn JV EAF (through ~2030) strains a ¥33bn-market-cap balance sheet; cost overruns, delayed ramp, or a weak steel market at start-up would pressure returns and free cash flow.
- **Cyclicality & single-site concentration.** Heavy reliance on one Osaka works; the **Sep–Dec 2025 (FY3/26) substation accident** illustrates single-point-of-failure risk.
- **Four consecutive years of profit decline** into FY3/26 and a further guided drop in FY3/27 — earnings momentum is firmly negative.

## 4. Product Strength, Moat & Bargaining Power

**Perceived value / branding.** Steel is largely a **commodity**; Nakayama is not a premium brand and competes primarily on price, reliability, specification and logistics rather than brand. Its genuine differentiators are (i) a **flat-product (plate & checkered plate) franchise rare among EAF makers**, (ii) **Osaka Bay location** with deep-water/coastal logistics serving the Kansai industrial belt, and (iii) the **Nippon Steel technical and supply relationship**.

**Economic moats — assessment:** *Narrow-to-none, with two partial, durable edges.*
- **Cost/location advantage (partial):** integrated coastal site, established rolling assets, proximity to Kansai customers and scrap supply.
- **Relationship moat (partial but real):** the Nippon Steel capital/business alliance and now the 51/49 EAF JV create a **quasi-captive, hard-to-replicate partnership** — technology transfer, slab offtake and co-funding that a standalone sub-scale mill could not obtain. This is the closest thing to a moat.
- **Low-CO₂ / regulatory positioning (emerging):** first-mover EAF status could become a moat if green-steel procurement and carbon pricing reward it — but this is an option, not yet a cash moat.
- **What it lacks:** scale economies, proprietary product, switching costs, or pricing power on commodity grades. On plain vanilla product it is a **price-taker**.

**Bargaining power:**
- **vs. customers (trading houses / processors):** *Weak-to-moderate.* Sales are intermediated by powerful trading houses (Nippon Steel Trading, Metal One, Hanwa, Mitsui & Co. Steel, etc.); on commodity grades customers hold the whip hand, though the plate niche and JIT logistics give some leverage.
- **vs. suppliers (scrap dealers, electricity, slab):** *Weak.* Scrap is a market-priced input; electricity is effectively administered/oligopolistic (Kansai Electric); purchased slab historically dependent on partners. The JV EAF **improves supplier power over time** by internalising slab.
- **vs. the strategic partner (Nippon Steel):** *Asymmetric.* Nippon Steel is technology provider, JV co-owner (49% of NN Steel LLC), slab supplier **and** future slab/coil offtaker — a source of strength (de-risking) but also of **dependence and related-party risk** on JV terms and offtake pricing. Note: **Nippon Steel is no longer an equity holder in Nakayama itself — it fully exited the share register in December 2022** (it had entered as a 2013 rehabilitation sponsor). The relationship is now contractual (alliance + JV), not ownership.
- **vs. the largest shareholder (Hanwa, 8078):** *Notable related party.* Hanwa is simultaneously Nakayama's **top shareholder (~14.9%) and a principal distributor/off-taker** of its steel — a dual role that aligns interests but concentrates channel power and warrants RPT scrutiny.

- **Beat/miss:** ordinary profit fell 40.8% YoY but **beat the company's own (already cut) forecast** — Japanese wires (Fisco/iFIS) headlined "経常40.8％減益・事前予想を上回る." A **"less-bad-than-feared"** print, not a beat of a rising bar. This was the **4th straight year of profit decline** (ordinary profit trajectory: ~¥12.2bn FY3/24 → ¥8.1bn FY3/25 → ¥4.8bn FY3/26).
- **The decisive event — the No. 5 substation accident.** On **26 Sep 2025 the No. 5 substation failed and halted the EAF for ~3 months; operation was restored 24 Dec 2025** (~**¥1.6bn** estimated hit). Downstream rolling kept running on **higher-cost purchased iron sources**. This drove a **31 Oct 2025 downward revision (FY net-income guide ¥4.2bn → ¥2.3bn)** and explains the H2 collapse. *(Note: this accident is an FY3/26 H2 event — earlier press summaries that pinned it to "FY2025" refer to the year ending March 2026.)*
- **Interim shape:** H1 FY3/26 (Apr–Sep 2025): sales **¥76.6bn (−14.4%)**, operating income **¥3.02bn (−36.9%)**, ordinary **¥2.93bn (−37.6%)**, net income **¥1.71bn (−46.7%)**. Implied H2: ordinary ~¥1.87bn, net ~¥0.75bn — the accident quarter.
- **Why margins fell:** (1) weak domestic construction demand; (2) **low-priced imports** (China/Asia) compressing spreads; (3) the **substation accident** and higher-cost substitute iron; (4) elevated energy (Kansai Electric 2024 tariff hikes), material and labour costs.

**Guidance / outlook (FY3/27, issued 8 May 2026):**

| FY3/27 company forecast | Value | vs FY3/26 |
|---|---|---|
| Net sales | **¥157.0bn** | **+5.9%** |
| Ordinary (recurring) profit | **¥2.0bn** | **−58.4%** |
| Net income (owners) | **¥3.5bn** | **+42.2%** |
| EPS | **¥64.56** | — |
| Dividend per share | **¥20** | **+¥6** |

- **Read the guidance carefully — this is the single most important nuance in the print.** FY3/27 **net income (¥3.5bn) is guided *above* ordinary profit (¥2.0bn)** — the reverse of a normal year. That gap implies **~¥1.5bn+ of non-operating/special gains**, most plausibly the **Osaka land-sale gains (Hirakata land ~¥2.36bn est. gain, delivery May-2026; Mitsuboshi Kaiun parking land ~¥1.30bn est. gain) helping fund the EAF**, and/or accident-related insurance recoveries. **Core operating profitability is still guided sharply lower** (ordinary −58%); the sales "recovery" (+5.9%) mostly reflects a **full year of EAF operation vs the 3-month halt**, not a demand rebound.
- **The dividend hike is real but partly one-off-funded.** ¥20 DPS on ¥64.56 EPS ≈ **31% payout** (consistent with the ≥30% policy) — but that EPS is inflated by the special gains. **Ex-special items, dividend cover on core earnings is thin** — a key watch-item (see forensic/short-seller sections). Still, raising the dividend into a trough is a genuine **shareholder-return / low-PBR-reform** signal.
- **Balance-sheet / cash-flow flags:** the group runs **net cash (~¥6.3bn; ¥9.06bn debt vs ¥15.33bn cash; 72% equity ratio)** — a fortress relative to peers, and a direct target of the retail low-PBR proposals. The forward watch-item is the **~¥95–105bn JV EAF capex through ~2030**, which will consume cash and likely lift gross debt (detail in Section 12).

**Market reaction / what's priced in:** the stock printed its **2026 low of ¥590 on 8 May 2026 (earnings/guidance day)** — a negative reaction to the weak print and −58% ordinary-profit guide — then **rebounded to ¥680 by 19 May** (dividend hike), settling ~¥603 by end-June 2026. It trades at **PBR ~0.30x** (BPS ~¥2,000), PER ~9.3x (FY3/27e), dividend yield ~3.3% on the ¥20 DPS. At ~0.3x book with net cash the market is pricing **chronic sub-cost-of-capital returns (ROE ~2–5%) and/or capex dilution** — i.e., disbelief in JV value creation and fear of a prolonged spread trough. Re-rating triggers: an ROE inflection, capital-return escalation, JV milestones, or a Nippon Steel move on the (now non-owned) equity.

## 7. Earnings-Call Summary & Sentiment Shift

*What management is focused on (recent briefings, FY3/25→FY3/26):*
1. **The Nippon Steel EAF JV** — the centrepiece: capacity ~2x, low-CO₂ slab, offtake, ~2030 start; framed as the vehicle of "Long-Term Vision 2030."

The dividend increase into the trough signals management confidence in downside asset support.

## 19. Adversarial Deep-Dive: Bull Case, Bear Case, Pre-Mortem, Contrarian View

### 19.1 Bull Case
1. **Deep asset-value floor (0.3x PBR).** At ~0.3x book with a net-cash-ish/asset-rich balance sheet and latent Osaka Bay real-estate value, downside to tangible/replacement value looks limited; the TSE low-PBR reform gives management a mandate (and cover) to close the gap via returns and disclosure.
2. **Transformational, subsidised, de-risked growth option.** The Nippon Steel 51/49 EAF JV roughly doubles crude-steel capacity to ~1.2Mt, internalises slab (kills the structural slab-purchase drag), delivers a low-CO₂ "green slab" product with a **contracted Nippon Steel offtake**, and is co-funded ~¥50bn by Nippon Steel plus likely GX subsidies. Few ¥33bn-cap companies control a ¥100bn strategic project with a giant partner absorbing risk.
3. **Decarbonisation tailwind.** All-EAF route + new low-CO₂ EAF aligns with carbon pricing/green procurement; potential green-steel premium and subsidy capture.
4. **Consolidation optionality.** Nippon Steel's 49% JV co-control and slab offtake make Nakayama a logical eventual full-consolidation target — a discrete take-out catalyst at a premium to a 0.31x-book price (see Section 14). Note Nippon Steel owns **0%** of Nakayama today, so this is an alliance/JV-driven angle, not an existing-stake creep.
5. **Shareholder-return inflection.** Dividend raised to ¥20 into an earnings trough signals confidence and a policy shift; capital-return upside as low-PBR reform bites.
6. **Earnings trough / operating leverage.** Four years of decline and a ¥2.0bn ordinary-profit guide set a low bar; any spread normalisation (import relief, demand recovery, energy cost easing) drives outsized recovery given spread operating leverage; the substation-accident base effect also flatters comparisons.

### 19.2 Bear Case (2–3 permanent-impairment risks)
1. **Structural spread compression from imports + energy.** If Chinese/Asian overcapacity persists and Japanese electricity stays high, an sub-scale EAF re-roller can be **permanently squeezed below its cost of capital** — 0.3x book may be the *correct* price for a value-destructive commodity asset, not a discount. The ¥2.0bn ordinary guide (≈1.4% of sales) is perilously thin.
2. **Capex supercycle destroys the balance sheet / dilutes returns.** A ¥95–105bn EAF build on a ¥33bn-cap company — even with Nippon Steel's 49% and offtake — loads leverage, depreciation and execution risk. If steel markets are weak at ~2030 start-up, or costs overrun, ROIC on the JV could sit **below WACC for years**, converting the "growth option" into a value trap and pressuring the dividend.
3. **Value leakage to the JV partner / channel.** Nippon Steel is JV co-owner (49%) *and* slab supplier *and* slab/coil offtaker, while Hanwa is the largest shareholder *and* a principal distributor. Offtake pricing, JV governance and distribution terms can be structured to favour the counterparties — a **related-party web** that can cap the value Nakayama's own minority shareholders capture from any upside (even though Nippon Steel is not itself a Nakayama shareholder).

### 19.3 Pre-Mortem (it's 2029 and the equity has halved — what happened?)
- Steel spreads stayed in a secular trough; the existing EAF ran below 50kt/month again (another reliability incident); FY3/27–29 ordinary profit hovered near/below ¥2bn.
- JV EAF capex ballooned past ¥105bn and/or slipped past 2030; interest cost and depreciation swamped a weak steel market at ramp; free cash flow turned deeply negative and the dividend was cut.
- Real-estate value never crystallised; PBR stayed at 0.3x because ROE stayed ~2–4%.
- Nippon Steel used offtake/JV terms to capture the green-slab economics, leaving Nakayama minorities with the capex and little of the margin.

### 19.4 Contrarian View — What the Market May Be Refusing to See
The consensus reads 5408 as a *melting ice cube* (four years of falling profit, ¥2.0bn guide, imports forever). The contrarian read: the market is **anchoring on the trough P&L and ignoring the balance sheet and the option**. It is pricing the JV as pure capex risk with zero credit for (a) the *elimination of the slab-purchase drag* (a permanent structural margin uplift once the EAF runs), (b) a *contracted anchor customer* (Nippon Steel) that de-risks utilisation, (c) *GX subsidy* capture, and (d) the rising probability that **Nippon Steel simply buys in the minority** rather than run a listed 51%-controlled JV vehicle indefinitely — a scenario in which 0.3x book is a gift. The market is refusing to see that this is less a steel-cycle stock than a **cheap call option on Nippon Steel's Kansai EAF strategy**, with a real-estate-backed strike price.

## 22. Short-Seller Brief — Dismantling the Bull Case

**Thesis: 0.3x book is a value trap, not a floor.**
- **What structurally breaks the model:** a sub-scale EAF re-roller with **no pricing power** on commodity grades, **high Japanese power costs**, and a **permanent Chinese/Asian import overhang** earns below its cost of capital through the cycle. The ¥2.0bn FY3/27 ordinary-profit guide is ~1.4% of sales — one bad scrap/electricity/spread quarter from breakeven.
- **Where revenue is concentrated & the risk:** ~90%+ in one **Iron & Steel** segment, one **Osaka site**, largely **one end-market (domestic construction)** intermediated by powerful **trading houses**. A construction downturn, another **single-site accident** (as in FY3/25), or trading-house destocking hits disproportionately.
- **Why the moat is weaker than bulls think:** the "moat" is really **dependence on Nippon Steel**. That partner is also a competitor-adjacent giant that can dictate slab and offtake terms; the JV is *Nippon Steel's* Kansai EAF strategy with Nakayama as the funding/host vehicle. Minorities may fund the capex and see the margin routed to the parent.
- **Most dangerous competitor bulls underestimate:** **Tokyo Steel Mfg (5423)** — a far larger, lower-cost, financially stronger EAF operator with its own flat-product ambitions and superior scale economics; and, more broadly, **imported flat steel**. Nakayama has no cost answer to either.
- **Worst capital allocation risk:** committing to a **¥95–105bn** EAF at the top of a capex-cost cycle into a structurally oversupplied product — classic **cyclical-capex-into-glut** value destruction; even a 49% Nippon Steel share leaves Nakayama carrying enormous relative exposure.
- **Related-party / incentive concerns:** the largest shareholder (Hanwa, 14.9%) is also a key distributor; Nippon Steel is JV co-owner + slab supplier + offtaker; low insider ownership (~2%) means alignment rests on governance, not equity. Scrutinise transfer/offtake pricing.
- **What must hold for the price to be justified:** stable-to-improving spreads, on-time/on-budget JV, a real-estate/return re-rating, *and* Nippon Steel structuring the JV/offtake in Nakayama's favour. **The dividend is weaker than it looks:** FY3/27's ¥20 DPS is set by a ~31% payout on an EPS (¥64.56) **inflated by ~¥1.5bn of one-off land-sale gains**; on *core* earnings the run-rate DPS is closer to **~¥8–12**. Once the land gains are spent (post-FY3/27), a payout-ratio policy applied to trough earnings mechanically **cuts the dividend** unless spreads recover — removing the yield support just as JV capex peaks. **If ordinary profit disappoints 20–30% from the already-low ¥2.0bn guide, core dividend cover evaporates.**
- **Single permanent-impairment scenario (and plausibility):** JV EAF completes into a weak-2030 steel market with cost overruns; depreciation + interest overwhelm a trough spread; FCF turns deeply negative; dividend cut; PBR stays ~0.25x. **Plausibility: moderate** — the balance sheet and Nippon Steel backing argue against outright insolvency, but a decade of sub-WACC returns is entirely plausible.

**Short-seller's honest caveat:** the 0.3x-book, asset-backed, Nippon-Steel-anchored setup makes this a **poor outright short** (limited downside, take-out/parent-buy-in tail risk, dividend support). The bear case is better expressed as "**dead money / value trap**" than "structural zero."

## 23. Fifteen Questions for the CEO (Ordered by Information Value)

1. **JV economics:** What after-tax **ROIC** do you underwrite for the ¥95–105bn new EAF, at what mid-cycle spread and utilisation, and what is the break-even steel price?
2. **Nippon Steel offtake terms:** How is the slab offtake **priced** (formula, benchmark, floor), and how do you assure minority shareholders the terms are arm's-length?
3. **Funding:** How is Nakayama's share of the ¥95–105bn funded (debt/equity/subsidy/cash), what is the **peak net-debt/EBITDA**, and is the dividend protected through the build?
4. **Existing-EAF reliability:** After the substation accident, what redundancy/capex ensures the **50kt/month** run-rate, and what is the true normalised cost per tonne?
5. **Spread defence:** How much of your book can you **re-price** against scrap/energy, and what is your structural cost gap vs. imports and vs. Tokyo Steel?
6. **Capital allocation / PBR:** At 0.3x book, why build a ¥100bn asset rather than **return capital / buy back stock**? What ROE closes the PBR gap, and by when?
7. **Consolidation:** Given the 51/49 JV, is a **full combination with Nippon Steel** (or a fresh Nippon Steel stake) contemplated, and how would the board protect minority value if approached — especially since Nippon Steel exited the register in 2022?
8. **Green-steel monetisation:** What **green premium** and **GX subsidy** are actually contracted vs. aspirational, and how much JV capex do subsidies offset?
9. **Real estate:** What is the **market/appraised value** of the Osaka Bay land vs. book, and what is the plan (develop, lease, monetise)?
10. **Mid-term plan targets:** What are the explicit FY2027 **ROE/ROIC/margin** targets, and what happens to the plan if spreads don't recover?
11. **Import scenario:** If Chinese flat-steel exports stay elevated for 3–5 years, what is your **downside operating plan** (curtailment, mix shift, cost)?
12. **Engineering & Real Estate:** Are these **core**? Would you divest to fund the EAF or simplify the story?
13. **Energy:** What is your **electricity-cost hedging/procurement** strategy and exposure to Kansai Electric tariff moves; any captive/renewable power plan for the new EAF?
14. **Governance:** How many **truly independent** directors oversee the Nippon Steel-related transactions, and is there a special committee for RPTs?
15. **Succession & incentives:** Post the 2025 CEO transition, how is management **incentivised on ROIC/TSR** (not volume), and what is insider ownership?
