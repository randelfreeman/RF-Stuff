## 1. Top of the stack

**1. USTR proposed Section 301 "excess-capacity" tariffs: Japan up to 15% and Korea 15%, below the 15–25% feared. Nothing takes effect before January.**
- **What was proposed (Nov 25).** USTR made adverse findings against 14 of the 16 economies investigated; Mexico and Norway got no proposed tariff ([USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural), [Investing/Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/us-proposes-new-tariffs-on-14-trading-partners-to-fight-excess-factory-capacity-5050714)).
  - **Japan:** up to 15% *including* MFN and existing duties, which matches the trade-deal cap. It could rise to 20% if Japan is judged to fall short on the deal, including the $550bn investment pledge ([NRI/Kiuchi](https://www.nri.com/jp/media/column/kiuchi/20261126.html)).
  - **Korea:** 15%, with a 25% fallback.
  - **Others:** China gets 20% on top of about 25%, Vietnam up to 35%, India up to 25%.
- **Timetable.** Comments are due **Jan 5, 2027**, with hearings in January.
- **Autos.** Vehicles and parts already under Section 232 are reportedly excluded ([Asiae](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026112523534805986), *single source; check the Federal Register notice*). Yesterday's low-tier "autos excluded, Japan capped at 15%" report now looks broadly right.
- **Market read.** Copper and gold firmed because the proposal was "less damaging than feared" ([Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40450844/copper-bounces-back-toward-12000-as-us-tariff-fears-ease)). Non-ferrous metals were Tokyo's best sector.
- **Verdict: MATTERS (relief).**
  - It removes a tail risk for Honda, Subaru, Suzuki, Kubota and the Korean exporters.
  - The live risk is now the deal-compliance clause (the 20% and 25% fallbacks).
