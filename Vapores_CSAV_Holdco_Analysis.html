1. Executive summary

What it is. Vapores is a single-asset holding company. Its 30.0% stake in Hapag-Lloyd AG (HLAG, Xetra), held through the wholly-owned German vehicle CSAV Germany Container Holding GmbH, represents 92.5% of consolidated assets; the remainder is cash and tax receivables. HLAG is equity-accounted, so Vapores' reported earnings are 30% of HLAG's profit less holdco costs and Chilean taxes on dividend flows. There is no other operating business, no financial debt and no treasury stock.

Where it trades. At CLP 49.20 the market capitalisation is US$2.7bn against a look-through NAV we estimate at US$8.7bn (CLP 157 per share), a 68.7% discount. That is wider than the ~52% long-run average and the 60–63% levels Chilean brokers used in 2021–25, and at the extreme end of any single-asset listed holdco globally (Porsche SE 40–55%, Heineken Holding 10–15%, Quiñenco itself ~26%). Put differently, the market is buying HLAG through Vapores at an implied EUR 37 per HLAG share versus a EUR 135 screen price.

Our view. The discount is not a simple mispricing waiting to close. Roughly a third of it is structural (Chilean corporate tax on dividends received from a tonnage-tax company, ~27% with no offsetting credit; holdco costs; a 66.5% controller with no simplification agenda). The balance is the market's refusal to capitalise HLAG's own share price, which is set by a 3.6% free float and stands at ~9.2x 2026E EV/EBITDA versus ~3.5x for Maersk. On look-through multiples Vapores is not expensive (~3.0x EV/EBITDA, 0.37x P/B), but the equity story now hinges on two things the stock does not yet price: (i) the outcome of HLAG's US$4.2bn ZIM acquisition, where Israeli agencies deliver positions on 9 September and a revised offer is due by end-September; and (ii) a likely sharp fall in the 2027 dividend after a loss-making H1 2026 at CSAV, which removes the mandatory-dividend floor. We see the stock as a dividend-and-event vehicle rather than a discount-closure trade; positioning should be sized to the ZIM binary and the dividend cliff, not to a mean-reversion of the discount.

5. Bull case: why own it, and how the discount narrows

5.1 The asset

  • A control block in a top-5 carrier with a fortress balance sheet. HLAG closed H1 2026 with net debt of US$2.0bn (0.7x LTM EBITDA), a 61% equity ratio and a US$5.9bn liquidity reserve. It is the co-controller in a structure where CSAV holds 30%, on equal footing with Kühne. Its Gemini network with Maersk runs schedule reliability of ~90%, well above competitors, and its terminals arm (Hanseatic Global Terminals) is compounding: throughput +9% YoY in H1, EBITDA US$102m, with a target of ~30 terminals by 2030.
  • Earnings trough may be behind. Q2 2026 liner EBIT recovered to US$153m from –US$174m in Q1; freight rates rose 8.9% QoQ to US$1,475/TEU; H1 volumes +1.5%; the July guidance raise lifted 2026 EBITDA to US$2.7–3.7bn (EBIT US$0.1–1.1bn) from US$1.1–3.1bn. The US$600m Middle East cost hit is largely realised.
  • ZIM is optionality either way. If it closes, HLAG adds ~US$4bn of revenue and 300–500m of annual synergies at ~0.6x book for a modern chartered fleet, cementing a top-5 position with a stronger Transpacific and Asia–Med franchise. If it is blocked, HLAG keeps a US$5.9bn liquidity reserve and the capital allocation debate returns to dividends and buybacks. Either outcome is a catalyst; only a value-destroying renegotiation is bad.

5.2 The vehicle

  • Cash conversion is real, not theoretical. Vapores has paid out US$5.7bn since 2020 (2024: US$1.15bn; 2025: ~US$0.39bn; 2026: US$0.48bn), equal to roughly CLP 104 per share, or about 2.1x today's price. The controller needs the cash (Vapores supplied ~46% of Quiñenco's dividend income in 2025), so the payout culture is durable.
  • Balance sheet is clean: zero debt, ~US$428m of cash and recoverable taxes, holdco costs of ~0.15% of NAV.
  • Look-through valuation is undemanding: ~3.0x 2026E EV/EBITDA and 0.37x book at the implied HLAG price, versus ~3.5x and ~0.7x for Maersk.

6. Bear case: why the discount stays wide or widens

  • HLAG's quote is the anomaly, not Vapores' discount. With 3.6% free float, HLAG trades at ~9.2x 2026E EV/EBITDA, 1.33x book and ~27x 2025 earnings, against ~3.5x, ~0.7x and ~14x for Maersk. Valued at Maersk's multiple, HLAG would be worth ~EUR 45/share and Vapores' discount to that NAV would be only 15%. On that reading the stock is roughly fairly priced and the "discount" cannot be harvested.
  • The dividend cliff. CSAV lost US$132.6m in H1 2026 (share of HLAG –US$61m, tax –US$69m). Chile's 30% mandatory dividend applies to net income, so if 2026 finishes at or near a loss the statutory minimum is zero and the 2027 payment is discretionary, funded from a cash pile already drawn down by ~US$170m in H1. A 2027 dividend of US$100–200m (4–7% yield) versus the 14–17% trailing yields would remove the main reason Chilean institutions own the name.
  • Structural tax leakage. HLAG pays tonnage tax, so CSAV gets little underlying foreign-tax credit; Chilean first-category tax at 27% applies to dividends received. Reported tax charges were ~35% of dividend inflows in both H1 2025 (US$163m) and H1 2026 (US$69m). Add the 26.375% German withholding cash lag and the fair "friction" discount alone is 25–30%.
  • ZIM re-levers HLAG at the wrong point in the cycle. Pro forma net debt of US$5–6bn (~1.5–2x EBITDA) plus a record orderbook delivering through 2027–28 and normalising Suez/Hormuz routings argues for a lower and later dividend. Israeli security conditions (fleet-availability covenants, golden-share constraints) could also dilute the synergy case.
  • No agency to fix it. The controller has never bought back stock, has renewed the pact to 2030, and needs Vapores' dividends rather than a re-rating. Minority holders cannot force anything (see §11).
  • Liquidity and index risk. Free float of 33.5% (~US$0.9bn) with modest turnover; any further Quiñenco creep reduces float-adjusted weightings in IPSA/MSCI Chile and removes the marginal foreign buyer.

Verdict. Against HLAG's screen price Vapores is very cheap; against what HLAG is worth on peer multiples it is roughly fair. The trailing yield of 18% flatters: it was paid partly from cash. A sustainable through-cycle payout is closer to 30% of HLAG's dividend stream after tax, which at a mid-cycle HLAG dividend of EUR 5/share is ~US$190m after leakage, a 7.0% yield on today's price. That is adequate, not compelling, for an illiquid single-asset holdco whose controller has no discount agenda. We would not call the stock too high, but the asymmetric upside is a function of HLAG's dividend and ZIM path, not of the headline discount. Fair value under a 60% "normal" discount is CLP 63; under a Maersk-multiple NAV it is ~CLP 41 at a 30% discount. The midpoint of those two frames (~CLP 50–55) is where the stock sits.

10. Contrarian view: what the market is refusing to see

The consensus frame is wrong on both sides. Bulls say "68% discount, must close"; bears say "HLAG's price is fake, so there is no discount". The market is refusing to see that Vapores is not a discount trade at all: it is a pre-paid, event-driven claim on HLAG's capital allocation, and the two live events (ZIM and the 2027 pact-and-dividend reset) are both skewed in the holder's favour.

  • The cash has already been returned. Since 2020 the company has distributed ~CLP 104 per share against a CLP 49 price. Anyone who bought at the 2021 Quiñenco auction price (CLP 43.7) has had their cost back twice and still owns a 30% co-control block in a top-5 carrier. The "static discount" complaint ignores that the discount has been harvested through the income statement, not the share price, and that this mechanism is still intact: Quiñenco's own cash needs make it structurally impossible for CSAV to hoard.
  • ZIM is a free option, not a threat. The arbitrage market prices ZIM at US$27.5 against a US$35 bid: it expects failure. If the deal dies, HLAG is left with a US$5.9bn liquidity reserve, net debt of 0.7x and a supervisory board whose two 30% holders both want cash; a return to EUR 8+ dividends (2024 level) is the base case, and Vapores' discount has historically compressed in exactly those years. If the deal closes on acceptable terms, HLAG becomes the only top-5 carrier with a growing terminals arm and a US-friendly Israeli franchise, and the block's strategic value to Kühne, QIA and PIF rises. The only bad path is a heavily conditioned deal, and the end-September revised offer will reveal that early.
  • The block is worth more than 30% of the quote, not less. Every listed-holdco framework applies a discount for lack of control. CSAV has joint control: it co-designs the dividend policy, sits on the supervisory board, and its consent is needed for any change of control at HLAG. Kühne, 87 years old and publicly keen to own more of HLAG, is the natural buyer of that block at a premium, not a discount, at or before the 2030 pact expiry. The market is pricing a passive minority; the asset is a swing stake.
  • ~16% of the market cap is cash and tax refunds that the screens do not show because the holdco's "net debt" line reads zero and the tax assets sit in three different notes.

The mirror image bears stating too. If you believe HLAG should trade like Maersk, you must also believe HLAG's anchor holders (Kühne, QIA, PIF) have all been wrong for a decade, and that no strategic value attaches to a co-control block in a 2.3m-TEU network. The more defensible contrarian position is that HLAG's price is high but not fictitious, and that Vapores is the cheapest available entry into an asset the anchors keep paying up for.

11. Activist case

11.1 The set-up

FactorAssessment
ControllerQuiñenco and affiliates 66.5% (Luksic family owns ~83% of Quiñenco). Patient, dynastic, has bought Vapores at wide discounts and has never sold. Not historically responsive to public campaigns.
Free float33.5%, ~US$0.9bn: Chilean AFPs, local mutual funds, retail and foreign EM funds. AFPs elected 28 directors at 21 companies in the 2026 AGM season and are increasingly vocal, but they are yield-driven, not restructuring-driven.
BoardChaired by Óscar Hasbún; majority Quiñenco-designated. Cumulative voting lets a ~14% minority bloc elect one of seven directors; a directors' committee (independent-led) reviews related-party dealings.
Legal levers (Law 18.046)10% of shares can convene an EGM. Two-thirds of all issued voting shares are needed for: buybacks, mergers, capital reductions, sale of >50% of assets, dissolution. Quiñenco at 66.5% is ~0.2 points short of two-thirds, so it cannot pass those measures alone; equally, minorities cannot pass them against it.
Related-party protectionA merger with, or tender by, Quiñenco requires the directors' committee, independent valuations and (for a tender) the Chilean OPA rules; no squeeze-out mechanism exists below 95%.

11.2 What an activist could credibly ask for

  • A 5% buyback programme funded from the ~US$428m of cash and refunds: NAV-per-share accretive by ~3.6% at the current discount. This is the one ask that is cheap, legal and aligned with Quiñenco's own NAV (Quiñenco marks Vapores at market). The controller's objection would be float shrinkage and the loss of the "eventual dividend" cash buffer.
  • A formal dividend policy (e.g. 100% pass-through of HLAG dividends after tax plus a stated cash floor), replacing ad-hoc "eventual" dividends; low cost, improves predictability, likely to be accepted.
  • A secondary listing or ADR programme to let German and US investors arbitrage Vapores against HLAG directly. Modest cost; the group has shown no interest.
  • Board representation via cumulative voting: a 14% coalition (two large AFPs plus a foreign fund) can seat one director; that is realistic and has precedent in Chile.
  • Not credible: forcing a sale of the HLAG block (pact to 2030; Quiñenco veto), an in-specie distribution of HLAG shares (breaks the pact, triggers Chilean and German tax, and needs two-thirds), or a Quiñenco buy-in at NAV (no legal route to compel it).

11.3 Probability-weighted view

The activist case is weak on control and moderate on persuasion. There is no path to force outcomes against a 66.5% holder. There is, however, an unusual alignment: because Quiñenco's own holding discount is computed off Vapores' market price, a narrower Vapores discount is worth more to Quiñenco than to anyone else, and the group is cash-rich after the 2025 Nexans exit. A campaign framed as "help the controller lift its own NAV" (buyback plus policy clarity) has perhaps a one-in-three chance of partial success over two years; anything structural is below one-in-ten. The more likely "activism" is the controller's: a creep above 66.7% would be the tell that Quiñenco intends to use the supermajority tools itself, and minority holders should want to be positioned before that rather than after.
