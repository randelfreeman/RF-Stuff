Our view: undervalued, but the discount needs a catalyst. The buyback is the catalyst, and the discount should narrow over the next 12–24 months.

  • At today's price the market pays about 5.4x earnings for Astra's unlisted "crown jewels" (Astra Honda Motor, the Toyota JVs, Astra Financial and the toll roads). After stripping out the listed stakes (Rp86T) and parent net debt, those businesses earn about Rp20T a year for Astra.
  • Capital allocation has changed. The May 2026 strategic review set a low-teens (10–13%) TSR target, a 45–50% dividend payout ratio and an Rp8T buyback over the next 12 months. The buyback equals about 4.3% of the market cap, on top of the ~1.4% already repurchased since November 2025.
  • The bear case is about earnings, not accounting. Mining (United Tractors) profit is down 46% in H1 2026. Martabe gold is effectively offline until 2028. Toyota/Daihatsu market share is slipping (51% vs 53.5%). There is also a record of diversifying into low-return assets: GoTo, Hermina, Halodoc, MMLP, nickel.
  • Activism is a low-probability, high-impact path. Jardine Cycle & Carriage (JC&C) controls about 50.5%, so change has to come through Jardine. JC&C's own TSR mandate points the same way as an activist would.

5. Bull case: why own it, and why the discount narrows

Franchise and moat

  • An integrated automotive ecosystem that nobody can replicate: Toyota/Daihatsu (~51% of cars), Honda (~77% of motorcycles), plus the captive finance, insurance, dealer, parts and after-sales businesses around them. The ecosystem earns returns at every step of the vehicle's life.
  • Financial Services is the hidden compounder. NI grew from ~Rp7.3T to Rp9.0T in two years, and has grown every half-year including H1 2026 (+6%). FIF's 0.20% NPF shows underwriting built on 50 years of data from the Honda dealer network.
  • Motorcycles are Indonesia's mass mobility and the platform for ride-hailing. Demand is non-discretionary for much of the 280m population.

6. Bear case: why the discount widens or stays where it is

  • A structural threat to the Japanese franchise. Chinese brands (BYD, Wuling, Chery, VinFast and others) have EV incentives and import-duty holidays. Astra's share already slipped from 53.5% to ~51% in a year. Every 5pt of share lost costs roughly Rp0.6–0.8T of NI, and pricing power erodes as well.
  • Mining is now the swing factor and it is going the wrong way. Coal RKAB quotas are policy-driven. Martabe carries licence risk (the final decision sat with the President) and low output until 2028. The ASA (US$540m) and nickel (A$943m, now worth ~A$730m) moves recycle coal cash into new commodity risk.
  • A poor record on diversification. GoTo, Hermina (−60%), Halodoc, MMLP (a new listed non-core subsidiary after the discount debate began), Acset (repeated rescues) and the Rp2.35T H1 2026 impairment. The market charges a "capital allocation discount" until it sees disposals, not just statements.
  • The controlling shareholder's incentives. JC&C and Jardine value dividends upstream. The payout is capped at 45–50%, and big special returns or radical simplification (e.g. collapsing UT) may not suit Jardine's own funding needs.
  • Macro and FX. Rupiah weakness, Indonesian risk premia, foreign outflows from the index and policy unpredictability (export-proceeds retention, royalty changes, Danantara-era state activism) keep the whole market at trough multiples. The discount can stay static even if NAV rises.
  • A consumer credit cycle. Rising motorcycle-loan NPLs at FIF would hit the least-appreciated profit pool.

9. Is the current valuation too high?

No. On every conventional metric ASII is cheap: 6.3x trailing P/E, 0.81x book, ~8% dividend yield, a ~27% discount to SOTP, and ~5.4x implied for the unlisted core. The real question is whether earnings are about to fall further (a value trap).

  • 2026 earnings are near a trough: H1 core NI −7%, with mining at −46% absorbing a Martabe outage and quota cuts. Consensus (~Rp6,700 target) may still be too high on 2027 mining assumptions.
  • Where the multiple could be too high: the Rp36T for car JVs assumes the franchise is intact. If you believe pre-mortem path 1, 5–6x is fairer, taking about Rp200–300/sh off NAV. Even in our bear case the stock sits at a 13% discount to a Rp5,375 NAV.
  • Downside anchor: book value Rp5,763/sh, plus about 9–12% annual cash return (dividend plus buyback) at the current price.

10. Contrarian view: what the market is refusing to see

The market prices Astra as a coal-and-cars cyclical. In reality it is increasingly a consumer-finance and motorcycle-annuity business with a mining option attached.

  • In H1 2026, Automotive plus Financial Services generated about Rp10.5T of NI against only Rp2.7T from mining. That is about 70% of Rp14.9T core profit, and it grew 8%. The stock nonetheless trades on mining-trough sentiment.
  • Financial Services alone (Rp9T NI, growing, with strong asset quality) is worth Rp70–90T at peer multiples, or ~40–48% of ASII's market cap. Paired with AHM (Rp50T), those two businesses exceed the equity value implied for all the unlisted assets.
  • The mining downturn is doing the simplification for them. A weak UT makes a UT minority buy-in or collapse accretive at the bottom of the cycle, and low coal prices force the conversation about selling non-core assets. The first real disposal (AALI at peak CPO earnings is the obvious one) would change the narrative.
  • Buyback arithmetic is underappreciated. Rp8T a year on a Rp187T market cap, at a 27% discount, combined with ~8% dividends, reaches the low-teens TSR target without any earnings growth. Management has now tied its own compensation (MSOP) to that number.
  • The Chinese EV threat is real but overdone for Astra. Its value is concentrated in motorcycles, financing, insurance and after-sales. Those parts of the vehicle life cycle are brand-agnostic, and Astra could finance and distribute Chinese brands too.

Suggested activist playbook

  • Ask 1: Dispose of the non-core stakes (AALI ~Rp12T, MMLP ~Rp3.5T, ASGR ~Rp2.2T, Hermina ~Rp2T, property and healthcare). Together that is about Rp20T, or ~11% of the market cap, which could all go to buybacks below NAV.
  • Ask 2: Raise the buyback to at least Rp12T a year and commit to it for the full five-year TSR horizon.
  • Ask 3: Publish stand-alone Astra Financial accounts and study a partial IPO (10–20%) to set a market mark for the largest unlisted asset.
  • Ask 4: Make no new diversification M&A outside the three core businesses without a hurdle rate above the implied buyback return (~15%+).
  • Ask 5: At trough valuation, evaluate buying in UT's minority (~36% of shares outstanding ≈ US$1.9bn) or, alternatively, a UT special dividend.

12. Conclusion and positioning

Positive, catalyst-driven discount trade. Base-case NAV is about Rp6,400/sh (a ~27% discount). If the discount narrows to ~15%, that implies about Rp5,450 (+16%), plus ~8% dividends, for a ~20–25% 12–18 month total return. The downside is cushioned by book value (Rp5,763/sh) and the buyback. Hedge options: long ASII against short UNTR isolates the unlisted auto/FS value from mining beta, and long ASII against short JC&C plays discount layering.

What would change our mind: buyback utilisation below 50% at the six-month mark; any new non-core acquisition over Rp2T; Toyota/Daihatsu share below 47%; FIF NPF above 1%.
