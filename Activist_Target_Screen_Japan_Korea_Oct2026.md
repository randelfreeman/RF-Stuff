# Japan & Korea Activist Target Screen

## 20 structurally sound, mis-managed businesses with fixable problems

**Universe:** 124 unique securities from the supplied list: 90 listed in Tokyo and 34 in Korea.
**Data as of:** prices at 14-Oct-2026; financials LTM to 30-Jun-2026 unless stated.
**Prepared for:** internal idea generation for activist and constructivist engagement.
**Sources:**
- S&P Capital IQ, via Adaptive Retrieval and the deterministic financial API, for market data, financials and dividends.
- ProntoNLP-indexed annual securities reports, tanshin and earnings calls.
- Company filings, DART and large-holding reports.
- Press and web sources cited in the working files.

---

### Key conclusions

- **The market moved on; these 20 did not.**
  - Over five years TOPIX rose 105% and KOSPI 200 141% (price).
  - The median 5-year TSR of the 20 names is +19%, and the median shortfall against their home index is 86 percentage points.
  - These are not broken businesses: all 20 are below 1x net debt/EBITDA, and 14 hold net cash outright.
- **The problems repeat, and most are board-actionable.** Five patterns account for almost all of the value gap:
  - **Idle cash and securities.** Cash and investments exceed half of market cap at Hi-Lex, Teikoku Sen-i, Kumho, GungHo, Mitsubishi Motors (auto net cash), TS Tech and NC.
  - **Margin gaps against peers.** Omron 9.0% vs 19.8%; Daikin 8.1% vs 14.2%; NC 13.5% vs 29.7%; Hi-Lex 1.0% vs 4.7%.
  - **Undisciplined M&A and write-downs.** Dentsu (¥638bn of impairments in two years), Canon (Toshiba Medical), NAVER (Poshmark), NC (₩449bn of mobile-casual deals in four months), T. Hasegawa (US), Hodogaya (Framochem), Omron (JMDC).
  - **Holding-company and parent–subsidiary discounts.** Toei/Toei Animation, Omron/JMDC, Canon/Canon Marketing Japan, GungHo/Gravity, Hodogaya/SFC, NAVER/LY Corp.
  - **Entrenched governance.** Long-tenured chair-CEOs (Canon, Daikin, Teikoku Sen-i), takeover defences (Hodogaya, Hi-Lex) and cross-holding webs (Toei, Teikoku Sen-i).
- **The regulatory wind is behind activists in both markets.**
  - **Korea:** the third Commercial Act amendment (effective 6-Mar-2026) makes cancellation of newly acquired treasury shares the default. Cumulative voting is now mandatory for listed companies with more than ₩2tn of assets. NAVER, NC and Kumho all qualify.
  - **Japan:** shareholder proposals were filed at 106 companies in the June-2026 season. TSE cost-of-capital disclosure and ISS's 20%-of-net-assets cross-holding policy give institutions a framework to vote with activists.
- **Half the list is already "in play".**
  - An activist is already on the register at Daikin (Elliott), GungHo and KH Neochem (Strategic Capital), Wacom (AVI), Rinnai (Dalton), Teikoku Sen-i (NAVF/Dalton), Shibaura Machine (Oasis) and Hi-Lex (LIM, Catalyst/Monex). For these, the opportunity is co-investment and coalition voting.
  - **First-mover opportunities**, with no incumbent campaign: Omron, Dentsu, NAVER, Yamaha, Hodogaya, NC, T. Hasegawa, TS Tech, Canon, Toei and Mitsubishi Motors.
- **Where we would start: Omron, Dentsu and Yamaha in Japan, NAVER in Korea, and Hodogaya as an event trade.** Each combines an open, institutional register, a quantifiable value gap and a clear, board-level ask.

### Tiering

- **Tier 1 (7 names): highest conviction.** Large and identifiable value gap, a register that will engage, and in most cases a dated catalyst. Omron, Dentsu, NAVER, Yamaha, Daikin, GungHo, Hodogaya.
- **Tier 2 (8 names): strong thesis, harder execution or an incumbent activist.** NC, Kumho, Rinnai, T. Hasegawa, Shibaura Machine, KH Neochem, Wacom, Teikoku Sen-i.
- **Tier 3 (5 names): good economics, difficult registers.** TS Tech, Hi-Lex, Canon, Toei, Mitsubishi Motors. Each has a family, strategic or anchor bloc, or a scale issue, that makes a campaign a multi-year commitment or an event-driven trade.

**Support score** (1–5) is a judgement of how easily a supportive coalition of top shareholders could be assembled. It considers register composition, blocking or quasi-blocking stakes, existing activists, past vote outcomes and AGM mechanics.
- 5 = a fully institutional, visibly frustrated register with no anchor.
- 1 = effectively impossible without the anchor.

# 2. Scope, method and screen

### Screen criteria applied

| Criterion | Threshold | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Balance-sheet capacity | Net debt/EBITDA < 1.0x, or net cash | LTM to Jun-2026, S&P Capital IQ. Two stated exceptions (below). |
| Ownership | No single holder or controlling group > 40% | Family members, family asset companies and family foundations are aggregated, as are parents with subsidiaries and Korean "specially related parties". Custody nominees are not treated as holders. |
| TSR underperformance | 3- or 5-year TSR below the market index **and** below the sector benchmark | Sector benchmark is the TOPIX-17 sector index (Japan) and/or a listed peer median. Market index is TOPIX or KOSPI 200. |
| Business quality | Structurally sound franchise | Excludes loss-making or structurally challenged models (e.g. Sanken, gumi) and founder-led growth stories (SHIFT, Appier). |
| Fixability | Identifiable, board-actionable remedies | Excess cash or overhead, undisciplined M&A, sub-scale or non-core segments, undervalued assets, cross-holdings, poor management or board |

### Data integrity — read before using any number

- **TSR is approximate.** It uses split-adjusted closes on fixed dates plus fiscal-year dividends paid in the window, with no reinvestment. Index comparisons are price-only, so they *understate* each target's shortfall by roughly 2pp a year.
- **Valuation multiples** are trailing (LTM) and on S&P's TEV definition.
  - Where cash and listed stakes are large, ex-cash and look-through figures are given in the dossiers, and those are the ones that matter.
  - Peer medians are only as good as the peer set. Weak sets are flagged (for example Kumho, TS Tech, Tokyotokeiba).
- **Shareholder registers** come from the latest annual securities report or DART filing. Dates differ by company. Custody accounts aggregate many beneficial owners.
  - Several AGM vote percentages and secondary holders could not be retrieved and are marked n/a.
  - Registers should be re-pulled, and a beneficial-ownership analysis commissioned, before any approach.
- **Web-sourced facts** came from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, because direct page retrieval was unavailable. Primary documents should be checked before external use.
- **Anything described as a company's likely defence is inference**, not a quotation.


# 3. Company dossiers

How to read each dossier:
- **TSR** is approximate total shareholder return: S&P Capital IQ split-adjusted closes on 15-Oct-2021, 13-Oct-2023, 15-Oct-2025 and 14-Oct-2026, plus fiscal-year dividends per share paid in the window, with no reinvestment.
- **Peer median** uses the same method across the named listed peers.
- **Sector index** is the NEXT FUNDS TOPIX-17 sector ETF on price return. Japanese market index returns (TOPIX) and the Korean KOSPI 200 are also price return.
- **Valuation and margins** are LTM to 30-Jun-2026 (S&P Capital IQ) unless stated.
- **Shareholdings** come from the latest annual securities report (Japan) or DART filings and large-holding reports (Korea).
- **Defences** are our inference of what management would argue, usually in its own medium-term-plan language.

Fiscal year-ends differ, so the campaign calendar is staggered. Japan's Companies Act requires shareholder proposals at least 8 weeks before the AGM, from a holder of 1% or 300 voting units held for 6 months. In Korea, proposals are due 6 weeks before the AGM, and listed-company holding thresholds are 0.5–1% held for 6 months.

### How we would allocate effort

1. **Open now, privately: Omron, Dentsu and Yamaha.** All three have institutional registers with no anchor, a quantifiable gap and no incumbent campaign. The asks are portfolio simplification and capital return, which long-only holders already support. Build to 1–3% ahead of the June-2027 (Omron, Yamaha) and March-2027 (Dentsu) AGMs.
2. **Event trades with dated catalysts: Hodogaya (SFC IPO), GungHo (Sony closing), Mitsubishi Motors (lock-up expiry).** Position size should reflect the event, not a multi-year campaign.
3. **Korea: NAVER first, then NC and Kumho.** The new treasury-share and cumulative-voting rules make board-seat and cancellation demands winnable without a majority. NAVER's 26-Feb-2027 EGM is the natural moment to engage on minority protection.
4. **Co-investment where an activist is already present: Daikin, KH Neochem, Wacom, Teikoku Sen-i, Rinnai, Shibaura Machine, Hi-Lex.** These registers are primed. A supportive second fund can move a 30–43% dissident vote to a majority at Wacom and Teikoku Sen-i, and adds weight to Elliott's ask at Daikin.
5. **Long-dated, relationship-led: Canon, Toei, T. Hasegawa, TS Tech.** The value is large but unlocking it depends on a few counterparties: Canon's CEO succession, the TV Asahi cross-holding at Toei, the Hasegawa family, and Honda at TS Tech. Engage privately and let the TSE/stewardship backdrop do part of the work.

### The Korean exceptions problem

Of the 20 Korean names that pass the leverage test, nine fail the 40% control test. Three are listed subsidiaries (Doosan Bobcat, Iljin Electric, KT Alpha). Six are family-controlled small caps where the family group owns 42–62%: Ubiquoss, Daehan Flour, Seoul City Gas, It's Hanbul, SNT Motiv and Sindoh. Seven more family-run small caps were not verified. These are among the cheapest stocks screened: Seoul City Gas, It's Hanbul and Sindoh trade below net cash. Minority-shareholder proposals have started to appear: Daehan Flour shareholders forced a vote on a buyback in March 2026. They are not campaignable on the mandate's ownership test. They could be revisited as *minority-protection* situations under Korea's amended Commercial Act, but that is a different strategy.
