## 6. Business & mismanagement evidence
- **Margins have been chronically near zero.** Operating income on roughly ¥230–300bn of sales: FY21 ¥0.9bn, FY22 −¥4.7bn, FY23 ¥3.0bn, FY24 ¥0.4bn, FY25 ¥3.4bn. Net losses were −¥7.1bn in FY22 and −¥3.0bn in FY23.
- FY10/26 guidance is sales ¥401bn and OP ¥5.4bn, a 1.3% margin.
- LTM EBIT margin is 1.0% against a peer median of 4.7%. SG&A is 10.8% of sales against a peer median of ≈9.2%.
- **ACT acquisition (from Mitsui Kinzoku, agreed 2025-05-13):**
  - Mitsui put the consideration at ≈¥11.3bn and booked a ≈¥19.7bn loss.
  - In 9M FY10/26 ACT added ¥78.4bn of sales but only ¥0.6bn of OP.
  - Hi-Lex booked a ¥28.3bn bargain-purchase gain. The deal buys scale rather than returns.
- **Write-downs:**
  - FY10/24: Hungary cable plant ¥1.68bn, plus small Japanese subsidiaries.
  - FY10/25: Hi-Lex Medical's exclusive pharma distribution right was written to zero (a non-core venture), along with impairments at PT Hi-Lex Cirebon and Hi-Lex Serbia.
  - Early-retirement and headcount programmes in Korea and Spain.
- **Excess capital:** P/B of 0.38 against equity of ¥216bn, with net cash and securities exceeding market cap.

## 9. Likely management defence (inference)
- ACT doubles scale in door systems. Integration and EV-related capex need the balance sheet, and margins will improve as synergies arrive.
- Returns have already been raised: DPS from ¥46 to ¥80, two ¥1.5bn buybacks, and ¥16bn of cross-holdings sold against the 10%-of-net-assets target.
- The family bloc (31.8%) and the shareholder-approved defence policy mean that change will not come from a proxy fight.
- Low margins are attributed to customer volume and price conditions and to one-off restructuring in Europe and Asia.
