**Positioning summary.** Items without a source are [TK] unless otherwise stated.

- **Kaspi.kz** dominates retail payments, P2P, the marketplace (e-commerce, BNPL / installment "Kaspi Red") and unsecured consumer lending. Its moat is its two-sided ecosystem, and its consumer-lending NIM is far higher than Halyk's. Halyk's 17.8% retail-loan share against a 28% asset share shows Halyk is underweight in consumer credit relative to Kaspi.
- **Halyk** dominates corporate banking (c.48% of corporate loans) and the large corporate and state-company relationship business. It also holds the strongest retail deposit franchise (27%+), heritage branch and ATM coverage, and payroll, pension and social-payment flows (heritage of the former State Savings Bank [TK]).
- **State development institutions** compete in subsidised segments: Baiterek group (Otbasy for mortgages, DBK for large project finance, Damu for SME guarantees and subsidies, KazAgro/Agrarian Credit Corp for agriculture) [TK]. Otbasy's rising share (to 8%) reflects state mortgage programmes. Halyk's mortgage share was NOT FOUND.
- **BCC** is the #3 universal bank, with strong SME and mortgage presence and a digital push. Its scale increased via Alfa KZ (2022).
- **ForteBank** is a corporate/SME bank with a growing retail book. The Home Credit acquisition gives it POS and consumer-lending capability, positioning it as a more direct retail challenger.
- **Freedom Bank** is the fastest-growing bank. It runs a brokerage-led ecosystem (Freedom SuperApp, Arbuz, Ticketon, insurance, telecom [TK]) and prices deposits aggressively.
- **Alatau City Bank (ex-Jusan)** is now owned by Kaspi co-founder V. Kim. It is pivoting to "Alatau City" crypto- and payments-friendly positioning (wiki.private.law [u]). Watch for Kaspi-style execution.

## 9. Takeover feasibility

| Factor | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Control** | Holding Group ALMEX (Timur Kulibayev & Dinara Kulibayeva) held **c.62%** after selling 7.6% in Nov 2025, down from 69.5–69.77% before. Free float is c.38% (GDRs on LSE/AIX plus shares on KASE). Sources: en.orda.kz; kase.kz; Substack analysis [u for the 38%]. |
| **Lock-up** | ALMEX accepted a **365-day lock-up** from closing on 25 Nov 2025, so it expires around **late Nov 2026**, with customary exceptions (kase.kz / investing.com). This is the near-term overhang and catalyst: further sell-downs become possible after expiry. |
| **Stake-sale rumours** | Reuters (via TradingView, 2025): "Main shareholder in Halyk Bank weighs a partial stake sale." The initial plan was c.4%, upsized to 7.6% on demand. No 2026 rumour of a control sale was found (search coverage was limited). |
| **Regulatory** | ARDFM consent is required to become a "major participant" or bank holding company. Thresholds are 10% for a major participant and 25% for a bank holding company under the Banking Law [TK]. The ARDFM consent processes in 2024–25 deals (Bereke, Jusan/Alatau, Home Credit) took roughly 2–6 months from SPA. |
| **Foreign-buyer precedents** | China CITIC (Altyn 60%, 2018 [TK]), Lesha Bank / Qatar (Bereke 100%, 2024), PPF / Czech (Home Credit KZ, via Russian HCF Bank until 2022). Plus a sale to a local buyer by HSBC (2014). |
| **Feasibility view** | A **full takeover of Halyk is impractical**. Its size (US$6bn+ market cap; 28% of sector assets; c.48% of corporate loans) means systemic-importance and political-economy constraints (ALMEX is controlled by the former president's family). Antitrust overlap would rule out any domestic bank buyer. Incremental ALMEX sell-downs to portfolio investors are the realistic path. |
