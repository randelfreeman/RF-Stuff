- **Net interest income:** rose despite an interest expense rise "mainly as a result of the increase in average interest rate and balances of amounts due to customers" (company release, as returned by search) [u].
- **NIM:**
  - Adjusted for the tighter minimum reserve requirements (MRR), NIM would be **7.2%**. The MRR drag is about 40bp [c].
  - Management called it "normalized"; that wording applies to cost of risk (see below).
- **Cost of risk:** 1.4%, flat yoy. Management described it as a "normalized level" [u].
- **Cost/income:** 19.2% "amid lower operating income". Opex rose +5.5% yoy, driven by salary indexation, IT and VAT [u].
- **Net fee and commission income:** -19.6% yoy for 1H, on BNPL regulation and VAT on certain banking services. 2Q fees rose +18.4% qoq [c].
- **Insurance:** posted a loss or weak result in 2Q. Causes were OSAGO (motor third-party liability) premiums lagging inflation, higher loss ratios, and a **one-off change in the insurance liability valuation model** [c: Freedom + Yahoo/GuruFocus summary].
- **FX income:** fell against a high base [u].

**Balance sheet and asset quality**

- **Loans:**
  - Total loans rose +4.1% qoq [u].
  - Retail gross loans were KZT ~4.8trn at 1 Jul 2026, +10.7% yoy [u].
  - Koshenov said growth was broad-based across corporate and retail.
- **Deposits:**
  - Retail deposits were KZT 8.3trn at 1 Jul 2026, +10.5% yoy [u].
  - Deposits are 80.8% of total liabilities [u].
  - No outflow flagged.
- **NPL 90+:**
  - **KZT 715bn versus KZT 358bn** a year earlier. The ratio rose to **5.3% from 3.0%** [u].
  - Management attributed part of the rise to a **temporary pause in sales of problem retail loans to collection agencies** ("partly mechanical") [u].
  - Provisions rose to **5.0% of gross loans from 4.4% at YE25** [u].
  - By segment: corporate NPL 90+ 2.6% with 122.6% coverage; SME 5%; **retail 9.2%** [u].
- **Capital:**
  - Unconsolidated k1-1, k1-2 and k2 were **all 19.0%** at 30 Jun 2026, versus 21.0% at end-1Q26 [u].
  - The minimums are k1 9.5%, k1-2 10.5% and k2 12%. These include a 3% conservation buffer and a 1% systemic buffer [u].
  - The decline came from **dividend payments**; RWA rose +3.2% YTD [u].
  - CET1 on a consolidated Basel basis: NOT FOUND.

**Guidance (FY2026), reaffirmed**
- Net income **~KZT 1trn**; **ROAE 29%**; **NIM 6.8%**; **C/I 18–20%**; **CoR 1.4–1.5%** [c].
- Net loan growth: **total 9–12%**, of which retail 8–10% and corporate & SME 10–13% [u: Quartr IR summary].
- **Changed:** net fee income growth guidance was **cut from +5–10% to about -10%** [u: Freedom Investments].
- **Implied H2 hurdle [d]:** KZT 1,000bn minus 447.6bn leaves **KZT 552.4bn for 2H26**.

  - The ROAE guidance of 29% compares with a 1H26 run-rate of roughly 24–25% [d from KASE 12.29%].

**What management emphasised, with quotes and paraphrases (all [u], from partial transcripts and summaries)**
- **On MRR pressure:** Koshenov described the MRR effect as "measurable and temporary" (search-summary wording, presented as a quote).
- **On growth:** "we might see some slowdown in growth in the second half" (Koshenov, per the Yahoo/GuruFocus highlights).
- **On margin:** management pointed to early signs that deposit rates are stabilising. The transcript excerpt is cut off where he discusses the base-rate cycle.
- **On retail strategy:** a pivot from BNPL and unsecured lending toward **collateralised products (auto loans, mortgages)**. Management is willing to **reactivate BNPL when risk-return improves** (paraphrase).

- **On dividends:**

  - He said the bank will "strive to stick to" its **dividend policy of a 50%+ payout**.
  - The **second dividend payment was moved earlier, to 3Q**, "to show confidence in earnings" (paraphrase).

  - B2B payment volumes rose +25% [u].

### 2.3 1Q 2026: results and call on 18 May 2026 (3:00pm London / 7:00pm Almaty)

**Headline results**
- **Net income:** **KZT 234,809m, -14.6% yoy** [c: Investegate RNS, Interfax-Kazakhstan, Quartr]. EPS KZT 21.57 versus 25.29 [u].
- **NIM:** **7.0% versus 7.5%**; MRR-adjusted 7.3% [c].
- **Net interest income** before credit loss expense rose +2.3% to KZT 333.4bn [u].
- **Fees:** net fee and commission income fell **-26.1%** [c].
- **Cost of risk:** **1.5% versus 1.2%** (Freedom) [u].
- **Effective tax rate:** **16.6% versus 15.6%** under the new Tax Code, partly offset by a deferred tax benefit (Edison) [u].
- **Retail:** retail loan **issuance fell -18.3% yoy** on the BNPL and unsecured-lending rules. Retail gross loans were KZT 4.6trn, +8.3% [u].
- **SME:** the SME book rose **+27.7%** yoy and the digital SME portfolio **+40.3%** [u].
- **Deposits:** retail deposits were KZT 7.9trn, +10.4% [u].
- **Capital:** capital adequacy was ~21.0% at end-1Q [u].

**Messaging and quotes (from the official transcript as surfaced by search, https://halykbank.com/storage/779/Call-Transcript.pdf) [u]**
- "The group performance in the first quarter of 2026 was in line with our expectations and supports the full year 2026 guidance communicated earlier."
- "Net interest margin declined to 7% for first quarter 2026 compared to 7.5% in first quarter 2025, mainly due to the introduction of new minimum reserve requirements coefficients. Excluding this regulatory impact, adjusted NIM would have been 7.3%."
- Koshenov on the prior-year comparison: "On the other income, actually, it was a one-off profit which was recorded in the first quarter last year related to some work out in our stressed asset SPV".

- Freedom's summary has the CFO saying it was **too early to change guidance**. The exact phrase "too early" was not confirmed in the transcript.
- Guidance was unchanged, and management expected fee income to improve from 2Q (paraphrase) [u].

**Q&A themes:** other income versus the prior-year one-off; the MRR step-up from April; retail slowdown (stricter underwriting, seasonality); fees.

### How sentiment shifted
1. **Late 2025 was the high-water mark.** In 3Q25 management talked up 30%+ ROE, NI above KZT 1trn, and a 7% NIM "sustained into 2026". The base-rate hikes and consumer-lending tightening were treated as volume issues only, explicitly said not to hurt profitability.
2. **March 2026 was the pivot to defence.** Halyk guided its first profit decline in years. The vocabulary shifted to external causes: "high rates, regulatory tightening, slower economic start". The dividend (~60%) became the main source of positive messaging.
3. **1Q26 was "in line with expectations".** Management used expectation-management language and attributed the decline to regulation (MRR, BNPL). It pre-announced the next MRR step-up while refusing to change guidance. The adjusted-NIM framing (7.3% "ex regulation") appeared for the first time.
4. **2Q26 brought more hedging.**
   - Guidance was kept on headline NI and ROE, while sub-guidance (fees) was cut and an H2 growth slowdown was flagged.
   - Asset quality became a visible new negative (NPL 90+ 5.3%).
   - Management tried to anchor confidence through capital return, pulling the second dividend tranche forward.
   - The gap between reaffirmed guidance (implying H2 NI up ~4% yoy) and the run-rate (2Q down 16%) is now the key credibility test for the 3Q26 print expected around mid-November.
5. **Net trajectory:** tone moved from +3 to -2, outlook from +2 to -1, and asset quality from +1 to -2. Capital and dividend held at about +2 throughout and are the one consistently positive pillar.

- URL: https://halykbank.com/results-and-presentations?tab=consensus

### 4.2 Segment and line drivers
- **Net interest income:**
  - NII rose modestly (+2.6% 1H, +3.0% 2Q). Asset yields kept rising: interest income was a record ~KZT 725bn (+10% yoy).
  - Funding costs rose faster: interest expense was +21.6% in 1H, on both higher deposit rates and higher balances.
  - The MRR sterilised a larger share of the balance sheet at zero yield, costing about 40bp of NIM.
- **Fees:**
  - Fees fell -19.6% on the regulatory shutdown and repricing of BNPL and on VAT on bank services. Payments growth (B2B +25%) did not offset it.
  - 2Q fees recovered by +18.4% qoq.
- **Insurance:** swung to a loss in 2Q (OSAGO tariffs lagging inflation, higher loss ratios, a one-off liability-model change).
- **Retail lending:**
  - The mix is moving from unsecured and BNPL to auto loans and mortgages.
  - Retail gross loans were up +10.7% yoy, but 1Q issuance was down -18.3%.
- **SME:** the strongest segment (+27.7% yoy in 1Q; digital SME +40.3%). The corporate book is described as broad-based.

### 4.3 NIM and cost/income: trends and why
- **NIM path [c except 2Q, which is d]:**
  - 7.5% (1Q25) → 7.3% (1H25) → 7.1% (3Q25) → 6.9% (4Q25) → 7.0% (1Q26) → 6.8% (1H26).
  - That implies about 6.6% in 2Q26 [d].
- **Why NIM fell:**
  1. New MRR coefficients from Sep 2025, with a further step-up in April 2026 (about 40bp drag).
  2. Higher deposit costs following the base-rate hikes (the average customer deposit rate went from 9.6% to 9.9% in 4Q25).
  3. Less high-yield unsecured and BNPL lending.
- **Mitigants cited:** early signs that deposit rates are stabilising, and active securities-portfolio rebalancing (Freedom 1Q note).
- **C/I:** 17.2% (1H25) → 17.5% (FY25) → **19.2% (1H26)**.
  - Drivers: opex +5.5% (salary indexation, IT, VAT) against falling operating income.
  - Still inside the 18–20% guidance, but at the upper half.

### 4.4 Guidance and tone changes
- **Kept:** NI ~KZT 1trn, ROAE 29%, NIM 6.8%, C/I 18–20%, CoR 1.4–1.5%, loan growth 9–12%.
- **Cut:** net fee income growth, from +5–10% to about -10% [u].
- **New hedges:** "some slowdown in growth in the second half"; MRR effects called "measurable and temporary".
- **New confidence signal:** the second dividend tranche was pulled into 3Q (paid 7 Sep).

### 4.5 Balance-sheet flags
- **Liquidity and deposits:** no outflow. Retail deposits rose +10.5% yoy to KZT 8.3trn, and deposits are 80.8% of liabilities. The issue is **cost** of deposits, not availability. **No flag.**
- **Loan quality: flag.**
  - NPL 90+ doubled yoy to KZT 715bn (5.3%). Retail NPL 90+ is 9.2%.
  - Management cites a temporary pause in sales of NPLs to collectors. Watch for normalisation, or for a CoR catch-up in 2H.
  - Provisions/gross loans rose to 5.0% from 4.4%.
- **Capital: mild flag.**
  - Ratios fell from 21.0% to 19.0% in one quarter after dividends; RWA rose +3.2% YTD.
  - The buffer is still about 700bp over the k2 minimum of 12%.
  - Management explicitly reserves capital for growth.
