## 0. How to read this dossier (method, limits, tags)

**Tags**
- No tag: confirmed by two or more independent sources, or by a primary source (NBK, stat.gov.kz, rating-agency release), in this session's search results.
- **[u]**: single source found this session. Treat as provisional.
- **[K]**: analyst background knowledge from before June 2026. **Not re-verified this session.** Check against the primary source before publication.
- **NOT FOUND**: searched for, or in scope, but no usable evidence obtained. Nothing has been made up to fill gaps.

**Big-picture context for 2026 (confirmed)**
- **Middle East war and Hormuz shock.** Military action began on 28 Feb 2026, and the Strait of Hormuz was effectively closed. Brent hit about USD 138/bbl on 7 Apr 2026 (EIA, via AOL/Motley Fool; MECouncil). It is about USD 110-115/bbl in late October 2026. The World Bank calls this "the largest oil market disruption in history".
- **Kazakhstan has gained on price but lost on volume.** CPC terminal attacks and Tengiz outages cut 9M26 oil output by 8.3% y/y. Even so, GDP grew 4.2% over 9M26 and the tenge has strengthened to about KZT 449/USD.
- **Easing cycle under way.** The NBK has cut from 18.00% to 15.75% since June 2026. The latest cut was today, 23 Oct 2026.

**Quantified effect on bank profitability**
- **No published estimate was found** from ARDFM, the rating agencies or brokers. Halyk management commentary on its effective tax rate is also NOT FOUND (Halyk 1H26 summaries do not mention the CIT change).
- **Analyst arithmetic (not sourced).** Assume unchanged pre-tax profit and that the higher rate applies to the whole tax base. Moving the statutory rate from 20% to 25% cuts net profit by (1-0.25)/(1-0.20)-1 = **-6.25%**. If income from business lending stays at 20%, the hit is smaller and depends on the retail and treasury share of taxable profit. For a retail-heavy, treasury-heavy bank like Halyk, the drag might be about -3% to -6% of net income. The effective rate also depends on tax-exempt income such as government securities, whose 2026 treatment is NOT FOUND.
- **Other channels.**
  - Bank fees: the VAT now charged on some fees (above) is a direct drag.
  - Higher VAT more broadly (12% to 16%): adds to inflation and weighs on consumer real incomes and retail-loan affordability.
  - Fiscal: the VAT increase supports fiscal consolidation, which the rating agencies view positively.

## 8. Summary: tailwinds and headwinds for Halyk

**Tailwinds**
1. **Oil windfall and a stronger sovereign.** Brent averaged ~USD 90+ in 2026 YTD (NBK assumes USD 89). The sovereign was upgraded by S&P to BBB in Aug-26, and Moody's (Baa1) and Fitch (BBB) are stable. Halyk's S&P outlook went to Positive. This should lower country risk premia and funding costs.
2. **Easing cycle.** The base rate is down 225bp to 15.75%. Disinflation has run 12 months (CPI 9.3%, NBK 2027 target 6.5-8.5%). Lower rates should revive loan demand. Halyk's large securities book benefits from revaluation as yields fall.
3. **Steady non-oil growth.** The non-resource economy grew 5.4% in 9M26. Fiscal stimulus and a large investment programme support corporate and SME lending, which also keeps the **20% CIT rate** under the business-lending carve-out.
4. **Stronger tenge** (~449 vs 505.73 at end-2025). This helps borrowers with FX debt and asset quality, though it lowers the KZT value of FX-denominated income.
5. **Tough rules hurt weaker competitors more.** The rules squeeze MFOs and smaller retail lenders hardest. Halyk's capital (CET1 ~18-19% per Fitch) and funding scale should let it gain share.

**Headwinds**
1. **Tax.**
   - Bank CIT rose to **25%** (except business loans), for an estimated -3% to -6% hit to net income (analyst calculation).
   - **VAT on some bank fees** has arrived, with Halyk's 1H26 net fees down 19.6%.
   - VAT rose to 16% overall, which squeezes consumers.
2. **Macroprudential tightening on retail.**
   - Sectoral CCyB of 2% (from Apr-26).
   - Pillar 2 add-on of up to 4.5% and a stress-test buffer of up to 3%.
   - Risk weights of up to 350% on long-tenor unsecured loans (draft).
   - Loan-size caps (KZT 9.5m) and a 5-year tenor cap.
   - GEIR cap cut to 46%; possible total-debt cap of 8x income from 2027.
   - Together these mean slower, lower-yield retail growth (sector unsecured +3.1% in 6M26).
3. **NIM compression.** MRR raised in three stages to ~KZT 4.3trn of reserves. Base-rate cuts reprice assets faster than deposits. Halyk's NIM fell from 7.3% to 6.8% in 1H26.
4. **Asset quality.** Halyk problem loans were 8.6% (Jun-26); MFO stress spills over; the personal-bankruptcy regime is in place.
5. **Oil volume and CPC risk.** Repeated Ukrainian drone strikes on the CPC terminal and Tengiz outages cut 2026 oil output (~96 mt vs a 100.5 mt plan). Tengiz maintenance is deferred to 2027, and NBK oil-price assumptions fall to USD 75 (2027) and USD 65 (2028).
6. **Inflation and fiscal risk.** The NBK sees risks tilted up, a stronger fiscal impulse, NFRK withdrawals outpacing gains, and a possible end-2026 inflation uptick from base effects. Further cuts are unlikely before Dec-26.
7. **Political and ownership tail risk.** There is a history of politically driven debt relief (2019) and the 2023 asset-return law. Halyk's controlling shareholders are linked to the Nazarbayev family. No action has been observed.
8. **Sanctions and compliance.** OFAC/EU secondary-sanction exposure on Russia-linked flows (no Kazakh bank designations found).
