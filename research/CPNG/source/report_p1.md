# 1. Executive summary

**The debate.** On 18-Nov-2025 Coupang discovered that a former employee had accessed personal data on up to 33 million customer accounts ([#0]({S}SEC000253203320-0), [#2]({S}SEC000253203320-2)). Since then the shares have fallen 61% from the September-2025 high of US$34.08 to a low of US$13.35 (2-Oct-2026) ‡. The Asian Century Stocks newsletter (11-Oct-2026, "A temporary glitch?") asks whether this is a Vernon-style "glitch", a temporary market overreaction, or a lasting change. Our answer: **at the customer level the evidence says glitch; at the P&L, balance-sheet and regulatory level the damage is real but finite and mostly priced.**

**What the data shows**

- **Customers came back.**
  - Product Commerce active customers recovered to [24.7m]({S}SEC000264557825-29) in Q2'26, above the Q4'25 level.
  - WOW membership is above pre-incident levels and at an all-time high ([#39]({S}T000003779883-39), [#271]({S}T000003779883-271)).
  - Returners skew to higher spenders and "have not split that spend with other alternatives" ([#268]({S}T000003779883-268)).
  - Spend excluding non-returning customers grew ~16% against 8% reported Product Commerce growth ([#43]({S}T000003779883-43)). This is a company-defined metric.
- **Margins did not come back yet.**
  - Product Commerce adjusted EBITDA margin fell from [8.8%]({S}T000003578475-86) (Q3'25) to [5.1%]({S}T000003779883-169) (Q2'26).
  - Consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin fell from [4.5%]({S}T000003578475-122) to [1.8%]({S}T000003779883-201).
  - GAAP operating losses were [-US$242m]({C}259645823&uctSearch=-242.000000) in Q1'26 and [-US$556m]({C}264559057&uctSearch=-556.000000) in Q2'26. Q2 includes ~US$410m of privacy fines ([#8]({S}SEC000261784634-8)).
  - Management now targets pre-incident Product Commerce margins "by mid-2027" ([#220]({S}T000003779883-220)). The recovery date has slipped once already.
- **Reported USD numbers understate the business.**
  - The KRW hit a 15-year-plus low in Q2'26 ([#150]({S}T000003779883-150)) and took 7pts off Product Commerce growth ([#60]({S}SEC000264559057-60)).
  - Consolidated revenue grew +10% in constant currency but +4% in USD ([#180]({S}T000003779883-180)).
  - The ~US$1.2bn customer voucher programme was netted against revenue. Redemption ended mid-April 2026 ([#16]({S}SEC000264559057-16), [#17]({S}SEC000264559057-17)), which flatters 2027 comparisons.
- **Valuation is near a five-year low on sales but not on earnings.**
  - LTM EV/Sales is 0.79x [d], against year-end readings of 1.10-1.29x in 2022-25 [d] and a peer median of 1.29x.
  - EV/EBITDA and P/E are optically high on trough earnings.
  - On our EV/Sales vs EBIT-margin regression across 14 listed peers, CPNG sits on the line. The market is valuing Coupang as if today's ~1-2% margins are permanent.

**Why BUY now (event-driven framing)**

1. **Catalysts.** The Q3'26 print is on 3-Nov-2026 ([Q3 2026 Earnings Call]({D}upcoming-doc-704377650)). 2027 guidance is promised "towards the end of the year" ([#221]({S}T000003779883-221)). The KFTC Eats-WOW bundling decision is pending ([#452]({S}SEC000264559057-452)).
2. **Downside cushion.** Large buybacks: 43.7m shares (US$850m) in H1'26 ([#369]({S}SEC000264559057-369)), with US$907m of authorisation remaining ([#140]({S}SEC000264559057-140)).
3. **Asymmetry.** Bear US$9.5 (-40%) / base US$21.0 (+33%) / bull US$31.7 (+100%), weighted 25/50/25. The probability-weighted value is US$20.8 (+31%) [e].

**What must go right**

- Product Commerce margin recovers toward ~8% through 2027.
- Developing Offerings losses fall from US$950m-1bn in 2026 ([#223]({S}T000003779883-223)).
- No forced Eats-WOW unbundling or night-delivery ban.
- Merchant settlement-period rules from Dec-2026 do not unwind much of the negative-working-capital float ([#619]({S}SEC000255973354-619)).

**What would make us wrong**

- FCF stays near zero (LTM ~US$99m [d]) while the buyback is funded by the revolver ([#165]({S}SEC000264559057-165)).
- Korean regulators keep fining. Cumulative accrued fines since 2024 are ~US$531m [d].
- Naver and the Chinese cross-border platforms keep the share they won during the incident.

# 2. A layman's guide: what Coupang does and how it makes money

**In one sentence:** Coupang is "the Amazon of South Korea". It sells almost everything online and delivers it, usually overnight, through its own warehouses and drivers. Most of its loyal customers pay a monthly fee (WOW membership) for free, fast delivery and extras.

**How it makes money**

1. **It buys products and resells them (first-party, "1P").** Coupang buys from brands such as CJ CheilJedang ([#16]({S}T000003370588-16)), stores the goods in its own fulfilment centres, and earns the gap between selling price and cost. This is gross revenue, so it is big revenue at a thin margin ([#1745]({S}SEC000255973354-1745)).
2. **It rents its shop window and warehouses to other merchants (third-party, "3P", and Fulfilment & Logistics by Coupang, "FLC").** It earns commissions and fulfilment fees. Only the fee is booked as revenue, so revenue is smaller but margins are higher ([#1745]({S}SEC000255973354-1745), [#145]({S}T000003665452-145)).
3. **It sells advertising** to suppliers and merchants who want to appear higher in search. Much of the supplier ad money reduces cost of goods rather than counting as revenue ([#1761]({S}SEC000255973354-1761)).
4. **It charges a membership fee (WOW).** WOW includes free Rocket Delivery and returns, Rocket Fresh grocery, Coupang Eats discounts and Coupang Play streaming ([#1755]({S}SEC000255973354-1755)).
5. **It is incubating new businesses** (Developing Offerings): Coupang Eats (food delivery), Coupang Play (video), Coupang Pay (payments), Taiwan (a second Rocket Delivery country), Rocket Now (Japan food delivery) and Farfetch (global luxury marketplace) ([#28]({S}SEC000264559057-28)).

**How each division contributes (FY2025)**

| Division | Revenue | Share of revenue | Adj. EBITDA | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Product Commerce (Korea) | [US$29.6bn]({S}SEC000255972090-50) | ~86% [d] | [US$2.5bn, 8.4% margin]({S}SEC000255972090-54) | The profit engine: retail, marketplace, Fresh, ads, WOW |
| Developing Offerings | [US$4.9bn]({S}SEC000255972090-55) | ~14% [d] | [-US$995m]({S}SEC000255972090-56) | The investment arm, led by Taiwan, Eats and Farfetch |
| **Group** | [US$34.5bn]({S}SEC000255972090-39) | 100% | [~US$1.5bn, 4.3%]({S}SEC000255972090-45) | Over 90% of revenue earned in Korea ([#1858]({S}SEC000255973354-1858)) |

So the Korean core earns roughly US$2.5bn of adjusted EBITDA, and about 40% of that is reinvested in the new ventures. Investors are really debating two things: how profitable the Korean core can become, and whether Taiwan will repeat the Korean playbook.

# 4. Business model, key drivers, customers, suppliers, competitors and geographies

## 4.1 Business model in simple terms

Coupang runs **an owned, vertically integrated network** of more than [78m sq ft]({S}SEC000255973354-1295) of fulfilment, sortation and delivery space, up from 67m a year earlier ([#1145]({S}SEC000237408246-1145)). It is staffed largely by direct employees plus "tens of thousands" of independent delivery partners ([#88]({S}SEC000255973354-88)). Free next-day "Rocket Delivery", launched "over 10 years ago" ([#31]({S}T000003711263-31)), is the customer proposition. The economic loop works like this:

1. Selection, speed and price bring in customers.
2. Customers raise purchase frequency and spend.
3. Each cohort's spend rises year after year ([#69]({S}T000003779883-69)).
4. Higher volume lowers cost per unit on the fixed network ([#54]({S}T000003779883-54)).
5. Lower unit cost funds lower prices.

The weakness of the model is the flip side of that loop. Much of the cost base is fixed and sized ahead of demand, so a demand shock shows up directly in margins ([#166]({S}T000003711263-166), [#58]({S}T000003779883-58)). That is exactly what happened in 2026.

## 4.4 Customers, suppliers, competitors

- **Customers.** Mass-market Korean consumers: 24.7m active customers. WOW membership was 14m at end-2023, the last count disclosed ([#41]({S}SEC000218768452-41)), and is now above pre-incident levels. No customer concentration exists.
- **Suppliers.** A broad 1P vendor base. The only named supplier verified in the tools is CJ CheilJedang, which reported record "Coupang 1P sales" after resuming supply ([#16]({S}T000003370588-16), [#98]({S}T000003307391-98)). Other named suppliers are AWS for cloud ([#695]({S}SEC000255973354-695)) and Samil PwC as auditor. Large brands (Samsung, LG, P&G and others) are not named for Coupang in the tools [u].
- **Competitors.** Naver (marketplace plus Plus membership, Kurly N Mart, CJ Logistics fulfilment alliance) ([#321]({S}NONSEC000256804358-321), [#326]({S}NONSEC000256804358-326)). AliExpress and Temu entered Korea in earnest in 2024 ([#308]({S}NONSEC000256804358-308)). Lotte ON ([#92]({S}NONSEC000256750115-92)). Shinsegae/Gmarket/SSG, 11st and Kurly [u as named rivals]. In food delivery: Baemin (Delivery Hero, subject to a pending Uber offer ([#1089]({S}SEC000263635795-1089))). In Taiwan: momo.com and others.

## 4.5 Key markets and geographies

- Over 90% of revenue is booked by Korean entities ([#1858]({S}SEC000255973354-1858)). The rest comes mainly from the US, UK (Farfetch), Taiwan, Europe and APAC.
- Korean online retail sales were KRW272.0tn in 2025 (+4.9%). Online's share of retail rose to 41.5%, from 34.6% in 2022 (Statistics Korea, quoted by Naver; [#305]({S}NONSEC000256804358-305)).
- The newsletter's claim of a ~28% Coupang share of Korean e-commerce is **not verifiable** in S&P/ProntoNLP [u]. The CEO describes "just a small share" of the markets Coupang competes in ([#355]({S}T000003711263-355)), and wallet share below US peer levels ([#80]({S}T000003779883-80)).

## 4.6 Contracts and payment terms (as disclosed)

- **Supplier payments.** Accounts payable were 94 days of COGS at FY25 [d], against inventory of 34 days. The cash conversion cycle has been negative for four years: -42, -64, -54 and -57 days for FY22-25 [d].
- **Payables flatter the true terms.** Payables include money collected on behalf of 3P merchants ([#1745]({S}SEC000255973354-1745)), so the true supplier terms are shorter than the reported days.
- **Supplier finance.** US$485m (Dec-25) and US$430m (Mar-26) of confirmed invoices sat in a supplier-finance programme on original terms ([#366]({S}SEC000259645823-366), [#1939]({S}SEC000255973354-1939)).
- **Regulatory change from Dec-2026.** The EFTA amendment and a proposed Large-Scale Retail Business Act change will shorten merchant settlement periods. Management says this "may reduce our accounts payable balances and cash on hand" ([#619]({S}SEC000255973354-619)).
- **Other contracts.** WOW is a monthly subscription recognised over time ([#1761]({S}SEC000255973354-1761)). Open purchase orders are generally cancellable ([#1507]({S}SEC000255973354-1507)). No long-term supply contracts are disclosed.
