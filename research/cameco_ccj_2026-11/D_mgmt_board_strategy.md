## 0. Key takeaways

1. **CEO succession has started but is not finished.**
   - Tim Gitzel is still CEO. He gave up the "President" title on 1 Sep 2025 [v].
   - Grant Isaac became **President & COO** and Heidi Shockey became **CFO** on the same date [v].
   - Isaac now looks like the heir apparent [d]. No CEO change had been announced as of the searches run.
   - 2025 saw unusually heavy executive turnover. Three executives announced retirements and one new officer joined [v]. COO Brian Reilly and CLO Sean Quinn moved to advisory roles ahead of their March 2026 retirements [v]. Marketing changed hands twice in 15 months [v].
2. **Pay is rising quickly.**
   - The CEO's long-term incentive (LTI) target rose from 370% to 470% of salary in 2025, justified by the larger scope after Westinghouse [v].
   - CEO 2024 total direct compensation was C$7.64m [v]. A secondary source puts 2025 total at about C$10.96m [u].
   - Say-on-pay support is high and has risen: 92.7% at the 2019 AGM, 95.4% in 2023 and 98.1% in 2024 [v]. The 2026 resolution passed; the percentage was NOT FOUND.
3. **Westinghouse is now the main strategic lever.**
   - The Oct 2025 binding term sheet with the US Department of Commerce covers at least US$80bn of AP1000s. It gives the US Government a **Participation Interest equal to 20% of Westinghouse cash distributions above US$17.5bn**. It also lets the government force an IPO by Jan 2029 if the valuation is at least US$30bn [v].
   - **No definitive agreements had been reported as of Nov 2026** [v, absence in the Q2/Q3 2026 filings found].
   - Follow-ups so far are a DOE conditional loan commitment of up to US$17.5bn for long-lead items for up to 10 AP1000s (Jun 2026) [v] and a non-binding Korean framework of up to US$120bn (Sep 2026) [v].
4. **The board is small, refreshed and mostly independent.**
   - 9 directors were elected in 2026 [v]. Catherine Gignac has chaired since 1 Dec 2023 [v].
   - Six of the nine have joined since Sep 2023 or have no verified start date. Four confirmed joiners: Cook-Searson and Minière (Sep 2023), Inkster and Kukielski (2025) [v].
   - Confirmed leavers: Deranger and Gowans (May 2024), Ian Bruce (died Oct 2023) and Daniel Camus (off the 2026 slate) [v/d].
   - Indigenous representation: Chief Tammy Cook-Searson of the Lac La Ronge Indian Band [v].
5. **Red flags are moderate.**
   - The Westinghouse US-government upside is still a term sheet and was presented as "transformational".
   - Operations were disrupted repeatedly in 2026: Key Lake halted by flooding in May, an extended Key Lake outage in Q3, a two-week Cigar Lake/McClean Lake stoppage, and JV Inkai acid and transport risk.
   - In the CRA dispute, C$480m was still held as of Mar 2023, and its later status was NOT FOUND.
   - A possible related-party/independence point to check: one director is Chief of a First Nation whose business arm may supply Cameco [m].
   - Statutory foreign-ownership limits shield management from takeover (25% non-resident voting cap [v]).

## 5. Founder vs professional manager assessment

- **Origins.** Cameco was created in **1988** by merging two Crown entities, Saskatchewan Mining Development Corporation (provincial) and Eldorado Nuclear (federal). It was privatised by IPO in 1991; both governments had sold down by around 2002 [m, well-established history; verify dates].
- **No founder or controlling shareholder exists.** The statute caps any single holder at 15% [m] and non-resident voting at 25% [v]. Cameco is therefore a **widely held company run by professional managers**.
- **CEO lineage** [m except where marked]:
  - Bernard Michel, about 1993 to 2003.
  - Jerry Grandey, 2003 to Jun 2011 [v for retirement and succession].
  - Tim Gitzel, Jul 2011 to present [v].
  - Every CEO has been an internal or industry-insider succession. That shows strong continuity of culture and of the "contract-first, tier-one" commercial philosophy.
- **Alignment:** Management owns a negligible share of the company (about 0.24% insider ownership [u]). Individual alignment is still meaningful: the CEO held about C$45m of shares and units at end-2023 [d].
- **Implications [d]:**
  - **Positives:** disciplined, institutional decision-making; long tenures that make strategy consistent (supply discipline 2016–2022, a long-term contracting book); and board refreshment with relevant expertise.
  - **Negatives:** the risk of an insulated, long-tenured management team, made worse by statutory takeover protection; pay that has ratcheted up with company scope rather than with shareholder-owner economics; and a 15-year CEO tenure with succession only partly executed.
