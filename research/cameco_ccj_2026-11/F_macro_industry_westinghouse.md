## 2. Supply side

### 2.1 Kazatomprom (KAP)

- **2025 guidance cut (Aug 2024).** KAP cut 2025 guidance, citing sulphuric acid shortages and construction delays [m].
- **2026 nominal level.** The subsoil-use level (100% basis) is cut from 32,777 tU (~85 Mlb) to 29,697 tU, about -10%. Most of the ~3,000 tU reduction is at JV Budenovskoye [v] (WNN, Aug 2025). KAP "expects to exercise its downflex opportunity within the acceptable 20% deviation" [v].
- **2026 guidance.** Production 27,500-29,000 tU (100%) [u] (Skillings). 2026 sales guidance is 50.7-53.3 Mlb [u].
- **Q1 2026.** All 2026 forecasts were confirmed; production (100% basis) was +9.1% YoY [u] (Freedom Broker).
- **2027.** Guidance: NOT FOUND.
- **Mineral extraction tax (MET).** The rate was 9% for 2025 only. From 1 January 2026 a differentiated MET applies, linked to each subsoil agreement's annual production volume and to the U3O8 price [v] (KAP RNS "Changes to MET rate"). Management guided 2026 C1 cash cost +34% and AISC +21% YoY because of the differentiated MET [u].
- **Sulphuric acid.** The weighted-average acid price rose 43% YoY to KZT 70,000/t [u]. Higher MET plus imported acid makes low-grade deposits less economic [u].

### 2.2 Niger (Orano / SOMAÏR)

- **Nationalisation, June 2025.** Niger's junta nationalised SOMAÏR, taking Orano's 63.4% stake; the mine is operated under Sopamin supervision [u] (Ecofin / Africa.com).
- **ICSID order, Sep 2025.** A tribunal ordered Niger not to sell or transfer the uranium [u].
- **Shipment, late Nov 2025.** Orano said a shipment had left site, with destination unknown. General Tiani asserted sovereign marketing rights [u].
- **Rosatom report.** Le Monde reported an agreement with Rosatom to sell 1,000 t of yellowcake. Rosatom denied earlier reports that it would buy Orano's assets [u].
- **Stalled cargo.**
  - January 2026: a cargo of at least 1,000 t was blocked at Niamey airport for weeks [u] (AFP).
  - February 2026: the FT reported the material stored at Air Base 101, with no buyer found despite talks with Russia, China, the US and the UAE [u].

### 2.3 Russia

## 3. Canadian and Saskatchewan regulatory framework

- **CNSC.** Cameco's mines and mills (McArthur River/Key Lake, Cigar Lake, Rabbit Lake) and its fuel-services facilities (Blind River, Port Hope, Cameco Fuel Manufacturing) operate under CNSC licences [m]. The recent CNSC approvals of NexGen and Denison show a functioning licensing pathway [v/u, above]. Specific Cameco licence renewal dates are NOT FOUND.
- **Saskatchewan royalties.** There are two components, as described in Cameco's AIF [m]:
  - A basic royalty of 5% of gross sales, less the Saskatchewan resource credit (0.75%).
  - A profit royalty of 10% on profit up to a per-kg threshold, and 15% on profit above it. The thresholds are indexed annually.
  - The current thresholds and any 2025-26 regime changes are NOT FOUND (not searched). Verify against the Cameco 2025 AIF / 40-F.
- **Canada-US trade.** See 2.4. Section 232 imposed no tariff as of January 2026 [v]. The 2025 IEEPA 10% energy rate applied only to non-USMCA entries and reportedly ended 24 Feb 2026 [u].
- **Foreign ownership.** Under the *Eldorado Nuclear Limited Reorganization and Divestiture Act*, Cameco's articles limit any single non-resident to 15% of voting shares, any single resident to 25%, and aggregate non-resident votes to 25% of votes cast [m]. The brief's "25%" refers to the aggregate cap. Verify against the current AIF. This is relevant to any change of control or to strategic stakes.
- **Indigenous rights and duty to consult.** The Crown's duty to consult applies to CNSC licensing decisions [m]. Cameco has collaboration agreements with Athabasca Basin communities, such as the 2016 Collaboration Agreement with English River, Hatchet Lake, Black Lake and Fond du Lac [m]. Not re-verified.

### 5.3 The 28 October 2025 US Government partnership (binding term sheet with the US Department of Commerce)

- **Reactors.** The US Government will arrange financing and facilitate permitting and approvals for new Westinghouse reactors with an aggregate value of at least US$80bn [v] (Cameco/Brookfield release, BusinessWire, 28 Oct 2025).
- **Participation interest.**
  - The USG receives a participation interest entitling it to 20% of Westinghouse cash distributions in excess of US$17.5bn [v].
  - It vests only after FID and definitive agreements covering at least US$80bn of new builds [v].
- **IPO clause.** If the interest vests by January 2029 and Westinghouse pursues an IPO valued at US$30bn or more, the USG can require the IPO. Its interest then converts into a warrant for 20% of the equity value in excess of US$17.5bn [v].
- **Status.** The definitive agreements replacing the term sheet were still pending. Cameco's 2026 outlook assumes definitive agreements with the USG are reached [v] (Cameco 6-K FY2026). A signing is NOT FOUND as of 3 Nov 2026.
- **Illustrative economics** [d]: at a US$30bn equity value, the USG share is 20% × (30 - 17.5) = US$2.5bn, about 8% dilution. At US$50bn it is US$6.5bn, about 13%.

That gives about US$1.6-2.3bn of EBITDA per pair, or about US$0.8-1.15bn per reactor [u/d]. This is about double the per-reactor figure above, and the source of the gap is unresolved.
- **AP1000s reaching FID by 2030** [d, analyst judgement, not a sourced figure]:
  - Base case: 6-10 units. That is 2-4 units under the DOE / US$80bn programme (the first Korea-funded pair plus 1-2 LOI projects), 3 in Poland (EPC by YE2026; the FID path supported by EXIM), and 0-2 at V.C. Summer.
  - Bull case: 12-16 units, with all 5 DOE-funded projects proceeding plus Bulgaria.
  - Bear case: 3-5 units.
- **Cameco share in the base case** [d]: 8 units × US$400-600m × 49% = about US$1.6-2.4bn of cumulative Cameco-share EBITDA, spread over roughly 7-10 years of construction. That is about US$0.2-0.3bn a year, plus recurring fuel/services afterwards. This compares with 2026 guidance of about $370-430m for Cameco's share of Westinghouse EBITDA [u].

## 8. Tailwinds and headwinds for Cameco

**Tailwinds**
1. **Term pricing.** The LT price of US$96.50-97/lb is an 18-year high and rose about 8% YTD [v/u]. Cameco's market-related contracts reprice upward, and term has stayed above spot [d].
2. **Structural deficit.**
   - WNA's reference case has demand more than doubling to over 150,000 tU by 2040 [v].
   - Kazatomprom is cutting (-10% nominal in 2026, with downflex) and its costs are rising (MET, acid) [v/u].
   - Niger supply is effectively lost to Western buyers [u].
   - Russian supply is barred from the US after 2027 [v].
3. **Under-contracting.** Utility term contracting of about 116 Mlb/yr is well below the ~179 Mlb/yr reactor requirement [v/d]. This points to pent-up contracting, and RFPs were rising in 2026 [v].
4. **Westinghouse optionality.**
   - The US$80bn USG framework [v], the US$17.5bn DOE conditional loans [v] and the US-Korea framework for 6 AP1000s [v] form the support package.
   - Also: the Japan framework [u], Poland's EPC terms agreed [v] and the S-1 filing as a valuation crystallisation route [v].
5. **Conversion and enrichment tightness.** Record SWU prices [u] and a tight conversion market [u] help Port Hope and GLE. The Paducah licence is on track for early 2027 [u].
6. **Policy.** US executive orders (400 GW by 2050) [m]; hyperscaler PPAs and restarts [v/u]; COP28 tripling [m]; Section 232 so far tariff-free for Canadian uranium [v].

**Headwinds**
1. **Spot weakness and equity sentiment.** Spot is about US$90/lb, down about 5% YTD [u]. CCJ fell about 9% on the Q3 2026 adjusted loss of C$2m [v/u].
2. **Operations.** A longer Key Lake shutdown in Q3 2026 hit uranium-segment EBITDA, down to C$179m from C$220m [v]. McArthur River production issues were reported in 2025 [u, Nasdaq headline].
3. **Kazakhstan / Inkai.** Higher MET from 2026, transport risk, and 2026 deliveries back-end-loaded (1.6 of 4.2 Mlb delivered by Q3) [v]. Delays could cut the Inkai dividend [v].
4. **Westinghouse execution and financing.**
   - Nothing is binding yet: the USG definitive agreements and the DOE loans are conditional, and the Korea framework is non-binding [v].
   - Westinghouse equity of US$500m per project [v] implies possible capital calls on Cameco [d].
   - First-of-a-kind cost history (Vogtle about US$35bn; 2017 Chapter 11) [m].
   - USG dilution of 20% of distributions above US$17.5bn [v].
   - A possible 5-10% Korean stake dilutes owners [u].
   - Westinghouse reported 9M 2026 net losses (Cameco share C$80m) [v].
5. **Policy and trade.**
   - The Section 232 negotiation window has lapsed, and any outcome is NOT FOUND [v/u]. Tariff risk on Canadian supply is not eliminated.
   - Russian waivers end on 1 Jan 2028 [v]. That helps the West, but timing uncertainty affects US utilities' procurement.
6. **GLE funding.** It received only US$28m of the US$2.7bn DOE awards [v], against the US$900m it sought.
7. **Contracting pace.** Utilities kept deferring term purchases (about 116 Mlb in 2025) [v]. Cameco's own LT book (~230 Mlb) [u] is large, but new contracting lags.
