## 3. Sentiment analysis (management tone, -5 to +5)

**Method.** Each quarter is scored on four things: (a) the release headline adjectives; (b) whether guidance was raised, held or cut; (c) how confident management sounded about contracting and price; (d) how much hedging or deflection appeared ("rarely linear", "lumpy", "not willing at current economics"). Calls were scored from summaries, not full transcripts.

|---|---|---|

| Q1 2025 (May 25) | **+1** | "Strong" results and "fundamentals remain positive", but tariff and Section 232 hedging. Guidance only held. |

**Trajectory.** Tone rose from +1/+2 in early 2025 to a peak of +3/+4 in mid/late 2025, driven by the Westinghouse windfall and the US-government partnership. It then fell steadily through 2026 to about -1. The *strategic* message has been almost word-for-word constant for two years: replacement-rate contracting, disciplined supply, record uncovered requirements, and Westinghouse as the growth engine. What changed is the *operational* tone: from "production above guidance" (Feb 2026) to repeated disruptions and two cost raises. Management is now asking investors to look through 2026 ("a 2026 problem", per analyst commentary in TIKR [u]), which is credible only if Key Lake comes back cleanly in Q4 2026 and Q1 2027.

## 4. Management focus now (top 5)
1. **Restoring Saskatchewan operating reliability and cost control.** Finish the Key Lake extended outage and modernization. Recover McArthur River after the 2025 development delays and the May 2026 flood. Keep McClean Lake/Cigar Lake running (Orano-operated mill). Get unit costs back below the C$67-70/lb guidance [v / u].
2. **Contracting discipline: holding out for replacement-rate, escalated, market-related terms.** Only fix the base portion at "three-digit escalated" prices. Keep floors with upside exposure. Hold back supply rather than chase volume [u].
3. **Westinghouse growth and the US-government partnership.** Turn the Oct 2025 binding term sheet into definitive agreements and a government FID on at least US$80bn of AP1000s. Manage lumpy new-build income (e.g. Dukovany). Keep IPO optionality: an IPO can be required if vesting happens by Jan 2029 at a valuation of at least US$30bn [v].
4. **Balance-sheet conservatism and capital allocation.** Zero net debt [u]; credit facility extended to 2030 [brief]; C$0.24 dividend held [u]; capacity for Westinghouse needs and any Cigar Lake interest step-up [u].
5. **Leadership transition and credibility.** CFO succession by Q1 2027, possible CEO-transition planning, and rebuilding guidance credibility after two misses.
