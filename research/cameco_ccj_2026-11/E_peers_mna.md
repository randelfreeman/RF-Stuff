## 0. READ FIRST: coverage limits and tagging conventions

**How the research was done:**
- WebFetch and curl were unavailable. Every sourced figure below comes from **WebSearch result summaries**, not from opened primary documents. Where a summary quoted a primary filing (for example an SEC 6-K/8-K or an ASX release), the figure is still tagged [u] unless it was cross-checked by a second, independent result.
- The **Financial Datasets MCP** returned "not supported" for CCJ and "balance $0.00" for every other ticker, so there was **no market-data feed**.
- The search budget was **28 queries**. **29 were run**, one over the cap because of a miscount, and 4 of them were in "extended" mode. Section 9 lists the queries.

**What this means for the numbers:**
- Market data come from **different dates, between February and October 2026**. Every cell carries its date, and the table is **not a same-day snapshot**.
- Third-party aggregator pages (stockanalysis, MarketScreener, roic.ai, Value Research and similar) often disagree with each other. Where they conflict, both values are shown.
- **Cameco consensus multiples:** two MarketScreener pages disagree widely (2027E P/E 44.5x vs 63.2x).
- **Before publication, re-pull the whole valuation table on one date from Bloomberg or FactSet.**

**Tags:**
| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[v]** | Verified: primary document, or cross-checked by two independent results |
| **[u]** | Single secondary source (search summary of an aggregator, broker or news page) |
| **[d]** | Derived by the analyst from figures shown; formula given |
| **[m]** | Analyst memory, not verified in this session. Kept to a minimum, mainly for structural ownership facts. **Verify before publication.** |
| **NOT FOUND** | Not retrieved in this session. No number has been substituted. |

**FX used in derivations:** AUD/USD 0.65, USD/CAD 1.38 and USD/HKD 7.78. These are **[m] approximations**; replace them with spot rates on the pricing date.

### 2.3 Read-across for Cameco

**Cameco's premium to Kazatomprom:**
- On P/NAV, Cameco trades at **2.3x** against **1.0x** for Kazatomprom (S&P, Feb 2026) [u].
- On multiples, Cameco trades at **2027E EV/EBITDA of c.23–29x** against **c.7–9x trailing** for Kazatomprom (2025A) [u].
- The premium reflects:
  - jurisdiction (Kazakh sovereign and tax risk);
  - Cameco's tier-one Athabasca assets;
  - the Westinghouse reactor/services option.

**Westinghouse is now the swing factor:**
- Cameco's share of Westinghouse Q2-26 adjusted EBITDA was **US$163m vs US$352m in Q2-25** [u, BNN/Northern Miner via search].
- Westinghouse posted a net loss of US$10m in Q2-26 [u].
- Q3-26 earnings also fell on lower Westinghouse income and higher costs (StreetInsider) [u].
- Desjardins values Cameco's 49% at **C$15.1bn (US$10.8bn)** [u]. That implies c.**US$22bn for 100% of Westinghouse equity** [d], against **c.US$4.5bn of equity contributed in 2023** [u] (c.4.9x [d]).

**Nuclear technology and services names trade at higher multiples than uranium miners:**
- Centrus: c.41x EV/EBITDA, c.97x trailing P/E.
- BWXT: c.37x 2026E P/E.
- GE Vernova: c.US$236bn market cap.
- This supports a sum-of-the-parts that values Westinghouse on technology/services multiples rather than at uranium-miner multiples.

## 6. Takeover considerations: could anyone buy Cameco?

### 6.1 Legal and regulatory constraints

**1. The Eldorado Nuclear Limited Reorganization and Divestiture Act (ENL Act), as amended by Bill C-3 (2001):**
- **Non-resident, individually:** voting shares capped at **15%** (raised from 5%).
- **Non-residents, collectively:** voting rights capped at **25%** (raised from 20%).
- **Canadian resident, individually:** capped at **25%**.
- The limits must be in Cameco's articles.
- A **majority of directors must be Canadian residents**.
- Parliament's stated intent in 2001 was that the 15% cap "rules out any possibility of takeover by a major multinational" and that control "cannot fall into foreign hands".
- Source: House of Commons Hansard, 25 Apr 2001, via openparliament.ca and noscommunes.ca [v as to the 2001 amendment]. Current consolidated text not checked; confirm on Justice Laws and in Cameco's articles.
- Head office must remain in Saskatchewan: [m], verify in the ENL Act.

**2. Consequences of the ENL Act:**
- **No single shareholder, Canadian or foreign, can own more than 25% of voting shares.** Even a Canadian bidder cannot reach control without an Act of Parliament.
- A foreign bidder (Kazatomprom, BHP, Glencore, a Chinese or Gulf sovereign, Brookfield's non-Canadian vehicles) is capped at 15%.

**3. Investment Canada Act:**
- National-security review applies.
- Canada's 2022 policy on critical minerals states that SOE acquisitions in the sector will be approved only on an "exceptional basis" [m]. Uranium is on Canada's critical-minerals list [m].
- In Nov 2022 Ottawa ordered Chinese divestments from three lithium juniors [m].
- Verify all of these points.

**4. Non-Resident Ownership Policy in the Uranium Mining Sector (1987):**
- Non-residents are limited to 49% of a uranium mine at the first production stage, unless the mine is Canadian-controlled [m].
- This is relevant to any foreign buyer of Cameco's Saskatchewan assets.

**5. CNSC licensing:**
- Change of control of licensed facilities (mines, Port Hope, Blind River) requires Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission approval [m].

**6. Saskatchewan politics:**
- Cameco is the province's flagship resource company (former Crown corporation, SMDC/Eldorado merger 1988 [m]).
- Royalty and political sensitivities, plus northern and Indigenous impact-benefit agreements, make any foreign takeover politically fraught.

- buying more of Westinghouse, or alternatively monetising part of it via the IPO;
- consolidating the Athabasca Basin (NexGen at c.C$11.2bn, Denison at c.C$4.7bn, Paladin's PLS);
- raising its stake in GLE (option to 75%) [u];
- adding to Cigar Lake / McArthur River JV interests from minority partners.

### 6.3 Illustrative takeout multiples (hypothetical; only relevant if the ENL Act were amended)

**Approach** (all [d], built on the [u] inputs above):
1. **P/NAV:** Cameco trades at 2.3x NAV (US$25.5bn NAV, Feb 2026) [u]. Uranium developers trade at 1.3–1.6x, and the largest producer (KAP) at 1.0x. A strategic control price would typically need a **25–40% premium to undisturbed price**; this is a general market convention [m], not a nuclear-specific source. That implies **c.2.9–3.2x NAV**, or **c.US$42–47bn equity** [d: US$33.5bn × 1.25–1.40]. That is well above any precedent uranium deal; the largest recent one was Paladin–Fission at c.US$0.8bn.
2. **EV/EBITDA:** The premium applied to 2027E consensus of 23–29x [u] implies **c.29–40x 2027E EBITDA** [d]. That compares with precedent nuclear-services deals at **11–14x** (Westinghouse 2022–23, Kinectrics 2025), so a strategic buyer would be paying mainly for long-dated pounds and for the Westinghouse option.
3. **Sum-of-the-parts cross-check:** Desjardins' C$15.1bn (US$10.8bn) for 49% of Westinghouse [u] is c.32% of Cameco's c.US$33.5bn market cap [d]. The uranium and fuel-services business is therefore implicitly valued at c.US$22.7bn [d] before net debt, which is small (c.C$0.14bn).

## 7. Key takeaways for the report

1. **Cameco's premium:** Cameco is the premium-rated Western uranium name at **2.3x P/NAV** [u], against Kazatomprom at 1.0x and North American developers and ISR names at 1.3–1.6x (S&P Global MI, Feb 2026). On 2027E consensus, Cameco trades at **c.23–29x EV/EBITDA** and **c.45–63x P/E** [u, conflicting MarketScreener pages], roughly half its 5-year average forward multiples (c.60x EV/EBITDA, c.83x P/E) [u].
2. **Westinghouse dominates both risk and opportunity.** The draft S-1 was announced on 31 Jul 2026 [v]. The US government holds a 20% participation interest above US$17.5bn of distributions [u]. Desjardins values Cameco's 49% at **US$10.8bn**, against US$2.2bn invested [d]. Q2-26 Westinghouse EBITDA to Cameco **fell 54% y/y** to US$163m [u], and Q3-26 earnings missed on lower Westinghouse income [u].
3. **Precedent nuclear-services M&A** prices at **c.11–14x EBITDA** (Westinghouse, Kinectrics). Uranium asset deals range from **c.US$1/lb** (historic US resource) to **c.US$9/lb** (Athabasca reserve). There is **no large uranium-producer takeover in 2025–26**. Chinese SOEs have been the active buyers of Kazakh JV stakes from Rosatom (Dec 2024, prices undisclosed).
4. **Cameco cannot be taken over** without Parliament amending the ENL Act (15% individual non-resident cap, 25% aggregate non-resident cap, 25% individual resident cap) [v, 2001 Hansard]. No takeover premium should be built into the valuation.
