## Q1. What are the current reporting segments, what does each do, and how did each contribute to revenue and profit in FY2025 and 1H FY2026?

### Takeaway
Tokyo Tatemono reports four segments: Building (ビル事業, called "Commercial Properties" in English summaries), Residential (住宅事業), Asset Service (アセットサービス事業) and Other (その他). I found no change to the segments in 2023–2026. The material change came with the 2025–2027 medium-term plan: the headline profit measure, "business profit" (事業利益), now also includes gains on sales of fixed assets.

In FY2025 Building provided about 46% of revenue and about 62% of segment operating profit, Residential about 35% and 24%, Asset Service about 13% and 11%, and Other about 5% and 4%. Group operating profit rose 20.2% to ¥95.8bn, driven by Building: higher rents and more profitable sales of buildings to investors. Residential fell because fewer condos were handed over. In 1H FY2026 this was more extreme. Building operating profit (¥40.1bn) was larger than the whole group's operating profit (¥39.9bn), while Residential operating profit fell 83%.

### Inferences
- **FY2025 shares (my calculations from the figures above).** Share of group revenue: Building 46.4%, Residential 34.8%, Asset Service 13.4%, Other about 5.4%. Share of the four segments' combined operating profit (¥108.3bn before adjustments): Building 61.9%, Residential 23.6%, Asset Service 10.6%, Other 3.9%. Corporate costs and eliminations come to about −¥12.5bn (¥95.8bn − ¥108.3bn).
- **Other revenue FY2025 ≈ ¥25.8bn (estimate):** operating profit ¥4,172m divided by the 16.2% margin. The range is ¥25.7–25.8bn depending on rounding. The four segments then sum to ¥474.5bn, matching group revenue of ¥474.6bn.
- **Profit mix has swung sharply toward Building.** Building operating profit rose about ¥25.7bn in FY2025 while Residential fell about ¥12.6bn. Implied FY2024 operating profit was about ¥41.4bn for Building and ¥38.2bn for Residential (back-calculated from the YoY rates). In 1H FY2026 Building was about 100.7% of group operating profit and Residential about 7.5%.
- 1H FY2026 revenue split (approximate, before eliminations): Asset Service about 13.8%, Other about 5.6%, Building plus Residential together about 80.7% (about ¥156.8bn).
- **Earnings are weighted to the second half.** 1H operating profit is only 37.8% of the revised full-year guidance (¥39.9bn of ¥105.5bn). The remaining 62% depends on 2H property sales and condo handovers.
- The extract's "+¥21.0bn YoY" for 1H Asset Service revenue would mean 1H FY2025 revenue of only about ¥5.8bn. That is implausible against FY2025 full-year Asset Service revenue of ¥63.5bn. The true figure is more likely about +¥2.1bn. Treat it as unverified.
- Net income fell 10.6% in FY2025 even though operating profit rose 20.2%. This suggests FY2024 had larger one-off gains (for example, on fixed-asset sales). That would fit the decision to count fixed-asset sale gains in business profit from 2025. This is an inference; I did not verify FY2024 extraordinary items.

## Q2. What are the key products, brands, flagship assets and group companies today, and at what scale?

### Takeaway
The core is a Tokyo Station-area office cluster (Otemachi, Yaesu, Nihonbashi, Kyobashi), most recently TOFROM YAESU TOWER, completed 28 Feb 2026, plus the next large project, Kyobashi 3-chome East (about 166,800 m², due FY2029). The condo brand is Brillia, with a landbank of about 7,300 units.

Around this sit:
- **Logistics:** T-LOGI warehouses.
- **Data centre:** a new 25MW centre in Osaka.
- **Parking:** Nippon Parking (日本パーキング, "NPC24H"), with about 1,905 sites and 86,792 spaces at Dec 2024.
- **Leisure:** Tokyo Tatemono Resort, with hotels, 13 golf facilities and 11 hot-spring baths.
- **Asset management:** TRIM, manager of the listed J-REIT Japan Prime Realty Investment Corp (JPR), and Tokyo Tatemono Investment Advisors, manager of the private REIT.
- **Brokerage and building management:** Tokyo Tatemono Real Estate Sales and Tokyo Fudosan Kanri.
- **Overseas:** small but growing, in the USA, Thailand, Australia, Indonesia and China.

## Q3. How does each division make money, and when is revenue recognised?

### Takeaway
There are four main profit engines:
1. Rent from offices, retail and logistics (Building).
2. Profits from selling completed or stabilised buildings to investors such as JPR, the private REIT and funds. This mainly sits in Building, with some in Asset Service.
3. Margins on condo sales, booked when units are handed over (Residential).
4. Fee and operating income: brokerage, appraisal and consulting; parking; leisure; and asset management fees.

Condo profit is very lumpy and concentrated in Q1. Property-sale profit decides each year's growth. FY2026 depends heavily on 2H property sales.

### Cited Findings
- **Building:** FY2025 growth came from rising rental income and "higher margins on sales of properties to investors". — [FY2025 決算ハイライト](https://pdf.irpocket.com/C8804/YpwX/GtIj/pQV9.pdf). Nikkei pointed to larger gains on property sales and higher office rents. — [Nikkei, Nov 2025](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUB132440T11C25A1000000/). In 1H FY2026 Building operating profit rose 122.7% on more property sales. — [FilingReader](https://filingreader.com/news-wire/tokyo/2026-08-20/tokyo-tatemono-profit-rises-on-commercial-property-sales). The FY2026 plan relies on more sales to investors. — [Nikkei](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUB126EM0S6A210C2000000/)
- **Residential:** condo sales plus leasing and management. — [Yuho FY2024](https://pdf.irpocket.com/C8804/wOrc/gZL7/oYqG.pdf). Q1 FY2026 net income fell 60% because condo handovers dropped to 235 units from 772 in Q1 FY2025. — [Nikkei, May 2026](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUB136Z90T10C26A5000000/). 1H FY2026: 270 units, average ¥69.11m, gross margin 27.8% (prior year 969 units, 30.4%). — [1H 決算ハイライト (search extract)](https://pdf.irpocket.com/C8804/xoA3/ieAo/gkiS/BBY6.pdf)
- **Asset Service:** income from brokerage, appraisal and CRE consulting, plus property sales. The 1H FY2026 increase came from more property sales. — [Gyokai Digest](https://www.gyokaidigest.com/companies/tokyo-tatemono/report/2025-FY); [Nikkei](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUB126EM0S6A210C2000000/)
- **Parking (NPC):** hourly, monthly and reservation car-park income. — [NPC recruit](https://recruit.npc-npc.co.jp/company/business.html)
- **Leisure (Tokyo Tatemono Resort):** income from hotels, golf (13 facilities) and hot-spring baths (11 facilities). — [Tokyo Tatemono Resort](https://www.tt-resort.co.jp/about.html)
- **Asset management:** TRIM's agreement with JPR covers buying and selling real estate, all leasing decisions, fund-raising and reporting to JPR. — [JPR: asset manager overview](https://jpr-reit.co.jp/en/about/asset.html)
- **KPI treatment:** since 2025, business profit also counts gains and losses on sales of fixed assets. Sales of properties classed as fixed assets therefore now count toward the headline profit measure. — [R.E.port](https://www.re-port.net/article/news/0000077759/); [Altvega](https://altvega.com/tatemono-bp-20250116/)

### Inferences
- **When revenue is booked (plain language):**
  - Condo revenue appears to be booked when the buyer receives the keys, not when the contract is signed. Quarterly profit tracks the number of units handed over (235 vs 772 in Q1).
  - Deliveries are heavily weighted to Q1 (January–March), when buyers move before the April start of Japan's school and work year. In FY2025, 772 of the 969 units handed over in 1H (about 80%) came in Q1.
  - Rent is booked monthly as earned.
  - Property-sale revenue is booked at closing, so it arrives in lumps. FY2026 is weighted to the second half (1H is about 38% of operating-profit guidance).
- Sales of buildings held for sale seem to run through revenue and operating profit, not extraordinary gains. Evidence: Building operating profit rose 62% on revenue up 24.7%, consistent with high-margin sales in revenue. Sales of fixed assets go to extraordinary gains but now count in business profit.
- The 1H FY2026 condo average of ¥69.11m is well below the Tokyo 23-ward market average of about ¥136.13m reported by the Real Estate Economic Institute ([nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/ja/japan-data/h02685/); period not verified). This suggests a large share of suburban or non-23-ward and family-type projects. This is an inference only.

### Gaps

  - The accounting-policy wording on revenue recognition (Yuho note on 重要な会計方針)

  - Margins on investor sales
  - FY2025 full-year condo units and average price
  - Fee levels for TRIM and the private REIT
  - NPC and leisure revenue and profit
  - Q4 vs Q1 seasonality beyond the Q1/1H unit counts above

### Inferences
- **Value-chain loop (inferred):**
  1. Acquire or assemble land (redevelopment rights, building purchases).
  2. Outside general contractors (Taisei, Obayashi, Kajima) build.
  3. Lease up (Tokyo Fudosan Kanri manages; Nippon Parking runs the car parks).
  4. Either hold for rent, or sell stabilised assets to JPR, the private REIT or third-party investors (managed by TRIM or Tokyo Tatemono Investment Advisors, earning fees).
  5. Recycle the proceeds into new projects.
- Brillia condos follow a shorter path: land, contractor, buyer at handover, then Tokyo Tatemono Real Estate Sales on resale. The condo management link is inferred, because the condo management company was not verified.
- The biggest single counterparty risks visible are:
  - concentration of construction among a few large general contractors, where cost inflation and labour shortages hit project costs (inferred);
  - reliance on investor demand, including JPR's capacity to raise capital, for the property-sale profits behind most FY2025–FY2026 growth (inferred from the Building segment's results).
