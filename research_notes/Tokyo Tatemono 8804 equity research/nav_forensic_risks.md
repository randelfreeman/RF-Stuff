## 1. NAV: rental-property (賃貸等不動産) unrealised gains FY2021–FY2025, NAV per share, P/NAV vs peers, and company NAV-type disclosures

### Takeaway
At 31 Dec 2025 the Yuho-basis rental property had a book value of ¥1,058.1bn and a fair value of ¥1,658.0bn, an unrealised gain of ¥599.9bn. At a 30.6% tax rate the after-tax gain is ¥416.4bn, about ¥2,007 per share. That gives a **NAV of about ¥4,853 per share at FY2025 year-end, or about ¥4,937 when rolled forward to the 1H FY2026 BPS of ¥2,930**. At ¥3,211 (2 Oct 2026) the stock trades at **about 0.65–0.66x NAV and 1.10x book**. That is level with Mitsubishi Estate and Mitsui Fudosan (about 0.64x) and above Sumitomo Realty (about 0.47–0.51x), based on secondary-source estimates. Unrealised gains were nearly flat over FY2021–FY2023 (¥514.6bn to ¥529.4bn) and then rose to ¥599.9bn by FY2025. The FY2024 gain and the FY2021–FY2024 book-value/fair-value split were not retrieved.

- **Growth in the gain is slow.** From FY2021 to FY2025 the gain rose 16.6%, a 3.9% CAGR. It was nearly flat over 2021–23 while assets were being sold, which realises gains and so removes them from the stock. The bear reading is that unrealised gains are being harvested into P&L roughly as fast as development replenishes them, so the stock of gains is not compounding much.
- **What the price implies about asset values.** At ¥3,211 × 207.5m ≈ ¥666bn of market cap against ¥608bn of book equity (2,930 × 207.5m), the market credits only ¥58bn after tax (≈ ¥84bn pre-tax) of the ¥600bn gain. That implies a portfolio fair value of about ¥1,142bn against the reported ¥1,658bn, a **31% haircut**. This equals cap-rate expansion of about +136bp, +158bp or +181bp from assumed starting cap rates of 3.0%, 3.5% or 4.0%. Those starting cap rates are assumptions; Tokyo Tatemono's portfolio cap rate was not retrieved. The haircut would be even larger if the embedded gains on inventory were counted.
- **Relative P/NAV gives no "quality discount".** Tokyo Tatemono's ~0.65x is level with Mitsubishi Estate and Mitsui (0.64x) even though it is about 1/7–1/9 their size by unrealised gain (¥0.6tn vs ¥4.0–5.6tn) and carries a thin 26% equity ratio. A short-seller would argue it deserves a wider discount than the two majors; Sumitomo Realty, at about 0.5x, is the obvious comparable for a leveraged name.
- **Earnings are high relative to NAV.**
  - Tokyo Tatemono's forward earnings yield on NAV is **6.3%** (EPS ¥313.3 / NAV ¥4,937).
  - The peers' implied figure (P/NAV ÷ P/E) is 2.7% for Mitsubishi Estate, 3.6% for Mitsui and 2.8% for Sumitomo.
  - Tokyo Tatemono therefore earns about twice as much per yen of NAV. Either it is genuinely more efficient, or (bear) its P&L converts NAV into reported profit through asset sales faster than peers do. That is consistent with Q2.
- **Why the stock fell less in the 2026 sell-off.** It dropped 26.6% from its high versus 34–42% for the majors. This is consistent with its P/NAV starting lower (about 0.89–0.90x at the February high) and its P/E being much lower (about 10x vs 18–24x).

  - whether Tokyo Tatemono carries a 土地再評価差額金 (land revaluation reserve) already inside equity, which would double-count if added again;

## 2. Revenue recognition and earnings quality: condos, sales to investors and sponsored vehicles, SPC/TMK/equity-method, business profit

### Takeaway
Profit growth relies more and more on property sales to investors and on asset recycling rather than recurring rent. Examples:
- FY2025 revenue rose 2.3% but operating profit rose 20.2%.
- FY2026 net-income growth is explicitly driven by investor sales.
- The company plans hotel-focused sales at "among the largest" levels in its history.
- It holds a ">¥100bn stock of gains".

The headline "business profit" (事業利益) KPI exceeds operating profit by the amount of equity-method income (SPC/JV), so reported growth can be shaped by deal timing and by which vehicle books the gain. Quarterly revenue is very lumpy.

### Inferences
- **Business profit includes equity-method income (inferred).**
  - FY2025 business profit (¥102bn) minus operating profit (¥95.8bn) is about **¥6.2bn**, or about 6% of business profit. This is consistent with business profit = operating profit + equity-method income. The definition itself was not verified in this session.
  - The headline KPI therefore includes profits from SPC/JV vehicles that do not pass through consolidated revenue and whose debt is off the consolidated balance sheet.
- **A forensic flag inside the "upward revision".**
  - Implied equity-method income in FY2026 guidance moved from **+¥2.0bn (original: 102.0 − 100.0) to −¥0.5bn (revised: 105.0 − 105.5)**, a ¥2.5bn swing to a loss, while operating profit rose ¥5.5bn.
  - Possible explanations: (a) expected losses at an equity-method affiliate (overseas JV?), or (b) a deal moved from an equity-method vehicle into consolidation, where it is booked in operating profit.
  - Either way, the composition of profit changed, not just its level. Check the 1H FY2026 equity-method line and the deck commentary.
- **FY2024 net income was flattered by extraordinary gains (estimate).** Net income of ¥65.9bn grossed up at 30.6% is about ¥94.9bn pre-tax, against ordinary profit of ¥71.7bn. That implies roughly **¥23bn of net extraordinary gains, about 24% of pre-tax profit** (about ¥25bn at a 32% tax rate), consistent with the reported cross-shareholding sales. FY2025's −10.6% NI is the normalisation.
- **FY2026 net income guidance also leans on extraordinary gains (estimate).** ¥65.0bn / 0.694 ≈ ¥93.7bn pre-tax against ¥83.5bn ordinary, so about **¥10–12bn of extraordinary gains (about 11–13% of pre-tax)** seems to be embedded, assuming no unusual tax or minority-interest effects.
- **Margin expansion with flat revenue = mix shift to sales gains.** Operating margin went from 17.2% (FY2024) to 20.2% (FY2025) to 20.1% (FY2026E). In 1H FY2026, revenue fell 6.9% while NI rose 13.7%. Higher-margin investor sales and fixed-asset recycling, not rent growth alone, are the most plausible driver; this is not verified because the gains-on-sale breakdown was not retrieved.
- **Round-tripping and conflict risk (structural, not proven).**
  - The plan explicitly pairs asset sales with growing AUM through sales to REITs and other managers. The sponsored vehicles named in the brief are Japan Prime Realty, Tokyo Tatemono's private REIT and funds.
  - Tokyo Tatemono can therefore book a sale gain *and* earn ongoing asset-management, property-management or leasing fees, and may keep exposure through unit holdings, master leases or sponsor support.
  - Sale prices to affiliates are usually anchored to appraisals the seller influences.
  - Red flags to test: the share of sales going to related or sponsored vehicles; prices versus appraisal; whether Tokyo Tatemono keeps master-lease/rent-guarantee or buy-back obligations; and whether it co-invests (TK/preferred stakes) in the buyer vehicle.
- **Condo revenue is lumpy and seasonal.**
  - Condo revenue is recognised on delivery (point-in-time under the J-GAAP revenue standard). This is standard J-GAAP practice and an analyst's note; Tokyo Tatemono's own policy wording was not retrieved.
  - Deliveries cluster in particular quarters (often Q1 and Q4 for Japanese developers), so quarterly revenue (1Q FY2026 −22%) says little. The real risk is a full-year shortfall if large handovers slip.

## 6. Bear-case analytics: rate and cap-rate sensitivity, condo/office/cost shocks, competitor, capital allocation, embedded assumptions, a 20–30% shortfall, permanent-impairment scenario

### Takeaway
The price (¥3,211; 0.65x NAV, 1.10x book, 10.2x FY2026E P/E) already implies:
- a **31% haircut to the company's fair values**, about +140–180bp of cap-rate expansion; and
- an ROE of about 10% that the market treats as a ceiling, not a growth path.

A short case therefore cannot rest on "NAV is overstated" alone. It must argue that the **earnings engine (sales gains plus cheap debt) breaks**:

- **When the cushion runs out.** Aggregate fair value would need to fall **36.2%** before it reaches book value and the cushion is exhausted. That equals about +170bp, +198bp or +227bp from 3.0%, 3.5% or 4.0%. Below that, impairments would hit book equity, and individual assets could be impaired well before then.
- **Equity sensitivity.** Because the equity ratio is 26%, every ¥100bn of impairment is about 17% of book equity, about ¥482/share (after-tax effects aside).

**(c) Condo price and volume.** Illustrative only, because Tokyo Tatemono's condo revenue and margins were not retrieved.

- Under lower-of-cost-or-net-realisable-value rules, a fall in expected selling price below book triggers immediate write-downs.

**(d) Office vacancy.** Each +1pt of portfolio vacancy costs about 1% of office rent with roughly 100% flow-through to profit, plus rent-free periods on re-leasing. Not quantified because the office rent base and Tokyo supply data were not retrieved.

**(e) Construction-cost inflation.** This squeezes development margins on land already bought. Contractors' 2026 share-price weakness (Nikkei) points to stress across the construction chain.

**(f) What the price embeds.**
- **On P/B:** P/B 1.10x with FY2026E ROE of 10.7% (¥65bn / ¥608bn equity, estimate). Using P/B = (ROE − g)/(r − g):
  - at a 9% cost of equity, implied g is about **−8.6%** (the market expects ROE to fade);
  - at 10%, g is about +2.8%;
  - with zero growth, the implied sustainable ROE is about 9.9% at r = 9%.
  - In short, the market is treating the ~10% ROE target as roughly the peak.
- **On NAV:** a 31% haircut to fair value, i.e. cap rates about +140–180bp, or a conglomerate/leverage discount.
- **On P/E:** 10.2x forward (11.3x trailing on estimated FY2025 EPS of about ¥284) versus peers at 18–24x. The market is capitalising Tokyo Tatemono's earnings as lower quality, which fits their reliance on gains on sale.

**(g) A 20–30% profit-growth disappointment.**

- **If it means the profit level** (FY2026–27 NI 20–30% below ¥65bn guidance): a miss of that size is ¥13–20bn after tax, or about ¥19–28bn pre-tax.   - EPS falls to ¥219–251;
  - at a constant 10.2x the price is **¥2,250–2,570 (−20% to −30%)**; at 8x, ¥1,750–2,000 (−38% to −45%);

**(i) Worst capital allocation (evidence-limited judgement).** The new **London office refurbishment (125 Shaftesbury Ave, launched Dec 2025)** takes a leveraged Japanese developer into a weak, foreign office market where it has little edge. It is a candidate for a future equity-method loss or impairment. **(j) Misaligned incentives.** Three features reward booking gains and growing AUM over long-term NAV per share:
- BBT stock compensation (KPIs unknown);
- a 40% payout target;
- a strategy of "growing AUM through sales to REITs" (sponsor conflicts with Japan Prime Realty and the private REIT).

**(k) The single scenario that would permanently impair the business: rate-driven closure of the exit channel.**
1. JGB yields keep rising, so J-REIT and private-fund buyers step back and prime cap rates widen by 100bp or more.
2. Tokyo Tatemono's sale-gain engine (the ">¥100bn stock"; the FY2026 hotel programme) stalls just as about ¥1.3–1.4tn of debt reprices and redevelopment capex peaks.
3. Higher mortgage rates also cool Greater-Tokyo condo demand.

5. Management then either sells core assets at cyclical lows or raises equity at about 0.5x NAV.

The **permanent** loss is the dilution or forced sale at the bottom, not the cyclical earnings dip.
