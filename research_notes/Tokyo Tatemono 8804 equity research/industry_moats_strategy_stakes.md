## 1. Industry structure: who the majors are and where Tokyo Tatemono ranks (revenue, OP, office, condos, logistics); is it an oligopoly?

### Takeaway
Tokyo Tatemono is a second-tier comprehensive developer. Its FY2025 operating profit was ¥95.8bn, about a quarter of Mitsui Fudosan's and a third of Sumitomo Realty's, and roughly 57–69% of Tokyu Fudosan HD's and Nomura RE HD's. In condominiums it is a mid-sized supplier: 1,054 units nationally in 2025, ranked #19 by the Real Estate Economic Institute (REEI). The condo market is fragmented, not an oligopoly. Prime central-Tokyo office redevelopment is far more concentrated.

  - Ordinary profit ¥78.2bn (+9%).

### Inferences
- **Relative size.** TT's FY2025 OP of ¥95.8bn is about 24% of Mitsui's, 29% of Mitsubishi Estate's, 32% of Sumitomo's, 57% of Tokyu Fudosan's and 69% of Nomura's. Fiscal years differ by three months.
  - By OP, TT is roughly the #6–7 listed comprehensive developer.
  - Hulic, which also has a December year-end, is probably larger by OP, but this was not verified.
  - Mori Building and Mori Trust are unlisted; their figures were not retrieved.
- **Condo market share.** TT's ~1,054 units against 21,962 Greater Tokyo launches is at most about 5% of that market, assuming most of TT's supply is in Greater Tokyo.
  - The leader nationally is Open House, a non-traditional player.
  - Nomura, the Greater Tokyo leader, has only about 10% of the market.
  - So condos are a fragmented, competitive market, not an oligopoly.
- **Prime office.** Large-scale office redevelopment around Tokyo's central business districts is effectively a territorial oligopoly of a handful of players (analyst judgement). Each has a home district: Mitsubishi Estate in Marunouchi, Mitsui in Nihonbashi, Mori in Toranomon/Roppongi, Tokyu in Shibuya, Sumitomo in Shinjuku/Roppongi, and TT in Yaesu–Kyobashi.
  - The barrier is control of land and redevelopment rights, not scale alone (see section 3).
- **TT's records are partly cyclical.** All five majors set records in the same period, so TT's growth reflects an industry-wide upcycle as well as company-specific execution.

## 2. Product strength and branding: Brillia against Park Homes, The Parkhouse, Proud, City House and Branz; office quality

### Takeaway
On resale value, Brillia is near the top: Mansion Review's 2026 Tokyo rating shows 93.1% average price appreciation, just behind The Parkhouse at 95.6% and ahead of Proud and Park Homes. On popularity and awareness surveys it ranks below the Mitsubishi, Mitsui and Nomura brands, which fits its smaller supply volume. No primary brand survey from Nikkei, SUUMO or Sumai Surfin was retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- **Mansion Review "Tokyo brand-condominium rating 2026".** Average price appreciation (高騰率) was The Parkhouse (Mitsubishi Jisho Residence) 95.6%, Brillia 93.1%, Proud (Nomura) 88.4% and Park Homes (Mitsui) 81.1% ([t23m-navi / Mansion Review 2026](https://t23m-navi.jp/magazine/news/mansionreview-brandmansion2026/); [2025 edition](https://t23m-navi.jp/magazine/news/mansionreview-brandmansion2025/)). The methodology (resale versus original price, sample) was not verified.
- **Aggregator "popular brand" rankings (2025–26).** The Parkhouse #1, Park Homes #3, Proud #5, Brillia #7 ([Mynavi](https://news.mynavi.jp/real-estate-assessment/29103); [Fukuoka Leap Up](https://fukuoka-leapup.jp/column/buying_and_selling/mansion_brand_top10/)).
  - These are aggregator lists, not a primary survey, so reliability is low.
  - The same sources describe Brillia's selling points as refined design and three-stage security (三段階セキュリティ).
- **"Brands people most want to live in, 2025".** Proud was #1 ([bunnmann.com](https://bunnmann.com/ranking-of-apartment-brands-2025/)). Brillia's position was not captured.
- **Scale behind the Proud brand.** Nomura supplied 2,273 Greater Tokyo units in 2025, against TT's 1,054 nationwide ([Nikkei/REEI](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZRSP704888_V20C26A3000000/)).

### Inferences
- Brillia's brand strength shows up mainly as resale and asset value; its 93.1% appreciation is the second best of the four brands rated, which supports pricing power on well-located Tokyo sites.
  - It is weaker on mass awareness, where Mitsubishi, Mitsui and Nomura dominate through larger annual volumes and advertising scale.
- In today's market, average 23-ward prices are rising 9–22% a year (section 4). Location and land drive outcomes more than brand, so the brand is a moderate moat contributor rather than a decisive one.

### Gaps
- Primary brand surveys were not retrieved: Nikkei Research "マンションブランド調査", Sumai Surfin (住まいサーフィン) brand/asset-value rankings, Oricon customer satisfaction for new condominiums, and SUUMO awareness. No comparison data was found for City House (Sumitomo) or Branz (Tokyu).
- No office-quality evidence was found for TT buildings (CASBEE/DBJ Green Building certifications, tenant-satisfaction surveys).
- Brillia's marketing approach (after-sales service, long-term repair programmes, community services) was not researched.
- Unverified background: the Brillia brand dates from the early 2000s, and flagship projects include Brillia Tower Ikebukuro, which is integrated with the Toshima Ward office [verify].

## 3. Moat assessment: evidence by category

### Takeaway
TT has a **narrow, location-specific moat**. It rests on hard assets and redevelopment rights around Tokyo Station's Yaesu side (TOFROM YAESU), backed by the statutory and time barriers of Type-1 urban redevelopment. The supporting factors are a solid Brillia brand and a captive J-REIT exit channel (JPR). Weaknesses are a sales-gain-dependent earnings mix, weak bargaining power over contractors in a cost-inflation cycle, and exposure to rates and housing policy.

### Cited Findings
- **Hard assets and redevelopment rights: TOFROM YAESU** (Yaesu 1-chome East district, 東京駅前八重洲一丁目東地区).
  - Names: A block "TOFROM YAESU THE FRONT", B block "TOFROM YAESU TOWER".
  - B district: site about 10,600 m², gross floor area about 225,000 m², 51 floors above ground and 4 below, about 250 m tall.
  - Uses: offices, retail, bus terminal, conference facilities, medical facilities, parking.
  - Total project cost about ¥210.4bn. Specified agents (特定業務代行者): TT and Obayashi.
  - Scheduled completion: 31 Jul 2025.
  - Source: [Kenbiya, 23 Mar 2025](https://www.kenbiya.com/ar/ns/region/tokyo/8849.html); see also [Kenbiya, project launch](https://www.kenbiya.com/ar/ns/region/tokyo/5592.html).
- **Statutory process.** The Tokyo Metropolitan Government approved the rights conversion (権利変換) for the Yaesu 1-chome redevelopment in 2020 ([Nikkei, 2020](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXMZO60992230Q0A630C2L83000/)). The project is described as a National Strategic Special Zone (国家戦略特区) project ([photo blog, low reliability](http://building-pc.cocolog-nifty.com/helicopter/2024/09/post-c408df.html)).
- **Anchor uses in the B district.** Nippon Medical School will open a medical facility ([Obayashi, 14 Feb 2022](https://www.obayashi.co.jp/news/detail/news20220214_3.html)). The theatre and conference facility will be run by Pia and Congres ([PR Times](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000230.000052843.html)). The Yaesu side of Tokyo Station is described as three redevelopment districts forming a new interchange and transport hub ([LIFULL HOME'S](https://www.homes.co.jp/cont/press/buy/buy_01851/)).
- **Pricing power over tenants (market level).** Miki Shoji, central 5 wards, Aug 2026: vacancy 1.87% and average asking rent ¥23,647/tsubo, +12.46% YoY ([Miki Shoji](https://www.e-miki.com/rent/tokyo.html)).
- **Weak bargaining power over contractors.**
  - The MLIT construction cost deflator (2015 = 100) was 135.4 in Mar 2026, +5.1% YoY ([genba-media](https://www.genba-media.jp/articles/fkj7w5k9w0rv); [aippearnet](https://aippearnet.com/column/management/koujihi-deflator/)).
  - The majors flag Middle East–related procurement risk in 2026 ([BigGo](https://finance.biggo.jp/news/8U8OIp4B6tLPsnrZmBwQ)).
- **Lenders and cost of capital.**
  - BOJ policy rate raised to 1.25% on 18 Sep 2026 ([NHK](https://news.web.nhk/newsweb/na/nd-20260918de50968)).
  - 10-year JGB yield touched 2.945% in Aug 2026 ([Nikkei](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUB1747W0X10C26A8000000/)).
- **Captive J-REIT exit channel.**
  - TT is the sponsor of Japan Prime Realty Investment Corp (JPR). Its asset manager is 100% owned by TT, and TT holds 122,700 JPR units (3.03%) per JPR's governance report of 26 Mar 2026 ([JPR 運用体制報告書](https://www.jpr-reit.co.jp/file/ir_library_other_file-e1fd4de66a9a0b87bc1127d8cbab0e2741bf0bb7.pdf)).
- **Cash-flow predictability.**
  - 1H FY2026 segment business profit: Building ¥42.7bn, Residential ¥2.9bn; group ¥41.3bn ([TT 1H FY2026 highlights](https://pdf.irpocket.com/C8804/xoA3/ieAo/gkiS/BBY6.pdf); [BigGo 1H summary](https://finance.biggo.jp/news/jpx_tdnet_140120260806512201)).
  - FY2025 equity-method result was a loss of ¥6,870m (FY2024: +¥819m), booked in overseas businesses in the "Other" segment ([TT FY2025 決算短信](https://pdf.irpocket.com/C8804/YpwX/Bm8F/l2Ip.pdf); [TT 決算概要 page](https://tatemono.com/ir/summary/summary.html)).
  - FY2025 profit growth came from higher leasing revenue and higher margins on property sales to investors ([Globe and Mail / company release](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/stocks/TYTMF/pressreleases/201050/tokyo-tatemono-lifts-dividend-as-2025-profits-dip-but-2026-outlook-improves/)).
  - Nikkei Veritas (Feb 2026): TT's share-price surge rides the property boom, and "the key is sustainability of sale gains" ([Nikkei Veritas](https://www.nikkei.com/prime/veritas/article/DGXZQOUC055XX0V00C26A2000000)).
  - In mid-Nov 2025 TT cut FY2025 revenue guidance from ¥503bn to ¥470bn after "partly revising its sales policy for the year", but raised OP guidance from ¥86.0bn to ¥92.5bn ([kabutan article via f-p.jp](https://f-p.jp/media/?p=21023)).
    - The article says the revision came "after the close on the 13th", following a year-to-date high on 7 Oct. Since 13 Oct 2025 was a market holiday, the date is most likely 13 Nov 2025 with Q3 results, which matches a [TT release dated 13 Nov 2025](https://pdf.irpocket.com/C8804/lAG8/eWC4/JVtg.pdf) [verify date].
- **Government interference: condos and foreign buyers.**
  - In Jul 2025 Chiyoda Ward asked industry bodies to put a clause in some new-condo contracts banning resale for five years in principle, and to bar one name from buying multiple units in the same building ([Diamond Fudosan](https://diamond-fudosan.jp/articles/-/1112835); [Haseko Mansion+](https://www.haseko.co.jp/mansionplus/journal/resale-control-251127.html)).
  - The land/MLIT minister called speculative condo trading "not desirable" ([Nikkei, Sep 2025](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUA022CN0S5A900C2000000/)).
  - A Mitsubishi UFJ Trust developer survey (2H FY2024) found foreigners bought 20–40% of new condos in Chiyoda, Minato and Shibuya. The government was reported to be compiling a policy direction by about Jan 2026, with options including extra taxes, a vacancy tax and resale limits ([Diamond Fudosan](https://diamond-fudosan.jp/articles/-/1112835)).
  - The government's survey of foreign condo purchases was extended from Tokyo to Kansai ([visa-asocia](https://visa-asocia.com/column/2129/)).

### Inferences

- **Overall verdict.** A narrow moat that is real but local, centred on Yaesu. It is not a wide moat, because most of the profit pool reprices with the market cycle (rents, sale gains, condo prices), and construction-cost and rate exposure is significant.

### Gaps
- TT's total landbank and redevelopment rights were not retrieved: holdings and planned projects in Yaesu 1-chome North, Nihonbashi, Kyobashi and Otemachi, the floor area under rights, and TT's ownership share in TOFROM YAESU after rights conversion.
- TT's own lease structure was not retrieved: weighted-average lease term, share of fixed-term leases, renewal spreads.
- The effect on TT of the "2024 problem" (overtime caps for construction workers from Apr 2024; background) was not retrieved: contractor terms and delays.
- No data was found on TT's history of government interference (for example, tax changes on condo or land holdings).

## 4. Macro factors: latest data points

### Takeaway
2025–26 has been a two-sided macro mix. Demand-side indicators are at or near cycle highs:
- Central Tokyo office vacancy is 1.87% with rents up 12.5% YoY.
- The 23-ward new-condo average is ¥142.5m.
- Prime cap rates are stable at 3.2%.

Financing and cost indicators are deteriorating:
- The BOJ policy rate is 1.25%, the highest since 1995.
- The 10-year JGB is about 2.9–3.0%.
- The construction cost deflator is up about 5% YoY.

### Cited Findings
**Rates and inflation**
- **BOJ policy rate path.**
  - Dec 2025: raised to 0.75%; that month the 10-year JGB passed 2% and touched 2.1% on 22 Dec 2025, the first time since Feb 1999 ([NRI Kiuchi column, 22 Dec 2025](https://www.nri.com/jp/media/column/kiuchi/20251222_2.html); [Yahoo! expert article](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/expert/articles/5e47a9df3d99d383478e33fb89e2074ba3ab6648)).
  - Jun 2026: raised to 1.00% ([Dai-ichi Life Research Institute](https://www.dlri.co.jp/report/macro/665928.html), which describes the Sep hike as "3 months after the June meeting").
  - 17–18 Sep 2026: raised to 1.25%, the highest since 1995, by a 7–2 vote. This was the shortest gap between hikes under Governor Ueda. The rate is still near the lower end of the nominal neutral range, and some members said the pace could quicken if inflation overshoots ([NHK](https://news.web.nhk/newsweb/na/nd-20260918de50968); [DLRI](https://www.dlri.co.jp/report/macro/665928.html); [Nikkei, summary of opinions](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUB30BNB0Q6A930C2000000/); [ichizen](https://ichizenholdings.co.jp/media/news/boj-rate-hike-125-2026-09/)).

  - Further JGB rises without matching rent growth would pressure valuations and the profitability of TT's investor property sales and J-REIT drop-downs.
- **Condo market.** Supply is at multi-decade lows and prices at records.
  - An initial contract rate of 64.8% is below the 70% level conventionally read as "healthy", and inventory is rising, which signals affordability strain at the top of the cycle.

- **Policy shareholdings.** Unwinding across Japan Inc supports TT selling its own (target of at least ¥130bn, section 8) and recycling the cash into buybacks and investment. It also means Fuyo-group holders of TT shares may sell (supply overhang; not verified).

- **Cross-holding web.** TT ↔ Hulic ↔ Yasuda Logistics is a classic Fuyo/Yasuda triangle, with Hulic holding TT, TT holding Hulic and Yasuda Logistics, and Yasuda Logistics holding Hulic. The MTP's 10%-of-net-assets cap and the at-least-¥130bn sale target mean much of this web is being unwound.
