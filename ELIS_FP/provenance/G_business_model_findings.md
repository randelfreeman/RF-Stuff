## 0. Key takeaways

1. **Product-as-a-service with ~87% recurring rental & maintenance revenue.** Elis owns the textiles/appliances, collects, launders/maintains and redelivers them on fixed routes; "Almost 87% of the Group's revenue relies on these principles of the product-as-a-service business model(1) through its product rental & maintenance service offering." [#2625](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-2625)
2. **Contracts: ~4-year initial term, auto-renewal, termination fee ≈ residual contract value** [#1946](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-1946); **~94% average customer retention (6% losses incl. business failures)** [#829](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-829); **top-10 clients <10% of revenue, largest contract <1%** [#1954](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-1954) [#1955](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-1955).
3. **Cost base = labour (~60% of costs) + energy (6-7% of revenue)**; energy is layered-hedged (2026: 93% gas / 94% electricity; 2027: 81%/87%; 2028: 61%/53% as of H1 2026) [#76](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003774119-76); labour inflation passed through with a lag via indexation (≈50/50 automatic-index vs negotiated contracts, FY2021 call) [#565](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000002520475-565).
4. **Pricing power demonstrated 2022-23** (pricing +8 pts of 21% organic in 2022 [#178](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000002760050-178); ~9 pts of 11.8% in 2023 [#156](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003054377-156)) at the cost of ~1 pt of growth from churn in 2023 and ~1 pt in 2024 [#452](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003054377-452). 2025 organic +3.8% split ~equally price/volume [#703](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-703).
5. **Moat = local density + scale + industrial know-how**: #1/#2/#3 in the vast majority of countries and #1 in more than half [#1032](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-1032); management calls the plant + logistics + purchasing network "really a high entry barrier", with acquired companies ~20% behind on KPIs [#484](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-484). Limits: UK margin capped by competition (incl. "[ Johnson ]") [#749](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-749); healthcare tenders can be price-only (Germany) [#847](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-847) or multi-lot (Mexico) [#137](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003774119-137); North America effectively closed (Cintas, Vestis, UniFirst ≈2/3 of uniform market) [#3092](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-3092).
6. **Supplier names are almost entirely undisclosed** in permitted sources: only **Malongo** (coffee, "captive" partnership) is named; laundry-equipment and traceability suppliers are described as captive/oligopolistic but unnamed [#1976](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-1976). Own manufacturing: **Le Jacquard Français** (France) and **Kennedy Hygiene** (UK), plus garment plants in Estonia, Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Malaysia [#4027](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-4027) [#5348](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-5348).

## 1. Business model in simple terms

### 1.1 What Elis owns, what it charges for
- Model: "Elis's rental and maintenance model is based on a product as a service business model, which favors the service over the product and rental over purchase." [#257](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-257). Product-as-a-service share of revenue: 87% in 2025 [#2609](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-2609); the remaining 13% = "Other Group activities excluding product-as-a-service business model: 13% Pest control Industrial wiping Medical waste management…" [#2431](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-2431). For EU-taxonomy (circular economy) reporting, consumables (paper products, cups) and nursing-home linen maintenance (where "the Group does not own the linen") are excluded from rental revenue [#3299](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-3299) [#3309](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-3309).
- Ownership/accounting: "Rental & maintenance agreements are generally considered service agreements that do not transfer rights of use for the asset to the customer (mainly due to the substantive substitution right for textiles)." [#5620](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-5620). Revenue recognised as services are delivered [#5755](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-5755); monthly-or-longer subscriptions are invoiced in advance (unearned part = contract liabilities) [#5757](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-5757) [#5764](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-5764).
- Customer economics example: Elis charges "only between EUR 5 and EUR 10 for the linen of the hotel room"; a 10-20% price increase = EUR 1-2 on a room "often sold for more than EUR 200 a night"; uniforms "are very often mandatory" [#58](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000002760050-58).
- Customer sizes: France serves customers "from smallest with only EUR 30 per month to EUR 3 million per month" [#1084](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-1084).
- Conversion lever: in Brazil the wash-only ("maintenance solution") portfolio (~50% of revenue in 2014) was converted to rental & maintenance (almost 89% of revenue in 2025) [#2627](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-2627); LatAm rental is "30% higher in terms of profitability" than washing customer-owned linen [#1539](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-1539).

### 1.5 Route-based logistics, plant network, capex
- Plants: "processing centers (over 373 laundries)" [#2031](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-2031). France alone: "69 plants and 46 distribution center … 150,000 customers" (CMD) [#1074](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-1074). Mid/large laundries can deliver "400 tonnes of flat linen and up to 150,000 pieces of garments" (period not stated) [#265](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-265).
- Proximity: sites "generally within 30 to 100 km" of customers [#3179](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-3179); "more than 6,000 routes per day … 5 million kilometers per week" [#286](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-286); fleet "over 11,800 vehicles (nearly 65% for deliveries and nearly 35% for commercial use)" [#3097](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-3097) (another URD page says "nearly 11,500 vehicles" [#378](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-378)); "full vehicles in both directions" (return legs carry soiled linen, mats, empty fountain bottles) [#3180](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-3180) [#3181](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-3181); in-house GLAD routing [#3186](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-3186); logistics insourcing strategy [#3098](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-3098).
- Build time: "it takes only 12 to 14 months to raise a full plant" but lead time is "driven by real estate constraints or licensing constraints" [#381](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-381); "acquiring capacity is more efficient and faster than building a new plant from scratch" [#110](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-110).
- Capex: covers growth & maintenance, hygiene appliances and linen (linen spend varies with activity) [#5401](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-5401); big-plant projects ≈20% of investments on a 5-year roadmap [#353](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-353), ~25% for growth (new plants/capacity) [#363](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-363); two-thirds of linen capex maintains stock in circulation and falls automatically in downturns [#1866](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-1866).

## 5. Macro & micro drivers

### 5.1 Energy
- Weight: "total energy costs represent 6% to 7% of … the top line" [#614](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-614). Guidance: "total energy bill to be around EUR 190 million in 2026, which would be below the level recorded in 2025" [#446](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-446) (reiterated July 2026 [#75](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003774119-75)). **Inconsistency to flag**: 6-7% of FY2025 revenue = €288-336m [calc] vs ~€190m — likely different perimeters (e.g., vehicle fuel), not resolved in sources (an analyst asked for "2025 energy cost, excluding vehicle fuel" [#635](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-635) but the answer was not retrievable).
- Hedging policy (since 2022): layered; at year-end "almost fully hedged for N+1, around 2/3 hedged for N+2 and roughly 1/3 hedged for N+3" [#441](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-441). Coverage: Mar-2026 — 2026 93% gas/94% electricity, 2027 85%/73% [#446](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-446); Jul-2026 — 2026 93%/94%, 2027 81%/87%, 2028 61%/53% [#76](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003774119-76). In Mar-2025 management guided that hedging would "save around EUR 30 million again in '25 in the total cost of energy" [#419](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003394029-419). 2023 gas hedged at ~€80-85/MWh [#379](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000002606072-379).
- 2026 Middle East shock: gas/electricity spot ~+50%; fuel, paper & chemicals +€7m cost in H1 [#75](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003774119-75); FY extra cost "EUR 20 million to EUR 25 million", offset by temporary index-linked surcharges and ~€10m (mostly temporary) cost savings [#466](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003774119-466). Competitive read-across: CEO bets most competitors also hedged 2026 and does not expect an energy-driven share gain [#612](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-612).
- Efficiency: direct-gas equipment instead of steam saves "up to 30% to 40%" energy [#319](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-319); heat pumps/steam-free trials [#484](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-484) [#3150](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-3150).

### 5.2 Labour
- "We have 60% of our costs that are related to labor"; European labour shortage and lower immigration push wages up → structural price increases even at low inflation [#3309](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-3309).
- 2026 wage inflation expected "around 4% … at the group level", less in France, much more in LatAm [#703](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-703); minimum wage +23% Colombia, +13% Mexico in 2026, with pricing lag in H1 [#147](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003774119-147); Germany healthcare: public contracts make minimum-wage pass-through hard [#743](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-743); reduced weekly working hours in some countries [#318](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003512646-318).
- Personnel costs: H1 2025 €1,022.3m vs H1 2024 €946.3m [#310](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000245135173-310); H1 2024 €946.3m vs H1 2023 €870.2m [#399](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000227468208-399).
- Labour-relations risk: strikes/turnover flagged as material financial risk (IRO8) [#3399](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_NONSEC000259591248-3399).

### 5.3 Inflation pass-through mechanics
- Pricing offsets cost inflation, "particularly wage inflation" [#47](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-47); "on average, … price increase above the level of inflation" [#3314](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003468571-3314); lag between cost increase and pricing [#75](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000002606072-75) [#94](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000002666115-94); costs in some countries rise "much faster than the global inflation of the country" [#549](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003774119-549).
- Pricing history: 2022 organic +21% incl. ~8 pts pricing [#178](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000002760050-178); 2023 +11.8% incl. ~9 pts [#156](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003054377-156); 2024 +5.2% (pricing contributed, % not given) [#191](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003394029-191) [#36](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003394029-36); 2025 +3.8%, ~half price [#22](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-22) [#703](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003676656-703); H1 2026 +3.2%, slightly more price than volume [#201](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003774119-201) [#367](https://gam.prontonlp.com/#/ref/$SENTID_T000003774119-367).
