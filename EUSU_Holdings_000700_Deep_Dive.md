## 1. Recommendation & summary

Eusu Holdings is a **pure holding company** (former Hanjin Shipping Holdings) trading at **~0.41× book and ~13.5× depressed earnings with a ~5.9% yield**. This is not a high-multiple growth name whose rating can compress — the discount is already extreme. The investable question is binary: **deep-value re-rating vs. permanent value trap.** Group NAV is credibly above the market price (crown-jewel software subsidiary CyberLogitec + Post Tower real estate + parent net cash), but realization is gated by a **~47%-controlling family whose chair holds a confirmed insider-trading conviction** and by an unlisted, opaque crown jewel. We are **Neutral**: constructive only as **yield plus optionality on a catalyst** (CyberLogitec IPO or a binding capital-return / independent-board commitment); absent that, the base case is dead money at a persistent discount.

## 3. Investment thesis

**Bull (SOTP / re-rating).**
- **Narrow but real moat in CyberLogitec** — embedded, high-switching-cost TOS/carrier software, ~90% export revenue, multi-year growth; a software asset trapped inside a shipping-holdco multiple.
- **Quantifiable asset gap** — 0.41× book ≈ 59% discount to net assets; a controlling CyberLogitec stake plus Post Tower plus parent cash plausibly covers or exceeds the ₩137.5bn cap, with the loss-making forwarding book effectively free.
- **Catalysts** — CyberLogitec IPO (transparent price on the jewel); Korea Value-up + dividend-tax reform; ~5.9% yield floor (dividend doubled 2021 under activist pressure); secular port digitization/automation demand.
- **Capital allocation (charitable read)** — ~80% payout, net-cash balance sheet, no dilutive M&A.

**Bear (three permanent-impairment risks).**
1. **Governance is the business model.** Chair **Choi Eun-young** + related parties ~47% (Choi ~18.1%; Yang Hyun Foundation ~9.9–10.5%). Chair holds a **confirmed criminal conviction** for using undisclosed information to sell Hanjin Shipping shares — *with her two daughters* — days before the Apr-2016 workout, avoiding ~₩1bn of losses (1.5yr prison, ₩1.2bn fine, ~₩503m disgorgement). The controller of the discount, disclosure timing, and dividend has a court-established record of extracting private value. The discount is an equilibrium, not an anomaly.
2. **Low-quality revenue base.** Largest line (forwarding) ran an **operating loss (−₩3.3bn, FY24)**; "revenue" is largely pass-through freight cost; consolidated ROE ~3%.
3. **Concentration + succession skew.** Value sits in one unlisted subsidiary minorities cannot cleanly value or access, while a second-generation transfer is underway — controlled families are incentivized to keep the *listed* holdco cheap (lower inheritance-tax base) precisely while transferring it. Minority and family interests diverge on the only variable that matters.

## 5. Short-seller teardown

- **Revenue vs. value mismatch:** what is visible (forwarding) is bad; what is good (software) is unlisted, unpriceable to minorities, and family-controlled. Slower CyberLogitec overseas TOS bookings collapse the SOTP while the visible P&L still reads as a loss-making forwarder.
- **Moat scope:** holdco none; forwarding none (losses prove it); only CyberLogitec — whose stickiness relies on customers *not* re-platforming, exactly during a cloud/automation migration cycle.
- **Most dangerous competitor (underestimated):** **Kaleris (absorbed Navis)** and cloud-native port/carrier platforms; consolidation onto global cloud TOS turns CyberLogitec's installed base into a legacy book to be displaced.
- **Capital-allocation / related-party flags:** convicted-insider-trader controller (and succession); large idle parent cash minorities argue enriches insiders; ~10% parked in the family **Yang Hyun Foundation**; minorities repeatedly forced to file counter-proposals (dividend, independent auditor, pay caps); IPO dangled and withdrawn as controller-timed optionality.
- **Assumptions to justify the price:** minimal — the price already discounts a permanent discount; the risk is that even floored expectations require the dividend to hold and the assets not to be expropriated, both dependent on the same family.
- **Growth −20–30%:** with no growth premium in the multiple, a miss threatens the **dividend, not the multiple**; a de-rate to 0.30–0.33× book (−20–27%) is plausible with no change to the asset story — in a value trap, book is a ceiling, not a floor.
- **Single permanent-impairment scenario:** controller-serving reorganization (CyberLogitec IPO/stake sale or a split structured for insider vehicles; or a low-ball take-private after engineered cheapness) severing minorities from the only asset worth owning. **Plausibility: moderate-to-high** — needs no external shock, only the incentives and record already on file.

## 6. Valuation

Multiples are not demanding — they are distressed-governance multiples. Correct frame: **NAV × (1 − controlled-holdco discount).** On SOTP, fair value is plausibly above the cap; but the market rationally applies a **~50–60% discount** for governance and non-access, and has done so persistently. **Verdict: cheap on assets, fairly priced on governance-adjusted realizable value.** The security is a ~6% coupon plus an option on the discount closing, with the family controlling the draw.

## 7. Contrarian view

Consensus ("cheap, controlled, value trap — collect the dividend or avoid") is probably right. The non-consensus angle: the forces bears cite may be turning *against* the controller.
1. **Value-up + dividend-tax reform change the controller's own math** — for a second-gen transition, higher dividends/price can become the cheaper path, flipping the historic incentive to keep the holdco depressed; the market still prices the old incentive.
2. **A CyberLogitec IPO is the family's best succession-funding tool** — the event bears fear as expropriation is also the likeliest value-crystallizer, and the controller may need it more than minorities do.
3. **Book is likely understated** — Post Tower and a growing unlisted software co are plausibly carried below economic value, so 0.41× P/B *overstates* asset expensiveness; the risk is access, not value.

**Contrarian bet, precisely:** value-up-era succession incentives may be aligning the controller with a one-time discount-closing event while the market still prices the pre-reform adversarial equilibrium. If real, you are paid ~6% p.a. to wait for a re-rate the controller finally wants too.

**Per-share note.** Aggregate valuation is expressed on a market-cap basis; a confirmed share count is required to convert to per-share. Cross-source data imply ~53m shares (if the chair's 9,676,374 shares ≈ 18.1%), giving an approximate price of ~₩2,600 — **flagged as derived; confirm via DART before modeling per-share.**
