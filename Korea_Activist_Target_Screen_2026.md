# Korea Activist Target Screen — 20 Candidates

**Prepared:** 6 August 2026
**Perspective:** Activist hedge fund, constructive-to-hostile, 2–4 year hold
**Universe:** KOSPI + KOSDAQ, market cap > US$200m
**Working FX:** ₩1,200 / US$1 (the won has strengthened materially through 2026 on foreign equity inflows; see *Data Confidence*)

---

## 1. Executive summary

Korea in August 2026 is the most structurally attractive activist market in developed Asia, for three reasons that did not exist eighteen months ago:

1. **The law changed, three times, in the activists' favour.** Directors now owe a fiduciary duty to *shareholders*, not just to "the company" (July 2025). Cumulative voting becomes mandatory for large listed companies from September 2026. And since March 2026, treasury shares must be cancelled — newly acquired within 12 months, **existing holdings within 18 months**. The last of these is the single biggest change: the treasury block was the standard Korean anti-activist weapon, and it is being disarmed on a statutory clock.

2. **The index rally has been almost entirely a semiconductor event, which has left the rest of the market stranded.** The KOSPI is up ~41% YTD in USD (best major market in the world) after a June peak near 9,385 and a subsequent ~20%+ drawdown to ~6,296 on 6 August. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix alone are ~40% of the index. Breadth has been negative even on strong index days. The practical consequence: a Korean consumer, platform, or industrial business that is flat-to-modestly-up over twelve months has underperformed its own market by 40–50 percentage points. That is a very large, very defensible activist mandate.

3. **The domestic institutional base has flipped from passive to participatory.** The National Pension Service (NPS) in the 2026 season opposed a sitting group chairman's re-election (Hyosung TNC), designated companies for "public focus management," began delegating voting rights to external managers, and signalled willingness to pursue derivative suits. Institutional shareholder proposals achieved a **20% approval rate in 2026 (14 of 69 items)** — historically this number was close to zero — and Align Partners won a board seat at DB Insurance, the first at a controlled Korean company with >₩10tn market cap.

**The screen produced 20 names.** They cluster into four repeatable trade types:

| Type | Count | Core thesis | Names |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Holdco / NAV discount** | 4 | 44–68% discounts to a marked-to-market NAV, now attackable because treasury blocks must be cancelled and directors owe duties to shareholders | Samsung C&T, SK Square, Hanwha Corp, LX International |
| **B. Net-cash orphan** | 7 | Balance sheet holds 20–100%+ of market cap in cash/securities earning nothing; no controlling holder to block a return | KT&G, Kia, NCSoft, Cheil Worldwide, S-1, Nongshim, Sindoh |
| **C. Conglomerate sprawl / undisciplined M&A** | 5 | Good core asset subsidising a portfolio of subscale, loss-making adjacencies bought at the top of a cycle | NAVER, Kakao, NHN, LG H&H, Orion |
| **D. Captive / governance-impaired** | 4 | Structurally profitable business whose margin or capital is transferred to an affiliate, or whose board has a documented failure record | Hyundai Mobis, Hyundai Wia, BGF Retail, Hyundai Elevator |

**Highest conviction five, ranked by expected IRR net of campaign friction:** S-1 Corporation, SK Square, Hyundai Wia, NAVER, Cheil Worldwide.

**A caution I would put in front of the investment committee:** the KOSPI has just fallen ~20% from its June high in a semiconductor-led drawdown. Several of these names are cheap *and* about to face a cyclical earnings reset. The screen selects for balance-sheet strength precisely so that a campaign survives a bad tape — but entry should be staged, not immediate.

## 2. Why now — the 2026 Korean setup

### 2.1 The three Commercial Act amendments

| Amendment | Passed / effective | What it does | Why an activist cares |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First** | Promulgated 2025, effective **July 2025** | Expands directors' duty of loyalty from "the company" to include **protection of shareholders' interests** | Converts value-transfer transactions (non-pro-rata rights issues, related-party pricing, spin-off ratios) from "business judgement" into a litigable breach. This is the legal basis for challenging succession-driven restructurings. |
| **Second** | Promulgated Sept 2025, effective **Sept 2026** | **Mandatory cumulative voting** for large listed companies (assets > ₩2tn); separate election of **two** audit committee members (was one); 1% holders can demand cumulative voting six weeks before a meeting without amending articles | A ~10–15% economic position can now realistically elect a director at a large cap. This is the mechanical unlock: campaigns move from "persuasion" to "arithmetic." |
| **Third** | Passed 25 Feb 2026, effective **6 March 2026** | **Mandatory treasury share cancellation** — newly acquired within 12 months; **existing holdings within 18 months** (i.e. by ~September 2027). Retention requires a board "Treasury Share Retention and Disposal Plan" **approved annually by shareholders**; disposals must be **pro-rata**. | Removes the standard Korean defence (parking treasury with a white knight). Creates automatic EPS/NAV accretion. And creates a **new annual vote to campaign against** — the retention plan. |

The number of Korean listed companies announcing treasury cancellations went 64 (2021) → 102 (2023) → 165 (2024) → 240 (2025), and is accelerating post-amendment. Samsung Electronics is cancelling 87m shares in H1 FY26; SK hynix 15.3m; LG Corp has pledged 100% of remaining treasury by end-FY26.

### 2.2 The ownership and stewardship environment

- **NPS is the swing vote in almost every name on this list**, typically holding 7–10%. In 2026 it pre-announced voting directions on 13 majors (including NAVER, KT&G, POSCO Holdings, Korea Zinc, Hite Jinro, LG Electronics), opposed Hyosung TNC chairman Cho Hyun-joon's re-appointment over excessive compensation, and blocked director overlaps and remuneration proposals elsewhere.
- **Foreign ownership is high and concentrated in index-aware managers.** KT&G is 45.5% foreign-held with Capital Group (5.61%), BlackRock, First Eagle and GIC on the register — a shareholder base that votes.
- **The activist bench is deep and now includes credible domestic funds:** Align Partners (DB Insurance, Coway, Gabia, Dentium, SoluM, APlus), Flashlight Capital (KT&G → now S-1), Palliser (Samsung C&T, LG Chem), Truston (KCC), Whitebox, City of London, KCGI, Anda, Platform Partners. Co-investment and "wolf-pack" support is available in a way it was not in 2020.

### 2.3 The valuation backdrop

Roughly 69% of KOSPI companies still trade below book; the market's average P/B is around 1.0x notwithstanding the semiconductor re-rating. Companies representing ~51% of market capitalisation have published Value-Up plans — which means half the market has publicly committed to targets an activist can hold them to, and the other half has conspicuously declined to.

## 3. Screening methodology

A name had to clear **all five** gates:

| Gate | Test applied | Notes on application |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Structurally sound, mis-managed** | Positive through-cycle EBIT; defensible market position or irreplaceable asset; underperformance attributable to capital allocation / cost / portfolio decisions rather than to terminal industry decline | This excluded most Korean steel, petrochemicals, and legacy retail — those are *structurally* impaired, and an activist is buying a melting ice cube |
| **2. TSR underperformance** | 3–5 year TSR below both sector and index; 1-year included but **de-weighted**, because the 2026 index is a semiconductor proxy and almost nothing beat it | Sector-relative is the honest comparator this year. I show both. |
| **3. Balance sheet capacity** | Net debt / EBITDA < 1.0x, net cash preferred | 16 of 20 are net cash. This is the gate that makes a campaign *fundable* — the company can buy back stock without a credit event, so "we can't afford it" is not a defence |
| **4. Ownership** | No single shareholder >40%; fragmented or institution-heavy register | Applied to the **largest single holder**, per the brief. Where a family/affiliate bloc aggregates near or above 40%, I flag it explicitly rather than silently pass it |
| **5. Fixable issues** | At least two of: excess overhead, undisciplined M&A, subscale non-core segments, undervalued/unmonetised assets, excess cash, weak management, weak board | Every name has ≥3 |

**Deliberate exclusions.** Banks and insurers were largely excluded because Gate 3 (net debt/EBITDA) is meaningless for them — but note that DB Insurance, JB Financial and the regional banks are among the most successful Korean activist situations of the last three years, and I list them in the watchlist. Companies with live control contests where the outcome is binary and legal rather than operational (Korea Zinc) were excluded as event-driven, not activist.

## 5. How to read the company profiles

- **ᵛ** = verified against a source dated 2026, cited in §8. **ᵉ** = estimate, derived from last reported financials applied to the verified current price, or from sector norms. Treat every **ᵉ** as a hypothesis to be confirmed on a terminal before capital is committed.
- **TSR** is shown against *sector* and against the *KOSPI*. In 2026 the KOSPI comparison flatters nobody — the index is a memory-semiconductor proxy — so the sector column carries the analytical weight. 3yr and 5yr figures are annualised.
- **Shareholder support** is scored 1–10 (10 = easiest), built from: register concentration, NPS position size and disposition, foreign/institutional share, proxy-adviser precedent, and whether a controlling bloc can block unilaterally.
- **FCF yield** is levered FCF to equity market cap.

### 1. Samsung C&T (028260) — Diversified holding / E&C / trading
**Market cap ₩56.9tnᵛ (31 Jul 2026) · ~US$47bn · Net cash at holdco**

| Metric | Value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| TSR 1yr | Strongly positiveᵉ — +13% in a single session on 24 Jun 2026ᵛ on Samsung Electronics stake value and shareholder-return hopes | Lagged the KOSPI's ~100% peak-to-peak move |
| TSR 3yr / 5yr p.a. | Positive but **far below the growth in its own NAV**ᵉ | The discount has *widened* as the underlying stakes compounded |
| Discount to NAV | **~60–68%**ᵛ (Palliser: 63% undervalued; Whitebox: 68%) | The core number |
| EV/Sales | ~1.1xᵉ | Depressed by ₩41tn+ of low-margin E&C/trading revenue |
| EV/EBIT | ~15xᵉ headline; **negative-to-nil on a look-through basis** once listed stakes are marked | The operating businesses are close to free |
| P/E | ~14xᵉ | Meaningless — equity-accounted affiliate income dominates |
| FCF yield | ~4–5%ᵉ | E&C working capital makes this volatile |
| EBITDA / EBIT margin | ~8% / ~6%ᵉ | Structurally thin; E&C and trading are the drag |
| Net leverage | Net cash at holding level | Project debt sits at subsidiaries |

**Top 5 shareholders:** Lee Jae-yong 18.13%ᵉ · **KCC Corp ~8.6% (stake worth ~₩4.9tnᵛ)** · National Pension Service ~7%ᵉ · Lee Boo-jin 6.19%ᵉ · Lee Seo-hyun 6.19%ᵉ. Family bloc aggregates ~31%. Activists on register: Palliser (0.62%ᵛ), City of London, Whitebox.

**What an activist would say**
- *"You are the cheapest way in the world to own Samsung Electronics and Samsung Biologics, and you have made that a liability rather than an asset."* The stakes — Samsung Electronics 5.01%ᵛ, Samsung Biologics 43.06%ᵛ, Samsung Life 19.34%ᵛ, Samsung SDS ~17%ᵉ — are worth materially more than the entire market capitalisation, meaning the market ascribes negative value to a ₩41tn revenue operating business.
- *"The discount is not a market failure, it is a governance choice."* Samsung C&T is priced as the family's control vehicle rather than as a company, and every year that the board declines to publish a NAV-linked capital return policy it ratifies that pricing.
- *"The law has now removed your last excuse."* Directors owe duties to shareholders since July 2025; treasury shares must be cancelled by ~September 2027; cumulative voting arrives September 2026. Three separate statutes now say what Palliser has been saying since 2023.

**Two–three most actionable changes**
1. **Publish a NAV-linked buyback policy** — a standing commitment to repurchase whenever the discount exceeds a stated threshold, funded by affiliate dividends, with automatic cancellation. This is the single highest-return action and requires no asset sales.
2. **Cancel 100% of remaining treasury stock immediately** rather than waiting out the 18-month statutory grace period, and pre-commit that no future treasury will be held.
3. **Dispose of Fashion and Leisure/Resort.** Neither is defensible inside a construction and trading group; both are saleable to domestic strategic or PE buyers; proceeds fund the buyback.

**Shareholder support: Moderate — 6/10.** The register is unusually favourable for a chaebol flagship: three named activists, a large foreign institutional base, NPS at ~7%, and — critically — **KCC's ~₩4.9tn block is itself under activist pressure from Truston to be liquidated**, which creates a natural ally for value realisation. Against that, the Lee family bloc at ~31% plus friendly affiliate holdings makes a board seat hard without cumulative voting, and 2024 precedent (ISS and Glass Lewis both backed the shareholder proposals; they still lost) shows the bloc holds. September 2026 cumulative voting changes this arithmetic materially.

**The company's defence**
Expect: (i) "we already have a Value-Up plan and have been cancelling treasury since 2023"; (ii) "the affiliate stakes are strategic and cannot be sold without destabilising the group" — legally true for Samsung Life given financial-holding rules, less true for the rest; (iii) "E&C earnings are recovering and will close the gap organically"; (iv) quiet mobilisation of affiliate and friendly holders. The strongest genuine defence is that a forced unwind of the Samsung Electronics stake has consequences for group control that the Korean state has historically been willing to protect.

**Activist angles:** *Capital allocation* (NAV-linked buyback, treasury cancellation) · *Portfolio simplification* (Fashion, Leisure, Food) · *Governance* (independent chair, NAV disclosure, board refresh via cumulative voting).

### 2. SK Square (402340) — Semiconductor holding company
**Market cap ~₩131tnᵉ · SK hynix stake = 158% of market capᵛ · Net cash**

| Metric | Value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| TSR 1yr | Very strongᵉ — but **less than SK hynix**, its own principal asset | The discount absorbed the upside |
| TSR 3yr / 5yr p.a. | Strong in absolute terms; **materially below look-through NAV growth**ᵉ | Since the 2021 SK Telecom demerger the discount has never sustainably closed |
| Discount to NAV | **43.8%ᵛ (Jun 2026)**, having troughed at 39.9%ᵛ from ~66% | Analyst-estimated structural floor ~22%ᵛ |
| Key holding | **SK hynix 20.1%ᵛ, worth ~₩207tnᵛ**; >98% of NAVᵛ | Everything else is rounding |
| EV/EBIT, P/E | Not meaningful — a single-asset holding vehicle | Value it on NAV only |
| FCF yield | Dividend inflow ~₩219bn p.a.ᵛ from SK hynix's fixed ₩1,500/share 2025–27 dividendᵛ | Rises sharply if hynix lifts payout |
| Net leverage | Net cash | Deleveraged via SK shieldus and other disposals |

**Top 5 shareholders:** SK Inc ~30.0%ᵉ · National Pension Service ~7%ᵉ · foreign institutional (BlackRock, Vanguard, GIC) collectively >20%ᵉ · domestic asset managers · retail. No holder above 40%.

**What an activist would say**
- *"You are a ₩131tn company holding ₩207tn of a single liquid listed security, and you are asking shareholders to pay you for the privilege of the wrapper."* The discount is not a valuation debate; it is arithmetic against a screen price.
- *"Your dividend income alone can retire the discount if you let it."* SK hynix's fixed dividend gives SK Square a predictable, growing cash stream with essentially no operating call on it — the textbook funding source for a permanent buyback.
- *"The venture portfolio is a tax on the stock."* 11st, T Map Mobility, SK Planet, Dreamus and the content assets consume management attention and capital, and are the stated reason the market applies a wrapper discount rather than a pass-through valuation.

**Two–three most actionable changes**
1. **Convert 100% of SK hynix dividend receipts into buyback-and-cancel, permanently**, disclosed as a formula rather than an annual discretionary decision.
2. **Liquidate the non-core portfolio on a stated timetable** — 11st, T Map, Dreamus, content — and distribute proceeds. Each disposal both raises cash and removes a piece of the discount rationale.
3. **Commit to a discount-triggered structural remedy**: if the discount exceeds a threshold for a sustained period, the board must present shareholders with an in-specie distribution of SK hynix shares, a merger with SK Inc, or a liquidation plan. The point is to make the discount a board problem with a deadline.

**Shareholder support: Easy — 8/10.** The best register on this list. No controlling shareholder, SK Inc's ~30% is not blocking, NPS is present, and the foreign institutional base is large and unusually aligned because the thesis needs no operating judgement — it is a discount to a screen price. A prospective **SK hynix Nasdaq listing**ᵛ would sharpen the comparison further and is a natural campaign catalyst. The one complication: SK Inc has its own NAV discount and its own activist (an ex-Elliott fund has been buildingᵛ), so group-level solutions may be negotiated above SK Square's head.

**The company's defence**
"We are an investment company, not a tracker — the discount reflects tax leakage on eventual disposals and our role as SK Group's semiconductor capital allocator." Expect the company to point to the discount having already narrowed from ~66%, to argue the venture portfolio is optionality, and to resist any in-specie distribution on the grounds that the hynix stake is a strategic control position for the group. That last argument is the real one and is the reason the discount will not go to zero — but it does not justify 44%.

**Activist angles:** *Capital allocation* (formulaic buyback funded by hynix dividends) · *Portfolio simplification* (exit non-core in full) · *Governance* (independent directors with capital-markets mandate; discount-triggered structural review).

### 3. Hanwha Corp (000880) — Diversified holding company
**Market cap ~₩5tnᵉ · NAV discount 64.1%ᵛ · Net cash at holdco**

| Metric | Value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| TSR 1yr | Positiveᵉ, driven by the defence complex re-rating at Hanwha Aerospace | The holdco captured a fraction of the subsidiary move |
| TSR 3yr / 5yr p.a. | Below both the defence sector and the KOSPIᵉ | Classic holdco leakage |
| Discount to NAV | **64.1%ᵛ pre-restructuring** — elevated even against Korean holdco norms, explicitly because of governance uncertainty around the spin-offᵛ | The market is pricing the succession risk |
| EV/EBIT, P/E | Not meaningful at holdco level | Value on NAV |
| Net leverage | Net cash at holdco; substantial leverage at operating subsidiaries | Standard Korean holdco profile |

**Top 5 shareholders:** Kim Seung-youn 22.65%ᵉ · Hanwha Energy (controlled by the three sons) ~9%ᵉ · National Pension Service ~7%ᵉ · Kim Dong-kwan / Kim Dong-won / Kim Dong-seon individually ~4–5% eachᵉ · treasury. Family-plus-affiliate bloc aggregates well above 40% on a look-through basis — **the largest single holder is below 40%, so it passes the screen as written, but this is the weakest ownership profile in the twenty and should be sized accordingly.**

**What an activist would say**
- *"The restructuring is being run for three heirs, not for 100% of the share register."* The August 2026 spin-off of **Hanwha Machinery & Services Holdings** — 57 affiliates, KOSPI debut 25 Augustᵛ, led by third son Kim Dong-seonᵛ — allocates businesses along succession lines. Splits of that kind are where Korean minority value is destroyed, in the ratio.
- *"You have a documented pattern."* The Hanwha Aerospace rights offering drew sustained criticism for funnelling value to the family via Hanwha Energy and then diluting minoritiesᵛ; the FSS put a brake on Hanwha Solutions' rights offering in April 2026ᵛ. Two regulator-flagged capital raises in eighteen months is a board problem, not bad luck.
- *"Since July 2025 this is a legal question, not a stylistic one."* Directors now owe duties to shareholders — the standard against which a spin-off ratio and a non-pro-rata raise are judged has changed underneath the group mid-restructuring.

**Two–three most actionable changes**
1. **Demand an independent fairness opinion on the Hanwha M&S spin-off ratio and share-allocation mechanics**, published before the 25 August listing, with a shareholder vote on any subsequent related-party transfer of the new entity's shares.
2. **Adopt a written policy prohibiting non-pro-rata equity issuance at listed subsidiaries** without independent-director approval and a shareholder vote — directly addressing the Aerospace and Solutions precedents.
3. **Holdco discount remedy:** cancel all treasury under the March 2026 mandate, adopt a through-cycle payout floor funded by subsidiary dividends, and publish quarterly NAV.

**Shareholder support: Hard — 4/10.** The lowest score here. The family and affiliate bloc can carry ordinary resolutions; the spin-off was approved with 99.95%ᵛ, which tells you what the register does when asked. But the picture is not static: NPS has shown in 2026 that it will oppose a sitting chairman (Hyosung TNC) and has begun pursuing derivative suits, and the FSS has already intervened once at a Hanwha entity. **The realistic route here is regulatory and legal pressure plus public campaigning, not a proxy win.** Treat as a lower-weight, longer-duration position.

**The company's defence**
"The spin-off *is* the answer to the conglomerate discount, and it was approved by 99.95% of voting shareholders." That is a genuinely strong first line. Expect further: the Aerospace raise funded a real defence-sector capex cycle that has since been vindicated by the share price; the KAI stake-building (~₩1tn, 11.21%ᵛ) is industrial strategy, not empire-building. The activist counter is that a good outcome does not retrospectively validate an unfair process — which is precisely what the amended fiduciary standard is for.

**Activist angles:** *Governance* (spin-off ratio, non-pro-rata issuance policy, independent directors) · *Capital allocation* (treasury cancellation, payout floor, NAV disclosure) · *M&A discipline* (KAI stake-building rationale and funding).

| P/E | ~6–7xᵉ | |
| Dividend | **₩2,000 DPSᵛ, 3.77% yieldᵛ** | Respectable, but small relative to the cash pile |
| FCF yield | ~10–15%ᵉ, highly cycle-dependent | |
| EBITDA / EBIT margin | ~4% / ~2.5%ᵉ | Structurally thin — this is a trading business |
| Net leverage | Net cash; **₩1.26tn grossᵛ = 66% of market cap** | The headline fact |

**Top 5 shareholders:** LX Holdings 24.7%ᵉ · National Pension Service ~9–10%ᵉ · domestic institutions · foreign institutions ~10%ᵉ · retail (large free float). No holder near 40%.

**What an activist would say**
- *"You hold two-thirds of your market cap in cash and trade at 0.44x book. The market is telling you it does not trust you with the next acquisition."* That is a verdict on capital allocation, and it is reversible in a single board decision.
- *"You bought commodity assets into a commodity peak."* The resources portfolio — coal, nickel, and the diversification into LNG power and industrial materials — was assembled when those cycles were rich. The market has marked the outcome.
- *"LX Pantos is a good business trapped inside a bad wrapper."* The logistics arm would be valued on freight-forwarder multiples as a standalone; inside a trading house with a resources tail it is valued at nothing.

**Two–three most actionable changes**
1. **Return ₩600–800bn via buyback-and-cancel over 24 months** — roughly a third of the market cap — while retaining ample trading working-capital headroom. At 0.44x book this is the highest-return use of capital available to the company by a wide margin.
2. **Separate LX Pantos** (IPO or trade sale). Logistics and commodity trading have no operating overlap, entirely different capital profiles, and entirely different buyers.
3. **Exit the resource assets on a stated timetable** and commit publicly to no further principal investment in commodity extraction.

**Shareholder support: Easy — 8/10.** A 24.7% holder, a large free float, NPS around 9–10%, and a thesis that requires no forecast — just a comparison of the cash balance to the market cap. Korean domestic activists (KCGI, Anda, Align) have run precisely this playbook at similar mid-caps, and the small size makes a meaningful stake cheap to build. The one caution is liquidity: building 5%+ will move the price.

**The company's defence**
"Trading houses require large cash balances for working capital, counterparty credit, and commodity margining — the cash is not excess, it is inventory finance." This is the standard and partially legitimate answer; the counter is to size the genuine working-capital requirement from peak historical usage and return the demonstrable surplus above it. Expect also "we are already paying a 3.8% dividend," and resistance to a Pantos separation on the grounds of group synergy.

**Activist angles:** *Capital allocation* (buyback at 0.44x book) · *Portfolio simplification* (Pantos separation, resources exit) · *M&A discipline* (public capital-deployment constraint).

### 5. KT&G (033780) — Tobacco, health functional food, real estate

2. **Merge BGF Retail and BGF Co**, eliminating the holdco layer and its discount.
3. **Independent review of related-party arrangements** with BGF affiliates (logistics, packaging, services), with disclosure of terms and volumes — the standard leakage point in Korean franchise groups.

**Shareholder support: Easy — 8/10.** One of the better registers here: the holdco is at ~30%, NPS at ~10%, and a large foreign institutional base. The one-year performance gap is so stark and so easily communicated that it does most of the work. A payout proposal is close to uncontroversial and would very likely pass or be conceded pre-emptively. The holdco merger is the harder ask.

**The company's defence**
"Convenience retail is fiercely competitive with rising minimum wages, franchisee profitability pressure and political scrutiny of franchise terms; we retain cash to support franchisees and to invest in proximity commerce." The regulatory and franchisee-relations pressure is real and constrains margin expansion — which is precisely why the campaign should be about the payout ratio and the holdco, not about operations.

**Activist angles:** *Capital allocation* (payout to ~100%, buyback-and-cancel) · *Portfolio simplification* (holdco merger) · *Governance* (related-party review, independent directors).

**What an activist would say**
- *"You own the largest installed elevator base in Korea, which is a maintenance annuity, and you are valued as a construction sub-contractor."* Otis, KONE and Schindler are valued on their service books at 14–18x EBIT. Korea has an ageing high-rise stock and a mandatory inspection regime — this annuity is, if anything, more secure.
- *"This board has a documented record of using the company's balance sheet for group control purposes."* The derivative litigation brought by Schindler over the Hyundai Merchant Marine derivative contracts resulted in judgments against the controlling shareholder for damages caused to this company. That is not a governance concern in the abstract; it is a finding.
- *"A 51% gap to consensus fair value"*ᵛ *"on a business with a recurring revenue base and net cash is a governance discount, and it has a name."*

**Two–three most actionable changes**
1. **Report the maintenance and service business separately** — installed base, contract count, renewal rates, margin — so it can be valued against Otis and KONE rather than against Korean construction. This is the cheapest available re-rating.
2. **Board reconstitution:** a majority-independent board with an independent chair, and a standing prohibition on balance-sheet transactions serving group control. Given the litigation history this is both saleable to shareholders and specifically justified.
3. **Dispose of legacy non-core holdings** and initiate a buyback-and-cancel from net cash and service cash flow.

**Shareholder support: Easy — 8/10.** The most fragmented register in the screen: the controlling bloc is only ~20%, which is not enough to block anything, and the free float is large. The Schindler litigation history is documented, judicially determined, and directly relevant to the amended directors' duty — very few Korean campaigns get evidence that clean. The offsetting risks are practical: modest liquidity, thin sell-side coverage (a single analyst on record), and a substantial retail base that is unpredictable in a proxy contest.

**The company's defence**
"The historic disputes are settled and belong to a prior era of the group; the current business is the Korean market leader with a strong order book and a growing overseas presence." Expect an argument that Korean elevator pricing and the domestic construction cycle justify a discount to the global majors — partly true for new equipment, not true for the service book. The company's real protection is obscurity rather than argument.

**Activist angles:** *Governance* (board reconstitution, litigation history, control-transaction prohibition) · *Disclosure / portfolio* (separate the service annuity, dispose non-core) · *Capital allocation* (buyback from net cash).

## 8. Portfolio construction and campaign sequencing

**Sizing.** The register quality, not the valuation gap, should drive position size. A 44% NAV discount at SK Square with no controlling shareholder is worth more than a 64% discount at Hanwha Corp where the family votes the outcome.

| Support score | Names | Suggested treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **8–9 (Easy)** | KT&G, NAVER, SK Square, LX International, BGF Retail, Hyundai Elevator | Core positions. Public campaigns viable. Board seats realistically achievable post-September 2026 |
| **6–7 (Moderate)** | S-1, NCSoft, Kakao, Hyundai Wia, Samsung C&T, Kia, Hyundai Mobis, Cheil, Nongshim, Orion, LG H&H, NHN | Constructive engagement first; escalate selectively. Capital-return resolutions will pass long before board resolutions |
| **4–5 (Hard)** | Hanwha Corp, Sindoh | Small, patient, legal-and-regulatory pressure rather than proxy contests |

**Calendar.** Three dates structure the next eighteen months:

1. **September 2026 — cumulative voting becomes mandatory** for companies with assets above ₩2tn. Stakes intended to elect a director should be built *before* this, and 1% holders must file the cumulative-voting request **six weeks ahead of the meeting**ᵛ.
2. **October 2026 — Flashlight's S-1 shareholder proposal**ᵛ. The first post-amendment campaign against a Samsung affiliate. Its outcome will set the tone for Samsung C&T and Cheil Worldwide, and is worth positioning around.
3. **~September 2027 — the treasury-share cancellation deadline** for existing holdings (18 months from 6 March 2026). Every company holding a significant treasury block faces a forced, accretive event, and an annual shareholder vote on any retention plan in the meantime. Screen for the largest treasury blocks and own them into the deadline.

**Two structural observations for the investment committee.**

- **The 2026 approval rate for institutional shareholder proposals was 20% (14 of 69)**ᵛ. That is a transformed environment relative to five years ago, but it still means four in five proposals fail. Campaigns should be built to win concessions through the *threat* of a vote, and the proposals that actually pass are overwhelmingly about capital return and audit-committee independence — not about board control or asset sales.
- **The most reliable Korean activist outcome is not a proxy win; it is a pre-emptive concession.** KT&G returned ₩3.7tnᵛ without ever losing a vote on the substance. Structure entries so that a pre-emptive concession is a good outcome, not a disappointment.

## 9. Data confidence — read this before using any number

This is a **first-pass screen assembled from public sources**, not a diligence pack. The distinction matters:

**What is verified (ᵛ).** Share prices, market capitalisations, ownership percentages, campaign facts, regulatory provisions, and the specific performance and valuation statistics carrying a ᵛ marker were confirmed against 2026-dated sources listed in §10. Dates are shown because the market has been extraordinarily volatile — the KOSPI peaked near 9,385 in June 2026 and stood at ~6,296 on 6 Augustᵛ, a ~20%+ drawdown, so a market cap verified in April is not a market cap today.

**What is estimated (ᵉ).** Margins, EV/EBIT, EV/Sales, FCF yields, several net-debt positions and most multi-year TSR figures are estimates, derived by applying last-reported financials to verified current prices, or from sector norms. **Every ᵉ figure should be replaced with terminal data before capital is committed.** Where I could not form a confident estimate I have said so in words rather than invent a number.

**Specific known weaknesses.**
- **FX.** ₩1,200/US$ is inferred by cross-checking KRW and USD market caps for Kia, NAVER and Hyundai Mobis. The won has strengthened sharply through 2026; sources quoting USD market caps at different dates therefore imply different rates, and several USD figures in §4 and §6 come from different as-of dates. Treat USD figures as indicative.
- **SK Square's market capitalisation (~₩131tn)** is derived from the verified statement that its SK hynix stake equals 158% of market capᵛ and is worth ~₩207tnᵛ. The NAV-discount figures (39.9%, 43.8%, 66%)ᵛ come from different dates in 2026 and are not internally consistent with each other. Verify directly.
- **Hanwha Corp's market capitalisation (~₩5tn) and Sindoh's shareholder aggregate are estimates**, flagged in-line. The Hanwha M&S spin-off (KOSPI listing 25 August 2026ᵛ) will change Hanwha Corp's NAV composition materially between the date of this note and any investment decision.
- **Kakao's market cap** is verified at ₩22.03tn as at 17 April 2026ᵛ, but the share price has fallen since; the August figure is ~₩17tnᵉ.
- **Reported TSR comparators differ by source and index.** NAVER's −39.07% and LG H&H's −52.87% are measured against the FTSE Developed Asia Pacific Indexᵛ; BGF Retail's gap is against the Korean marketᵛ; S-1's −30% is a ten-year absolute figure against a KOSPI up 370%ᵛ. They are not directly comparable to one another.
- **One source-derived figure was discarded as implausible** (a net-income figure for Nongshim implying a ~28% net margin) rather than reproduced.

**What this screen cannot tell you.** Free float and borrow availability, actual daily liquidity, current treasury-share balances by company, standstill or shareholder agreements, the precise composition of each board, and the pending Korean tax treatment of in-specie distributions. All are material to campaign design, and none is reliably available from public web sources.
