1. Executive Summary

YTL Corporation is the top holding company of the Yeoh family's Malaysian infrastructure group. Its value is overwhelmingly a claim on four listed vehicles — YTL Power International, Malayan Cement, YTL Hospitality REIT and Singapore-listed Starhill Global REIT (plus SGX-listed NSL Ltd held through YTL Cement) — wrapped around a set of unlisted businesses in construction, hotels, property and cement.

  • The listed stakes alone are worth roughly RM31.3bn, or RM2.70 per share — approximately 105% of the current market capitalisation. On our estimates the market is therefore ascribing a negative value to the entire unlisted estate (hotels, construction, property, the non-Malayan-Cement part of YTL Cement) net of holding-company debt.
  • Adding an estimated RM9.2bn of unlisted assets and deducting an estimated RM4.5bn of net debt sitting outside the listed subsidiaries gives a sum-of-the-parts of RM3.10/share — a 16.8% discount. This is materially narrower than the "~30% conglomerate discount" often quoted, because most sell-side SOTPs do not fully charge the ex-listed leverage or the outstanding warrants.
  • The single most important, least-discussed fact in this story is the 2025 bonus warrant issue. Up to 2.27bn free non-tradeable YTL Corp warrants struck at RM1.50 (three-year tenure) are deeply in the money against a RM2.58 share price and a RM3.10 SOTP. Full exercise adds ~20% to share count and destroys roughly 20 sen per share of NAV. Fully diluted, the discount is only ~11%.
  • YTL Power is up ~63% year-to-date on AI data-centre enthusiasm. YTL Corp is a levered claim on an asset that has already re-rated. That is the central risk: the stub is cheap, but the anchor asset is not.
  • Management's track record on discount narrowing is mixed-to-poor. Genuine positives (2012 YTL Cement delisting, 2018 YTL Land privatisation, the 2022 cement consolidation, the January 2026 RM755m Malayan Cement placement) are offset by adding two new listed layers since 2024 (Ranhill, NSL), a dormant buyback (58.7m treasury shares, ~0.5% of capital, with no purchases in the 12 months to the 2025 statement date) and a warrant issue the market read as dilution — both stocks fell sharply on announcement.

Our conclusion in one line

YTL Corp is modestly cheap on assets and expensive on earnings (~20x FY26 attributable EPS). The discount is real but roughly half as wide as the consensus narrative claims once warrants and ex-listed debt are charged. We would own it as a lower-beta way to hold YTL Power's data-centre optionality plus a free option on Malaysian cement — not as a discount-closure trade, because the controlling family has a structural economic incentive to keep the holding-company layer in place.

Look-through leverage — the number bulls understate

On a look-through basis an investor is buying roughly RM50bn of enterprise value with RM30bn of equity. The holding-company discount is partly compensation for that gearing, and gearing is why the discount should not be expected to close to zero.

8. Bull Case — and the Path to a Narrower Discount

The core proposition

You are buying, at a ~17% discount (11% diluted), a controlling interest in the only Malaysian-listed platform that combines a regulated UK water monopoly, a Singapore generation and retail utility, a genuinely contracted AI data-centre business, the dominant Malaysian cement franchise and a Singapore/Malaysia prime-retail REIT — with a captive construction arm to build it all.

1. The data-centre business has crossed from narrative to earnings

  • YTL Power's data-centre segment pre-tax profit rose more than tenfold to RM345.5m in FY2026 from RM32.1m — this is now a real, ramping P&L line, not a slide.
  • The first Nvidia GB200-powered facility at Kulai was completed October 2025; ~300MW built with a further 200MW targeted for 1Q2027; ~298MW contracted as at March 2026.
  • August 2026: a gigawatt-scale campus with JLand Group at Sedenak Tech Park West takes planned capacity toward 2.4GW by 2030. Land optionality of up to 545 acres.
  • Critically, YTL owns the power, the land, the construction arm and the cement. Very few operators capture the whole value chain; the intra-group margin recapture is real and accrues to YTL Corp shareholders at multiple levels.

2. The cement business is being systematically underrated

  • Malayan Cement FY2026: pre-tax profit +35% to RM1.33bn, net profit +34% to RM903m, revenue +10% to RM4.99bn. Q4 cement & building materials segment profit RM493m, +31% y/y.
  • This is a consolidated domestic industry with pricing discipline, feeding a construction upcycle (data centres, JS-SEZ, rail, housing) with multi-year visibility.
  • YTL Corp's 66% of Malayan Cement alone is worth RM6.3bn — 21% of YTL Corp's market cap — for an asset the market treats as a late-cycle commodity.

3. Wessex Water is at maximum pessimism

  • The CMA's PR24 redetermination concluded March 2026 with marginally improved terms and £104bn of sector AMP8 investment confirmed. The regulatory reset risk that has capped the stock for two years is now behind the asset, not ahead of it.
  • UK regulated water assets are typically valued at a premium to RCV in M&A. Nothing in YTL Power's price reflects that.

4. The holdco has genuine, near-dated catalysts for narrowing

  • A YTL Data Center Holdings IPO (Malaysia, Singapore, US or UK have all been floated) or a data-centre sukuk would put an explicit market value on the asset, which is currently embedded and invisible. A PowerSeraya listing has been mentioned for 2027–28.
  • Capital returned from the Malayan Cement placement (RM755m, January 2026) sits at YTL Cement — deployable into a buyback rather than more bolt-ons.
  • Warrant proceeds of up to RM3.4bn (YTL Corp) and further sums at YTL Power arrive as primary equity over 2026–28, removing any need for a dilutive rights issue to fund Wessex equity or DC capex.
  • EPF is buying — 10.28% to 12.6% of YTL Corp during 2026. A large domestic institution accumulating is the most plausible source of governance pressure in this market.

5. Valuation support and the discount arithmetic

  • FY26 DPS held at 8 sen (interim raised to 6 sen from 5 sen) — a 3.1% yield, covered ~1.6x by attributable earnings.
  • Consensus one-year targets cluster around RM3.42 (range RM2.79–4.25), broadly the undiluted SOTP.
  • If the discount merely compresses from 17% to 8% with no change in underlying asset values, the stock is worth ~RM2.85 (+10%). Combine that with mid-single-digit NAV growth and the holding-period return is respectable without requiring heroics.

9. Bear Case — Why the Discount Widens, or Simply Never Closes

The structural objection

The Yeoh family holds ~47.7% of YTL Corp, which in turn holds ~45.6% of YTL Power. Their economic interest in YTL Power is therefore only about 21.8%, yet they control it absolutely. The holding-company layer is the mechanism that converts a minority economic stake into full control. Collapsing the structure — the single action that would eliminate the discount — would force the family either to dilute control or to spend a great deal of capital. The discount is not an oversight the market will one day correct. It is the price of a control arrangement the controller has every incentive to preserve.

1. The warrants are a structural, dated headwind

  • Up to 2.27bn YTL Corp warrants at RM1.50 and 1.67bn YTL Power warrants at RM2.45 — both deeply in the money, both expiring on a three-year clock from mid-2025.
  • Full exercise expands YTL Corp's share capital by ~20% at a price ~52% below our SOTP. The mechanical NAV destruction is roughly 20 sen per share.
  • YTL Power's share count has already grown 5.1% in twelve months; net income fell 15% while EPS fell 17% — the wedge is the dilution.
  • Because the warrants are non-tradeable, minorities who cannot or will not fund the exercise are diluted outright. The market understood this instantly: both stocks fell sharply on the January 2025 announcement.

2. Two businesses are losing meaningful money

  • YTL Communications ("Yes"). 3QFY26 loss before tax widened to RM115.0m from RM41.4m; 9M FY26 loss RM310.6m versus RM125.4m. Revenue fell 28% to RM522.7m for the nine months. This single line accounted for roughly 35% of the entire decline in YTL Power's group pre-tax profit. There is no credible path to scale against Maxis, CelcomDigi and U Mobile.
  • Construction. Estimated at approximately a RM62m loss for FY2026. The captive EPC arm the bulls describe as strategic is, on current disclosure, destroying earnings.

3. Group earnings are going backwards

  • FY2026: revenue +3% to RM31.61bn but profit after tax fell to RM2.83bn from RM3.58bn. Net profit attributable for 9M FY26 fell to RM1.11bn from RM1.35bn; basic EPS 9.61 sen versus 12.20 sen.
  • Q4 FY26 pre-tax profit of RM1,192.8m was +38% q/q but –15% y/y. The sequential improvement flatters a declining year.
  • PowerSeraya — historically the group's cash engine — earned S$70m in 3QFY26 against S$128m a year earlier, on weaker retail and vesting margins, lower volumes and an adverse MYR/SGD move. Forthcoming LRMC-based vesting resets (carbon tax rising to S$45/tCO2e in 2026 and potentially S$50–80 by 2028–30) are a live margin risk.

4. Wessex Water is a call on shareholder capital, not a source of it

  • 2024–25 RoRE of 0.4% against a 4.2% base allowance, driven by overspend and a negative retail margin.
  • Credit outlooks moved from Stable to Negative in March 2025 pending clarity on shareholder support to strengthen the capital structure.
  • Wessex's own PR24 plan contemplated a full dividend restriction through AMP8 (2025–30) with no new equity. Wessex has estimated net ODI penalties of around £162m over 2025–30 and has said some targets make underperformance more likely than outperformance.
  • Translation: for five years the UK asset may pay YTL Power nothing and may require an equity injection. In the current UK political climate, water is a franchise with regulatory, reputational and political tail risk.

5. The group keeps adding complexity

  • Ranhill (2024) and NSL (2024) added two listed layers. Cepco (2026) added another unlisted one. The direction of travel is more structure, not less.
  • Related-party flows are pervasive: YTL Construction builds YTL Power's data centres using Malayan Cement's cement on YTL Land's land, financed at the holdco. Every one of those is a governance question mark for a global allocator, and each is a reason to apply a discount.

6. Attarat Power (Jordan) remains an unresolved liability

  • YTL Power holds 45% of the oil-shale plant. Jordan and NEPCO have pursued ICC arbitration alleging "gross unfairness" in a PPA tariff of ~US$0.17/kWh against peers at ~US$0.10/kWh.
  • An adverse or renegotiated outcome is a discrete impairment and cash-flow risk that no part of the current price appears to reflect.

7. The mechanical case for a static discount

  • Discounts close through forced events — liquidation, merger, a hostile bid, an activist. None is available here. Family control at ~47.7%, no meaningful Malaysian activist precedent, a family-dominated board and no takeover possibility.
  • Malaysian conglomerates persistently trade at holdco discounts. There is no reason to assume mean reversion toward a global norm.
  • The most likely outcome is not "widens" or "closes" but "oscillates in a 10–30% band forever" — in which case the return you earn is simply NAV growth minus dilution, and dilution over the next two years is ~20%.

What the pre-mortem tells us to monitor

  • Contracted vs built MW at Kulai and Sedenak — the single most important disclosure. If contracted MW stops tracking built MW, the thesis is broken.
  • Warrant exercise pace and who is exercising — family exercise funded by share pledges would be a red flag.
  • Wessex dividend to YTL Power — any equity injection is a thesis-changing event.
  • Whether the buyback is ever actually used. This is the cleanest available test of management intent.

  • But it is not a bubble. 20x earnings for an entity whose earnings are cyclically depressed (telco losses, construction losses, a Seraya trough, a Wessex trough) is not obviously excessive. If Yes is fixed or exited, construction turns, and Seraya normalises to S$110m a quarter, FY2028 attributable earnings could plausibly be 40–50% above FY2026 — putting the stock on ~13–14x forward with the DC ramp still ahead.

Position-sizing conclusion: we would treat RM2.58 as fair-to-slightly-full, not expensive. It is a hold and an accumulate-on-weakness rather than an initiation. A pullback toward RM2.10–2.20 (a ~25–30% diluted discount) would be a materially better entry, and the warrant expiry cycle in 2028 makes such a pullback more likely than usual.

Verdict on the scorecard

Over fifteen years YTL has done three genuinely good structural things (two delistings and the cement consolidation) and one genuinely good capital action (the January 2026 placement). Against that, it has since 2024 added two listed layers, left a 10% buyback mandate almost entirely unused through a period of extreme cheapness, and issued 2.27bn warrants at a 42% discount to SOTP. Net, over the last three years, management's actions have widened rather than narrowed the structural discount. Any thesis that relies on management closing the gap is relying on a behavioural change for which there is currently no evidence.

13. Contrarian View — What the Market Is Refusing to See

Three things, in ascending order of how uncomfortable they are.

13.1 The discount is smaller than everyone says — and that is bearish, not bullish

The consensus story is "YTL trades at a 30% conglomerate discount and that gap will close." Our work says the gap is 16.8% undiluted and 11.0% fully diluted. The difference is the RM4–5bn of net debt sitting outside the listed subsidiaries and the RM2.5bn of warrant dilution — neither of which appears in the back-of-envelope "add up the stakes" calculations that circulate in the market.

The contrarian implication is uncomfortable for bulls: the "discount closure" leg of the thesis is worth ~11%, not ~30%. Anyone underwriting a double on discount closure is mis-specified. The return has to come from NAV compounding, which means this is a growth investment that must be judged on data-centre and cement execution — not a value investment with a structural catch-up.

13.2 The warrants are not dilution — they are the funding plan, and that is bullish

The market punished both stocks in January 2025 for "dilution". That reading is incomplete. Read the warrants instead as a pre-committed, three-year, self-selecting rights issue that raises up to RM3.4bn at YTL Corp and further sums at YTL Power, at a moment when the group faces three simultaneous capital calls: Wessex Water's AMP8 equity requirement, RM10bn+ of data-centre capex, and the residual ex-listed leverage.

What the market is refusing to see is the counterfactual: without the warrants, YTL would have needed a discounted rights issue or a forced asset sale in 2027–28 — precisely when Wessex needs equity. The Yeoh family, with 47.7%, must fund ~RM1.6bn of exercise to hold their stake; they have effectively pre-committed to injecting their own capital. The instrument is ugly and unfriendly to small holders, but it removes the single largest financing tail-risk in the story. The dilution is real; the alternative was worse.

13.3 The unlisted stub is being marked at less than zero, and the cement franchise inside it is a different business than the market thinks

At RM2.58 the listed stakes alone are worth ~RM2.70 per share. The market is paying nothing — arithmetically, less than nothing — for: a 294-acre KL city-fringe land bank at historic cost; a global trophy-hotel portfolio (Niseko Village, Threadneedles, Saint-Tropez, Monkey Island); the Singapore concrete and IBS operations; two REIT management franchises; and the construction arm that is currently building Malaysia's largest AI campus.

And on cement specifically, the market still models Malayan Cement as a cyclical. It is not. Post-consolidation it is the price-setter in a domestic duopoly, delivering FY2026 PBT +35% to RM1.33bn into a construction cycle underwritten by data centres, the Johor–Singapore SEZ and rail — with visibility to 2030. YTL Corp's 66% is worth RM6.3bn against a RM30bn market cap, and the market is valuing it as though the next downturn is imminent. If cement holds these earnings for three years, that stake alone re-rates by RM3–4bn.

The synthesis — and the trade it implies

The genuinely contrarian position is not "buy YTL Corp because the discount will close" (it is too narrow and the controller will not close it), nor "avoid YTL Corp because of dilution" (the dilution is the funding solution). It is: own YTL Corp as the cheapest available way to hold Malaysian cement and the group's unlisted real assets, with a 45.6% call on the data-centre business attached — and accept that the discount will never close. Underwrite NAV growth of 8–12% p.a. against ~7% p.a. of warrant dilution over the next two years, and require the discount only to stay stable. On that basis the stock works. On any basis requiring management to act, it does not.

14.2 Why classical activism cannot work here

  • The controller has an absolute blocking position. At ~47.7%, plus board control and family members in every senior executive role, no resolution passes without consent and no special resolution can be forced. There is no proxy path.
  • No takeover pressure. A change of control is not available at any price, so the classic "close the discount or we sell the company" lever does not exist.
  • Jurisdiction. Malaysia has essentially no successful activist precedent against a founder-controlled conglomerate. Minority Shareholder Watch Group engagement is advisory. Litigation risk to the controller is negligible.
  • The controller is economically motivated to keep the structure. As set out in §9, the family's ~21.8% look-through economic interest in YTL Power is converted into full control by the holdco layer. Collapsing YTL Corp into YTL Power — the single highest-value action available — would either dilute their control or require them to fund a buy-in. The discount is the rent minorities pay for that arrangement.

14.3 Where realistic pressure could come from — and what to ask for

EPF is the only credible agent. At 12.6% and rising, EPF is large enough to matter, domestic enough to be heard, and increasingly governed by a stewardship framework. The realistic mechanism is not a proxy fight but private engagement backed by voting on the annual buyback and RRPT mandates. Note that EPF's accumulation into the discount is itself a signal — it is being paid to wait.

15. Conclusion and Monitoring Framework

Summary judgement

  • Assets: RM3.10/share SOTP; RM2.90 fully diluted. Listed stakes alone are RM2.70 — 105% of the share price.
  • Discount: 16.8% undiluted, 11.0% diluted. Real but roughly half the popular claim. Unlikely to close; underwrite it as stable.
  • Earnings: ~20x FY26 attributable EPS on cyclically depressed earnings. Rich on the print, reasonable on normalised power.
  • Balance sheet: ~RM4.5bn of net debt outside the listed subsidiaries (range RM3.0–5.1bn); ~RM20bn of look-through attributable net debt against RM30bn of equity.
  • Management: net negative on discount-narrowing over the last three years. Do not pay for a re-rating that requires them to act.
  • Stance: Hold / accumulate on weakness. Better entry RM2.10–2.20. The 2028 warrant-expiry cycle raises the probability of getting it.
