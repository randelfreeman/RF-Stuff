## 14.2 Is Coupang a takeover target?

**Pros**

- The valuation is at a five-year low on sales (0.79x) with net cash.
- It is a scarce asset: a dominant owned-logistics network in a large, high-penetration e-commerce market.
- Strategic logic exists for global platforms (Amazon has no direct Korean presence [u]; Walmart; Uber, which is consolidating delivery) and for large private-equity or sovereign capital.
- Developing Offerings assets could be carved out: Farfetch, Eats, Taiwan.

**Impediments**

1. **Control.** Bom Kim holds all Class B shares and **74.1% of the votes** ([#1187]({S}SEC000255973354-1187)). No deal happens without him, and the 10-K says his control "will limit or preclude your ability to influence corporate matters... including... any merger" ([#1188]({S}SEC000255973354-1188)).
2. **Size.** An EV of ~US$28bn rises to ~US$37bn at a 30% premium [d].
3. **Korean regulation.** There is political and regulatory sensitivity after the breach, a business-group designation ([#1007]({S}SEC000255973354-1007)), data-sovereignty concerns, and KFTC merger control.
4. **No obvious domestic buyer.** NAVER's market cap is ~US$20bn.
5. **Litigation and regulatory contingencies** would need to be priced.

**Most plausible path.** A founder-led take-private with financial partners, or minority carve-outs. A hostile approach is impossible.

**Potential takeout multiples [d]**

| Method | Multiple | Applied to | Implied EV | Implied value per share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery-platform precedent median | 1.15x sales | LTM revenue US$35.5bn | US$40.8bn | ~US$23 |
| Range of precedents | 0.9-1.3x sales | LTM revenue | US$31.9-46.1bn | ~US$18-26 |
| Premium approach | 30-40% over US$15.82 | | | US$20.6-22.1 |
| EBITDA cross-check | ~20x FY25 adjusted EBITDA (~US$1.49bn) | | US$29.7bn | ~US$17 |

**Conclusion.** A plausible control value is **~US$20-26 a share (1.0-1.3x LTM sales)**. The probability of a bid is low, given the control structure. Takeover premium should not be part of the core thesis. Treat it as a tail option if the founder's own incentives (personal tax needs; [#311]({S}SEC000232383623-311)) or regulatory fatigue push toward a take-private.

## 15.1 Capital allocation history

**ROE and ROIC under the current team [d].** ROE was ~3.7% in FY24 and ~4.5% in FY25. ROIC uses a notional 25% tax on GAAP EBIT, with invested capital defined as equity + debt + leases − cash:

| Year | ROIC |
|---|---|
| FY23 | ~22% |
| FY24 | ~14% |
| FY25 | ~13% |
| LTM | Negative |

ROIC looks high because negative working capital funds most of the asset base. That flatters it, and it is exactly what the Dec-2026 settlement rules threaten.

**Good decisions**

- Reinvesting the Korean cash flow into the logistics network.
- Opportunistic, cheap entry into Farfetch (an administration deal).
- Refinancing expensive Farfetch term loans (SOFR + 6.25%) with cheaper revolver debt ([#1403]({S}SEC000255973354-1403)).
- Buying back stock in 2025-26 at an average ~US$20.8 ([#369]({S}SEC000264559057-369)).

**Questionable decisions**

- Buybacks of US$850m in H1'26 funded alongside a US$750m revolver draw while FCF fell to ~US$0.1bn.
- Ongoing ~US$1bn a year of Developing Offerings losses with no published return hurdle. No ROIC target was found ([#424]({S}T000003779883-424)).
- A related-party buyout of Greenoaks at a premium to its contributed capital (Section 25).

## 15.2 Archetype and implications

Coupang is a **founder-controlled, founder-led company**. That brings long-term orientation, willingness to absorb losses (Taiwan) and speed. It also carries key-person and governance risk: dual-class shares, no external check on capital allocation, and political exposure in Korea.

At this stage the founder model is an asset operationally: rebuilding trust and fixing utilisation. It is a liability in regulatory diplomacy, where a Korean-resident professional CEO for Coupang Corp. would help. The prolonged "interim" status at the Korean subsidiary is the key management gap to watch.
