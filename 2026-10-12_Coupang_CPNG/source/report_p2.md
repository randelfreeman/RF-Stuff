
# 6. Strength of products and services; competitive advantages (moats)

## 6.1 Perceived value, branding and marketing versus competitors

- **Value proposition.** Management's description is "the combination of selection, price and delivery that no one else offers" ([#356]({S}T000003711263-356)).
  - The WOW bundle is priced at roughly the cost of two deliveries a month, against 14 benefits (Q2'24 call, [#261]({S}T000003215027-261)).
  - The newsletter cites a KRW7,890 monthly fee. The 2024 increase is confirmed ([#248]({S}T000003215027-248)), but the KRW amount is [u].
- **Revealed preference.**
  - Each annual customer cohort keeps spending more over time ([#69]({S}T000003779883-69)).
  - After the incident, nearly 80% of the WOW decline had been recovered by end-April 2026 ([#36]({S}T000003711263-36)). Membership has since passed pre-incident levels ([#39]({S}T000003779883-39)).
- **Brand.** The brand is built on delivery reliability rather than advertising. The 2026 marketing step-up is described as a "onetime investment to accelerate reacquisition", to be reduced in 2027 ([#420]({S}T000003779883-420), [#55]({S}T000003779883-55)). The data incident damaged trust, and "some trust issues remain" among non-returners ([#478]({S}T000003779883-478)).
- **Competitors' propositions.**
  - **Naver:** marketplace aggregator plus Plus membership (Netflix, Game Pass and Uber benefits; [#93]({S}NONSEC000264476510-93), [#78]({S}T000003585246-78)) and Kurly N Mart dawn delivery ([#321]({S}NONSEC000256804358-321)). Logistics are asset-light via CJ Logistics and the NFA alliance ([#326]({S}NONSEC000256804358-326)).
  - **Chinese cross-border platforms:** compete on price, not speed. Combined users exceed 10m ([#344]({S}T000003711263-344)).
  - Naver reported gains in commerce GMV and new members after Coupang's breach ([#298]({S}T000003646016-298)). This is the clearest evidence the moat leaked, at least temporarily.

**Moat verdict.** The moat is narrow but real. It rests on the physical network, the habit and bundle economics, and supplier scale. It does not rest on regulation, contracts or market structure. The incident showed the moat can leak (Naver GMV gains) but not drain (customers returned with full wallets). The biggest threats to the moat are regulatory rather than competitive: forced Eats unbundling, a night-delivery ban, and settlement-period rules.

It spans procurement (1P buying and private label via CPLB, [#74]({S}SEC000255973354-74)), warehousing and fulfilment (own network, including fulfilment for 3P sellers), last-mile delivery (direct-employment drivers plus independent partners, [#88]({S}SEC000255973354-88)), the consumer interface (app, WOW), and payments (Coupang Pay).

- AWS cloud: "we cannot quickly or easily switch" ([#696]({S}SEC000255973354-696)).

- Landlords: US$3.0bn of lease liabilities and US$689m of signed leases not yet commenced ([#1931]({S}SEC000255973354-1931)).
- Card networks: competitors "control... credit card interchange" ([#368]({S}SEC000255973354-368)).

**Named counterparties verified in the tools.** CJ CheilJedang (supplier), Amazon Web Services (cloud), Mandiant and Palo Alto Networks (incident forensics, [#36]({S}T000003665452-36)), Samil PwC (auditor), Greenoaks (co-investor in Farfetch and a related party, [#2050]({S}SEC000255973354-2050)), and Farfetch boutiques and brands. Brand names such as Samsung, LG, Nongshim, Amorepacific, P&G and Unilever, and carriers such as CJ Logistics and Hanjin, are commonly cited as part of Korea's e-commerce supply chain. **None is named for Coupang in S&P/ProntoNLP [u].**

## 8.4 Trends: growth and margins, and why

1. **Growth decelerated from hyper-growth to mid-teens before the incident.** Revenue compounded at ~17% a year over FY21-25 [d], with constant-currency growth above reported growth in every year.
   - FY2023 reported growth was depressed by ~520bp when FLC revenue moved to a net basis ([#28]({S}SEC000218768452-28)).
   - FY2024 was boosted by Farfetch; growth ex-Farfetch was 17% against 24% reported ([#36]({S}SEC000237406755-36)).
   - The 2025 USD figures absorbed a 5pt FX drag on Product Commerce ([#1389]({S}SEC000255973354-1389)).
2. **Gross margin expanded by 12.5pts in four years** (16.9% to 29.4%). The drivers were mix (3P/FLC and ads), procurement scale and automation, and Farfetch's higher-margin marketplace model. The incident reversed about 1-2pts in H1'26 through vouchers, promotions and supply-chain inefficiency ([#102]({S}T000003711263-102), [#167]({S}T000003779883-167)).
3. **Product Commerce profitability is the real story.** PC adjusted EBITDA margin climbed from ~3% (FY22) to 8.4% (FY25), and 8.8% in Q3'25. Developing Offerings losses grew from US$225m to US$995m over the same years, consuming about 40% of PC EBITDA in FY25.
4. **The GAAP bottom line is noisy.** Items include a US$905m valuation-allowance release in FY23 ([#1901]({S}SEC000255973354-1901)) and a US$175m fire-insurance gain and US$121m KFTC fine in FY24 ([#1360]({S}SEC000255973354-1360)). Effective tax rates were 86% in FY24 and 64% in FY25 [d], because losses in Taiwan, the US and Farfetch get no tax benefit ([#1413]({S}SEC000255973354-1413)).
5. **2026 broke the margin trajectory.** Revenue held up, at +8% to +10% constant currency. But fixed costs sized for pre-incident demand, extra marketing and vouchers cut the PC margin to ~5% ([#170]({S}T000003779883-170)).

# 9. Earnings calls: what management is focused on, and how sentiment has shifted

## 9.1 Summary of the last four calls

- **[Q3'25, 4-Nov-2025]({D}T000003578475), before the incident.** Confident and growth-led: "growth and expanding margins yet again" ([#22]({S}T000003578475-22)).
  - Product Commerce margin 8.8%. Consolidated margins expanding annually "for the foreseeable future" ([#126]({S}T000003578475-126)). PC margins to move "well past the 10%" level ([#271]({S}T000003578475-271)).
  - The worry was Developing Offerings losses: guided to the high end of US$900-950m on Taiwan ([#98]({S}T000003578475-98)).
- **[Q4'25, 26-Feb-2026]({D}T000003665452).** Opened with an apology ([#26]({S}T000003665452-26)) and an incident review by the General Counsel ([#35]({S}T000003665452-35)). Palo Alto Networks called it a targeted insider attack, not a systemic failure ([#53]({S}T000003665452-53)).
  - Guidance: margin expansion "disrupted" in 2026 but not structurally ([#199]({S}T000003665452-199), [#200]({S}T000003665452-200)). Developing Offerings loss guided at US$950m-1bn ([#202]({S}T000003665452-202)). No full-year revenue guide ([#197]({S}T000003665452-197)).
- **[Q1'26, 5-May-2026]({D}T000003711263).** The recovery case: active customers dipped 3% QoQ on a lagged definition, but nearly 80% of the lost WOW members were back by April ([#36]({S}T000003711263-36)).
  - Management explained the fixed-cost structure and chose not to cut capacity ([#186]({S}T000003711263-186)).
  - Q2 margin guided -300 to -400bp; annual expansion to resume in 2027 ([#147]({S}T000003711263-147)). A further US$1bn buyback was authorised.
- **[Q2'26, 4-Aug-2026]({D}T000003779883).** More confident on customers: WOW above pre-incident levels, and underlying spend +16% ([#43]({S}T000003779883-43)).
  - The margin recovery date slipped to "mid-2027" ([#220]({S}T000003779883-220)). "Nothing has structurally changed" ([#252]({S}T000003779883-252)). Marketing to step down in 2027 ([#55]({S}T000003779883-55)).
  - Focus areas: cohort economics, Taiwan's playbook, and AI across operations ([#390]({S}T000003779883-390)).

**Where management's attention has moved.** Before the incident it was selection, FLC, automation and Taiwan. After it, the focus is customer retention and trust, the fixed-cost and margin bridge, the regulatory and legal process, and capital return.

**Key assumptions and their logic**

- **H2'26 revenue.** Q3: PC +6.5% constant currency (in line with the Barclays-implied 6-7%) less 5pts of FX; DO +20% reported. Q4: an easy December-25 comparison, so PC +12% constant currency less 4pts of FX. That gives US$9.64bn and US$9.69bn.
- **H2'26 margins.** Q3 adjusted EBITDA margin of 1.2% (the -330bp end of the guide); Q4 3.1%.
- **Industry growth and share.** Korean online sales grew +4.9% in 2025 ([#305]({S}NONSEC000256804358-305)). We assume Coupang grows PC at 11% and 10% in FY27 and FY28. That is roughly 2x the market, consistent with past share gains, and reflects recapture of the non-returning cohort and lapping of the vouchers.
- **Pricing and cost pressures.** Promotions normalise in 2027 and marketing steps down ([#55]({S}T000003779883-55)). Labour cost inflation and the night-delivery risk are not in the base case.
- **Operating leverage.** We take PC margin to 7.3% in FY27, the mid-2027 recovery path, and 8.6% in FY28, roughly the pre-incident level. This is below the "well past 10%" ambition ([#271]({S}T000003578475-271)).
- **Financing costs.** ~US$2.6bn of debt at ~3-4% (Korean lines at 2.89% ([#173]({S}SEC000264559057-173)), term loan at 3.80%, revolver at SOFR+0.75-1.25%) against ~US$5.5-6bn of cash.
- **Share count.** US$907m of remaining authorisation absorbs SBC dilution; 386m shares remain available under the equity plans ([#1886]({S}SEC000255973354-1886)).
- **Tax.** ETR stays well above the ~25% long-term guide ([#120]({S}T000003578475-120)) while Taiwan and US losses get no benefit.

**Versus consensus.** ProntoNLP's consensus for 3Q26 and 4Q26 is stale: EPS of US$0.17 and US$0.20 dated March 2025 ‡. No current FY2027 or FY2028 consensus is available from S&P or ProntoNLP, so we cannot quantify the gap to street estimates.

**Sensitivities [d]**

- A 10% move in the KRW is ~±US$3.5-4bn of revenue and ~±US$0.10 of EPS (scaled from the 10-K sensitivity, [#1624]({S}SEC000255973354-1624)).
